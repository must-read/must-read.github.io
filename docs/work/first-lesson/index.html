Synopsis

A forty-five-year-old joins an adult recorder class on a whim, buys an $8.99 Yamaha, and discovers that failing at a children's instrument in front of strangers triggers something uncomfortably close to an identity crisis.

Sedaris's comic timing and classroom humiliation engine meets Smith's self-aware essayistic thinking, structured around Me Talk Pretty One Day's adult-as-incompetent-student and Feel Free's joy-as-intellectual-position, in an essay about a forty-five-year-old who joins an adult recorder class and discovers that competence may have been a performance all along.

The Formula

Author A David Sedaris
  • Self-deprecating narrator reporting incompetence with war-correspondent accuracy
  • Punchlines buried inside sentences, not at paragraph ends
  • Catalog of fellow students rendered with forensic comic specificity
  • The fool-of-his-own-story engine driving every classroom scene
Author B Zadie Smith
  • Essayistic thinking-aloud paragraph following an idea past its comfortable limit
  • Self-awareness as comma, not punchline — catching the narrator mid-performance
  • Cultural observation of the teacher's earnestness as an unsolvable problem
  • Comedy arriving three sentences after the observation that undermined itself
Work X Me Talk Pretty One Day by David Sedaris
  • Adult placed in total incompetence at a basic skill, observed and judged
  • The classroom as theater with teacher as irrational authority figure
  • Shame and humor as the same substance — the Sedaris engine
  • Specific parallel: can't play B on a recorder as can't conjugate in French
Work Y Feel Free by Zadie Smith
  • Essay that admits confusion and arrives at questions rather than answers
  • Beginnership examined as a place rather than a transit point
  • The ending refuses to learn the lesson it seems to be teaching
  • Joy as intellectual position — delight found inside the thinking itself
