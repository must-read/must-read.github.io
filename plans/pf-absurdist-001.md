## Wikipedia Concepts

20 random articles surveyed. Selected 2:

1. **Docimium** — Ancient marble quarries in Phrygia (modern Turkey). Produced pavonazzetto marble: white with purple veins. Used in Pantheon, Hagia Sophia, Forum of Trajan. The quarries are now destroyed by modern extraction. The stone carries its geological history in its coloring.
   - **Integration**: Ewa meditates on surfaces that carry their origins. The marble veins as geological autobiography. Used in "Fragment: On the Docimium quarries" section.

2. **Daria-i-Noor** — "Sea of Light" diamond, 182 carats, looted from Mughal treasury 1739, possibly cut from a larger stone (The Great Table), passed through multiple dynasties. Two halves exist separately, each with its own provenance.
   - **Integration**: Parallel to the dissected knee — a single whole broken into parts, each acquiring its own catalog number. Used in "Fragment: On the Daria-i-Noor" section. The two halves "do not know they are halves."

## Premise
A woman who catalogs anatomical specimens in a museum has been sent by train to retrieve a preserved knee from another collection. The collection may have been dissolved. The knee may not exist. Her body registers the journey in ways her catalog cannot. Meanwhile, back at the museum, the director who sent her sits amid a failing climate-control system and contemplates the gap on the shelf where the knee should be.

## Protagonist
**Ewa**, 47, specimen cataloger. 23 years at the museum. Has never traveled for work before. Carries a gray pebble compulsively. Wants to complete the retrieval and return. Needs to understand that naming things is not the same as knowing them. Flaw: she has confused description with comprehension — she catalogs surfaces without ever opening the jars.

## Structure
Dual-narrator structure echoing Molloy:
- **Part I (Ewa)**: Train journey outward, increasingly fragmented, punctuated by Tokarczukian knowledge-fragments about anatomy, marble, diamonds
- **Part II (Director)**: At the museum with the gap on the shelf, Beckettian monologue about the catalog as the real collection, the gap as the best specimen
- Fragments orbit between the two narrations like Tokarczuk's constellation structure

## Key Scenes
1. Ewa on the train, losing her ticket to a phantom conductor
2. The platform with the woman carrying a jar (the almost-encounter)
3. The museum director contemplating the gap on shelf 14
4. The departure board emptying — the most honest text
5. Ewa at the end, pebble in hand, boarding a train to nowhere

## Emotional Trajectory
Reader begins in wry amusement at the bureaucratic absurdity. Moves through recognition (the body's complaints, the compulsive pebble-handling). Arrives at something unresolvable — not sadness, not comfort, but the particular feeling of watching someone continue a motion that has lost its object and finding, in that continuation, something that is either dignity or habit and may be both.

## Formula Integration
- **Beckett**: Ewa's voice — the entropic, self-correcting narration ("it is possible, I want to say probable, but I will say possible")
- **Tokarczuk**: The fragments on anatomy, marble, diamonds — essayistic knowledge sections orbiting the narrative
- **Molloy**: Dual narration, the body crawling toward a destination, the pebble ritual (sucking stones), the absurd errand
- **Flights**: Preserved specimens as philosophical objects, travel as epistemology, the relic separated from its origin

## Title
"Cartilage and Timetable" — no "The", Beckettian ugliness, Tokarczukian conjunction of body and schedule
