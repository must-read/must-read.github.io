# Batch 11 Specification

## Work 1: hist-tudmed-001
- **Genre**: Historical Fiction / Tudor-Medieval
- **AuthorA**: Viet Thanh Nguyen — Vietnamese American experience; the refugee as narrator; postcolonial critique with literary precision
- **AuthorB**: Ken Follett — Sweeping multi-plot architecture; medieval and 20th-century settings; accessible, cinematic prose
- **WorkX**: *The Name of the Rose* (Eco) — Medieval monastery mystery where intellectual inquiry and institutional power collide inside stone walls
- **WorkY**: *Wolf Hall* (Mantel) — A man navigating a lethal court through cunning and self-erasure, where survival requires becoming what power demands
- **Target**: 7,200 words (6,900–7,500)
- **Risk Card**: #7 MULTIPLE VOICES — 2-3 distinct narrators whose accounts overlap, contradict, or illuminate each other

## Work 2: magr-slip-001
- **Genre**: Magical Realism / Slipstream
- **AuthorA**: Jorge Luis Borges — Philosophical, labyrinthine short fictions; libraries and mirrors; infinity in miniature; cerebral magic
- **AuthorB**: Karen Russell — American fabulism; swamp Gothic; inventive premises; playful, sensory language
- **WorkX**: *The Unconsoled* (Ishiguro) — Dream-logic spatial anxiety where rooms connect impossibly and the protagonist accepts impossible architecture as normal
- **WorkY**: *Her Body and Other Parties* (Machado) — The body as site of slippage between real and unreal, where domestic and erotic life become territory where genre bends
- **Target**: 4,100 words (3,800–4,400)
- **Risk Card**: None

## Work 3: phil-absurd-001
- **Genre**: Philosophical Fiction / Absurdist Fiction
- **AuthorA**: Olga Tokarczuk — Constellation novels; fragmentary, wandering narratives; Central European philosophical fiction
- **AuthorB**: Franz Kafka — Bureaucratic nightmare in precise, matter-of-fact prose; metamorphosis as everyday event; alienation incarnate
- **WorkX**: *The Crying of Lot 49* (Pynchon) — A conspiracy narrative that may or may not exist, where the search for meaning generates the patterns the protagonist thinks she's discovering
- **WorkY**: *Waiting for Godot* (Beckett) — The act of waiting as the human condition; the comedy and despair of repetition; whether the waited-for thing was ever real
- **Target**: 5,800 words (5,500–6,100)
- **Risk Card**: #10 THE PROTAGONIST IS WRONG — The main character's worldview is fundamentally flawed, revealed through consequences the character may or may not understand

## Work 4: cnf-braided-001
- **Genre**: Creative Nonfiction / Braided-Hermit-Crab Essay
- **AuthorA**: John McPhee — Structural innovation in nonfiction; geological patience; the architecture of the long piece
- **AuthorB**: Roxane Gay — Confessional, vulnerable, politically engaged; the body and culture; accessible intellectual voice
- **WorkX**: *Citizen: An American Lyric* (Rankine) — Hybrid form moving between genres, registers, and modes of address; fragment and accumulation as primary tools
- **WorkY**: *Underland* (Macfarlane) — Deep time, underground spaces, the hidden worlds beneath the surface; what the earth holds and what it refuses to yield
- **Target**: 6,800 words (6,500–7,100)
- **Risk Card**: None

## Work 5: dyst-biopunk-001
- **Genre**: Dystopian / Biopunk Dystopia
- **AuthorA**: Margaret Atwood — Speculative precision; wry, ironic narration; feminist dystopia grounded in historical precedent
- **AuthorB**: P.D. James — Literary dystopia; elegiac, morally serious; the quiet end of humanity
- **WorkX**: *The Windup Girl* (Bacigalupi) — Multi-perspective biopunk world where the engineered being's perspective is the story's moral center
- **WorkY**: *Brave New World* (Huxley) — Biological engineering as social control; the manufacturing of contentment; whether a designed life can be called human
- **Target**: 4,900 words (4,600–5,200)
- **Risk Card**: #3 STORY-AS-DOCUMENT — The story takes the form of a non-literary document; the bureaucratic format creates friction with the emotional content
