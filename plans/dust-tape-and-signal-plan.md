## Research Notes

### Writing Samples Collected

**Hunter S. Thompson — Hell's Angels (1966)**

From the motorcycle passage: "The only sounds are wind and a dull roar floating back from the mufflers. You watch the white line and try to lean with it... howling through a turn to the right, then to the left and down the long hill to Pacifica... letting off now, watching for cops, but only until the next dark stretch and another few seconds on the edge... The Edge... There is no honest way to explain it because the only people who really know where it is are the ones who have gone over. The others — the living — are those who pushed their control as far as they felt they could handle it, and then pulled back, or slowed down, or did whatever they had to when it came time to choose between Now and Later."

Key Thompson signatures: the reporter becomes participant, present-tense immersion that accelerates toward recklessness, direct address ("you"), the American fringe rendered without condescension, violence and strangeness observed at intimate range, sentences that build velocity then snap.

Also: "There is not much mental distance between a feeling of having been screwed and the ethic of total retaliation." — The compression of political anger into a single sentence. Thompson's talent for reducing a sociological phenomenon to a gut instinct.

Opening of Hell's Angels: "California, Labor Day weekend... early, with ocean fog still in the streets, outlaw motorcyclists wearing chains, shades and greasy Levis roll out from damp garages, all-night diners and cast-off one-night pads in Frisco, Hollywood, Berdoo and East Oakland, heading for the Monterey peninsula, north of Big Sur... The Menace is loose again." — Sentence fragments, geographic catalog, the menace introduced before the people.

**John McPhee — Coming into the Country (1977) / The Control of Nature (1989)**

From Coming into the Country: "In Alaska, where waters flow in many places without the questionable benefit of names, there are nineteen streams called Salmon — thirteen Salmon Creeks, six Salmon Rivers of which this one, the Salmon River of the Brooks Range, is the most northern, its watershed wholly above the Arctic Circle. Rising in treeless alpine tundra, it falls south into the fringes of the boreal forest, the taiga, the upper limit of the Great North Woods."

From The Control of Nature (Atchafalaya): "Among navigable rivers, the Atchafalaya is widely described as one of the most treacherous in the world, but it just lies there quiet and smooth. It lies there like a big alligator in a low slough, with time on its side, waiting — waiting to outwait the Corps of Engineers — and hunkering down ever lower in its bed and presenting a sort of maw to the Mississippi, into which the river could fall."

Key McPhee signatures: geological patience, the long careful accumulation of technical knowledge rendered in clean prose, subjects speaking in their own language, structural innovation hidden beneath simple surfaces, the eccentric loner as keeper of vanishing knowledge, landscape given equal weight to human narrative. Sentences that inventory the world without rushing.

From The Pine Barrens: "'I went to school there, by that red cedar,' Fred said. 'There was twenty-five of us in the school. We all walked. We wore leather boots in the winter that got soaked through and your feet froze. When you got home, you had to pull off your boots on a bootjack. In the summer, when I was a boy, if you wanted to go anywhere you rolled up your pantlegs, put your shoes on your shoulder, and you walked wherever you was going.'" — The subject's voice preserved intact, dialect and rhythm honored.

### Wikipedia Random Concept Mining (20 Concepts)

1. **UVB-76 ("The Buzzer")** — A Russian shortwave radio station broadcasting a monotonous buzz tone 24 hours a day since the late 1970s; purpose unknown
2. **Numbers stations** — Shortwave stations broadcasting formatted number sequences, believed addressed to intelligence officers abroad
3. **Foxhole radio** — WWII makeshift radio built by soldiers using a razor blade as detector and safety pin as cat's whisker
4. **Sporadic E propagation** — Unpredictable ionospheric phenomenon that occasionally bounces radio signals far beyond normal range
5. **Ground wave propagation** — Radio signals following Earth's contour, the physics behind AM radio's reach
6. **Skip zone / dead zone** — The annular region between ground wave reception and skywave return where no signal can be heard
7. **National Radio Quiet Zone** — 13,000 square miles in West Virginia where radio emissions are restricted to protect the Green Bank Observatory
8. **Karst topography** — Landscape formed by dissolution of soluble rock; sinkholes, caves, losing streams
9. **Stamps-Baxter Music Company** — Dallas-based gospel music publisher; quartets broadcast on KRLD from 1936; sponsored 35 quartets by end of WWII
10. **Centralia mine fire** — Underground coal fire burning beneath Centralia, Pennsylvania since 1962; town abandoned
11. **Phantom island** — Islands appearing on maps later found not to exist (Hy-Brasil, Pepys Island, etc.)
12. **Null Island** — The point at coordinates 0,0 in the Atlantic; no land exists there but it serves as a database error placeholder
13. **Whistler (radio)** — Electromagnetic signal that descends in pitch, caused by lightning impulses propagating through the magnetosphere
14. **Dead reckoning** — Navigation by estimating current position from a previously known position, using speed and elapsed time
15. **Terra nullius** — "Nobody's land"; legal concept for territory not claimed by any state
16. **Bir Tawil** — Strip of land between Egypt and Sudan claimed by neither country
17. **Language death** — When a language loses its last native speaker; 50-90% of world's languages expected to die by 2100
18. **FCC Part 15** — Federal regulation governing unlicensed low-power transmitters; maximum penalty $10,000 per violation per day
19. **Crystal radio** — Earliest radio receiver type, requiring no external power, using only the energy of incoming radio waves
20. **Shepard tone** — An auditory illusion of a tone that seems to continually ascend or descend in pitch but never actually gets higher or lower

**Selected for integration:**

- **Skip zone / dead zone** (#6): The annular region where no signal reaches — neither ground wave nor skywave. This becomes a literal and figurative concept in the piece. The FCC field agent stands in the skip zone between bureaucratic mandate and human reality. The dying operators broadcast into a demographic skip zone — the audience is aging out, the next generation has moved away, and the signal reaches empty houses. The term appears naturally in the technical documentation.

- **Foxhole radio** (#3): Dale Otterbein's transmitter, built from salvaged and kit parts, echoes the WWII tradition of improvised radio. The foxhole radio was entertainment built from desperation in a war zone. Otterbein's is entertainment built from stubbornness in a demographic war zone. The field agent notes the construction method — soldered connections, salvaged components — and the parallel to the foxhole radio tradition surfaces in an attachment or footnote without being made explicit.

## Premise

An FCC Enforcement Bureau field case file — Case No. EB-2024-FL-0847 — documents the investigation and repeated citations of Dale Otterbein, an unlicensed low-power AM radio operator in Laclede County, Missouri, who has been broadcasting obituaries, weather, farm reports, and gospel music on 1240 kHz for nineteen years. The file spans four inspections across eight years, three field agents (one retired, one transferred, one current), and accumulates technical measurements, correspondence, broadcast transcripts, and field observations that gradually exceed what any enforcement file should contain — until the document is holding an entire vanishing world inside a regulatory framework designed to erase it.

## Central Figure: Dale Otterbein

- Age 74 at time of most recent inspection (2024)
- Retired rural mail carrier, Laclede County, Missouri
- Has been fined four times ($2,400, $3,200, $5,000, $7,500 — escalating), pays each time, resumes broadcasting within 48 hours
- Broadcasts on 1240 kHz AM, approximately 0.8 watts ERP (estimated), from a transmitter he assembled from a Ramsey Electronics AM-1 kit and salvaged components from a defunct KZLR studio liquidation
- Morning broadcast: 6:45 AM, reads obituaries from the Lebanon Daily Record, then weather from NWS Springfield, then farm reports from the University of Missouri Extension
- Sunday broadcast adds gospel music: Stamps-Baxter quartet 78s, Carter Family recordings, some unidentified acetate discs
- Lives alone since wife Darlene died (2019). Son in Joplin, rarely visits
- Antenna: homemade, bolted to the chimney, approximately 12 feet of copper wire with a loading coil
- Flaw: Otterbein is not a romantic resister. He is stubborn, somewhat tedious, occasionally mean about his neighbors in off-air conversation, and genuinely believes the First Amendment protects his broadcast. He is wrong about the law and refuses to be corrected. He is also the only person reading the names of the dead aloud in a county where the newspaper's circulation has dropped to 1,100.

## Document Architecture (Risk Card: STORY-AS-DOCUMENT)

The entire piece is formatted as an FCC Enforcement Bureau field case file. No frame narrative, no first-person journalist, no conventional prose wrapper. The reader encounters the story as if reading the actual file. The document's architecture IS the story's architecture.

### Document Sections (in order):

**1. CASE SUMMARY SHEET** (~400 words)
Standard bureaucratic header: case number, subject name, location, frequency, violation type (47 U.S.C. § 301 — operation without a license), date range, assigned agents, status. Dry, formatted, procedural. Establishes the regulatory frame. Includes a field labeled "Disposition" that reads: "PENDING — See attached."

**2. INITIAL FIELD INSPECTION REPORT — Agent R. Toomey (2016)** (~1,200 words)
The first inspection. Agent Robert Toomey drives to Laclede County with direction-finding equipment, locates the signal on 1240 kHz, confirms unauthorized transmission from a residential address. Toomey's report is professional and terse — technical measurements, antenna description, equipment inventory. But even in this first report, observations leak in that don't belong: the length of the gravel driveway, the number of outbuildings, the fact that the operator answered the door holding a cup of coffee and a copy of the county paper folded to the obituary page. Toomey notes that the operator was "cooperative but noncompliant" — answered all questions, refused to agree to cease transmission. The report includes Otterbein's statement, transcribed: "I read the names so people know. That's all I do. I read the names."

The ground-wave propagation characteristics of 1240 kHz in the Ozark terrain are noted technically — the karst topography, the ridge interference, the estimated coverage radius of 12-15 miles depending on atmospheric conditions. This is McPhee's geological patience rendered in FCC technical language.

**3. CORRESPONDENCE FILE — Warning Letters and Responses (2016-2019)** (~800 words)
A sequence of official FCC warning letters (boilerplate, escalating in severity) interleaved with Otterbein's handwritten responses. The FCC letters are perfect bureaucratic prose — citation of statute, demand for compliance, statement of penalties. Otterbein's responses are brief, handwritten, sometimes misspelled, and completely unintimidated. One reads: "Dear Sir, I recieved your letter. I understand what you are saying. I disagree. The people here need to know who died. Nobody else is telling them. Sincerely, Dale Otterbein." Another, after the second fine: "Dear Sir, Check enclosed. I will be on air tomorrow at 6:45 as usual. Best, Dale."

The gap between the official language and the handwritten responses IS the story. The reader fills the gap.

**4. BROADCAST TRANSCRIPT EXCERPTS — Monitoring Log (2017-2023)** (~1,500 words)
The FCC's spectrum monitoring division has logged selected broadcast samples. These are presented as transcripts with timestamps, frequency notations, and signal quality annotations. The transcripts are the emotional center of the piece.

They include:
- An obituary reading: names, dates, survivors, service details. Otterbein's voice comes through the transcription — he adds brief personal notes that aren't in the newspaper. "Harold Jessup, born 1931, died Tuesday. Service Saturday at First Baptist. Harold drove the school bus on Route H for twenty-two years. If you rode that bus, you remember him."
- A weather report that includes Otterbein's editorial commentary: "NWS Springfield says high of 94, chance of storms after four. I'll add that the creek behind Dobbins' place has been dry since June, which they don't mention, but you should know."
- A Sunday gospel broadcast: the transcript notes "[MUSIC — unidentified quartet recording, approx. 1940s, significant surface noise, 78 RPM playback]" and then silence for the duration. The transcript cannot capture the music. It can only note that music occurred. This is the document's most revealing failure.
- A broadcast from the morning after Otterbein's wife Darlene dies (2019). The obituary he reads that morning includes hers. The transcript is annotated: "[Subject's voice noticeably altered. Broadcast duration shorter than typical — 4 min 12 sec vs. avg 11 min 30 sec. No weather or farm report followed.]" The document records the grief through measurements.

**5. SECOND AND THIRD FIELD INSPECTION REPORTS — Agents Toomey and Vidal (2019, 2021)** (~1,200 words)
Toomey's 2019 reinspection is briefer, more routine, but includes a paragraph about the condition of the property that wasn't in the first report — the garden gone to weeds since Darlene's death, a barn roof partially collapsed. He notes that Otterbein offered him coffee and played a Stamps-Baxter recording "at the subject's insistence, for approximately six minutes, during which this agent remained in the kitchen to maintain rapport for the purposes of the inspection." This is Thompson's reporter-becomes-participant, translated into bureaucratic self-justification.

Agent Lucia Vidal's 2021 inspection is different in tone — more clinical, shorter, but she includes a detail about the signal propagation that Toomey missed. She notes that the 1240 kHz signal, due to the karst terrain's effect on ground-wave conductivity, creates a **skip zone** — a dead zone — in the valley between two ridges, approximately 3 miles from the transmitter. Residents in that valley cannot receive the broadcast. "The existence of this skip zone," she writes, "has no enforcement relevance but is noted for completeness." The document accommodates more than it was designed to hold.

**6. FOURTH FIELD INSPECTION REPORT — Agent J. Ware (2024)** (~1,500 words)
The longest and most revealing section. Agent James Ware is newer, younger, and his report begins with proper FCC format but gradually, almost imperceptibly, begins stretching. He arrives at 6:30 AM to observe the morning broadcast in progress. He sits in his government vehicle in the driveway and listens to 1240 kHz on his monitoring equipment. Instead of entering immediately, he writes what he hears: the obituaries, the weather, the sound quality ("significant harmonic distortion, consistent with Class D amplifier operating beyond rated output"). He notes the time of sunrise. He notes the fog in the hollows. He notes that a pickup truck slowed on the county road, and the driver waved toward the house — toward the antenna — as if greeting someone.

Ware's technical description of the transmitter is the most detailed: the Ramsey AM-1 board, the modifications Otterbein has made over nineteen years, the salvaged components, the soldering quality ("inconsistent but functional — several cold joints, one repaired with electrical tape and rosin-core solder"). He notes that the construction method "is consistent with techniques documented in amateur and field-expedient radio construction, including wartime foxhole receiver modifications adapted for transmission." The foxhole radio reference surfaces naturally in technical documentation.

Ware interviews Otterbein and includes a longer transcription than any previous agent. Otterbein talks about the listeners — he knows some by name, though he's never met most of them. He knows because they call him on the landline after broadcasts. "Mrs. Kessler calls every morning at eight to tell me if I got a name wrong," he says. "She's been calling for eleven years. I've never met her." Ware notes: "Subject maintains awareness of his listener base through informal feedback channels not visible to spectrum monitoring."

Ware's report ends with a recommendation section that is technically proper but contains a sentence that has no place in an enforcement document: "The broadcast on 1240 kHz serves as the sole source of locally originated information for an estimated listener base of 40-120 individuals, predominantly elderly and without broadband internet access. This fact does not affect the enforcement recommendation but is noted for the record."

**7. ATTACHMENTS** (~600 words)
- Attachment A: Photograph log (described, not shown): "Photo 1: Exterior antenna, east chimney face. Photo 2: Transmitter assembly, kitchen table. Photo 3: Collection of 78 RPM recordings, shelf above transmitter. Photo 4: Handwritten broadcast schedule, taped to wall adjacent to transmitter."
- Attachment B: Spectrum analysis printout (described): frequency, bandwidth, spurious emissions. Technical data that means nothing to a lay reader and everything to an engineer.
- Attachment C: A single page — a list of names. Undated, in Otterbein's handwriting. The attachment label reads: "Document found near transmitter, not relevant to enforcement action, included for completeness." It is a list of every person whose obituary Otterbein has read on air. There are over three hundred names. The list is not complete — it appears to stop mid-page, as if still being added to.

## Emotional Trajectory

The document begins as a document — cold, procedural, formatted. The reader is positioned as someone reviewing a file. The first inspection report is competent and impersonal.

Gradually, through the correspondence (where Otterbein's handwritten voice contrasts with the FCC boilerplate), the broadcast transcripts (where the names of the dead accumulate), and the successive inspection reports (where each agent lets in a little more of what doesn't belong in the file), the document stretches without breaking. It accommodates the landscape, the grief, the community, the sound of a scratched gospel record — all within the frame of regulatory enforcement.

The acceleration comes in Ware's 2024 report, where the field agent begins to write like a journalist rather than an inspector — not because he's rebellious but because the reality he's documenting exceeds the form's capacity. This is Thompson's crack in the form, rendered not as gonzo explosion but as bureaucratic leakage.

The piece does NOT end with a resolution. The case disposition is "PENDING." Otterbein is still broadcasting. The FCC has not shut him down. The fine has been assessed but not paid. The file remains open. The last document in the file is the list of names — three hundred dead people, handwritten, still being added to, "not relevant to enforcement action."

No announcement of what it means. No lyric final paragraph. The document ends as documents end: with an attachment that shouldn't be there, included for completeness.

## Formula Integration

**Author A — Hunter S. Thompson (gonzo immersion, reporter as participant-provocateur, prose that accelerates toward chaos, the American fringe without condescension)**:
- Agent Ware's fourth inspection report progressively loses its bureaucratic distance, becoming immersive reportage. The agent sits in the driveway listening instead of immediately knocking — he has become an audience member, like Thompson becoming an Angel.
- Toomey listening to the Stamps-Baxter recording "at the subject's insistence" and writing a justification for why he stayed — the bureaucrat constructing a rationale for having participated in what he was sent to document.
- The acceleration: each successive report is longer, more personal, less contained. The form strains toward something it cannot hold.
- The American fringe without condescension: Otterbein is stubborn, sometimes petty, wrong about constitutional law, and also the only person performing a civic function that no institution will perform. He is not romanticized.

**Author B — John McPhee (geological patience, structural innovation beneath simple surfaces, letting subjects speak in their own language, deep reporting on forgotten places)**:
- The karst topography of Laclede County is documented in the technical sections — ridge interference, ground-wave conductivity, the skip zone created by limestone terrain. The geology shapes the signal. McPhee's insistence that the ground matters is honored through the FCC's own technical language.
- Otterbein's voice is preserved in direct transcription — his syntax, his additions to the obituaries, his way of speaking. The document lets him speak in his own language, even within the frame of a regulatory transcript.
- The structural innovation: the document form itself is McPhee's kind of gambit — a simple-seeming container that reveals its depth gradually. The reader discovers the story by reading what appears to be a bureaucratic file.
- The accumulation of names in the broadcast transcripts and the final attachment — three hundred dead people, listed — is McPhee's patient cataloging applied to human mortality.

**Work X — Hell's Angels (Thompson): embedding with a subculture until the line between journalist and subject blurs, violence observed at close range, America's outcasts as mirror for mainstream**:
- The successive field agents are the embedded journalists. Each one spends time with Otterbein and comes back changed — not dramatically, but in the small ways visible in what they include in their reports. Ware, the final agent, has effectively crossed the line: he's documenting a world, not a violation.
- Otterbein is the outcast-as-mirror: his illegal broadcast performs a function (reading obituaries, connecting isolated elderly residents) that the legal information infrastructure has abandoned. His lawbreaking reflects the mainstream's failure.
- The "violence" is demographic: the county is dying. The obituaries are the body count. The FCC enforcement action is the institutional violence — procedural, slow, indifferent.

**Work Y — The Pine Barrens (McPhee): a forgotten American landscape and the people who persist, deep reporting on a place most drive past, the eccentric loner as keeper of vanishing knowledge**:
- Laclede County, Missouri is the Pine Barrens: a place most drive through on the way to somewhere else, with a population that is aging, thinning, and invisible to the institutions that nominally serve it.
- Otterbein is the eccentric loner — Fred from the Pine Barrens translated to the Ozarks. He keeps knowledge (who died, what the weather is, what the extension office reports) alive through a medium (low-power AM radio) that the modern world has declared obsolete.
- The landscape is given equal weight: the karst terrain, the cedar ridges, the sinkholes, the way the ground itself is dissolving. The technical radio measurements inadvertently document the geology. The land is there before and after.

## Title

**Dust, Tape, and Signal**

"Dust" — the Ozark landscape, the accumulation of time, the deteriorating equipment, the dead. "Tape" — the electrical tape on the transmitter's cold joints, the recording tape of the monitoring logs, the tape that holds improvised infrastructure together. "Signal" — the 1240 kHz broadcast, the act of reaching out, the thing the document is trying to contain.

Does not start with "The." Diverges from existing title patterns in the creative-nonfiction collection.

## Key Scenes / Moments the Writer Must Not Miss

1. The first time Otterbein's handwritten response appears against the FCC boilerplate — the tonal collision must be immediate and felt.
2. The broadcast transcript of Darlene's obituary — the measurements recording grief ("4 min 12 sec vs. avg 11 min 30 sec") must carry the full weight without commentary.
3. Agent Ware sitting in the driveway at 6:30 AM, listening on his monitoring equipment, not entering — the moment a bureaucrat becomes an audience.
4. The pickup truck driver waving toward the antenna — a gesture of acknowledgment directed not at Otterbein but at the broadcast itself, the signal as neighbor.
5. The final attachment: three hundred names, handwritten, "not relevant to enforcement action, included for completeness." The document's last act of stretching to accommodate what it was never designed to hold.

---

## What Must NOT Happen

- No lyric frame narrative wrapping the document. The document IS the piece.
- No moment where an agent explicitly reflects on the meaning of what he's documenting. The leakage must be inadvertent, visible in what gets included rather than in what gets said about it.
- No resolution. The case is pending. Otterbein is still broadcasting. The file is still open.
- No sentimentalizing Otterbein. He is stubborn, sometimes petty, wrong about the law, and irreplaceable. All four of those things simultaneously.
- No announced theme. The reader does the work.
- No symmetrical bookend. The piece opens with a case summary sheet and ends with an attachment of names. These are not mirrors — they are different registers of the same bureaucratic container, one empty and one full.
