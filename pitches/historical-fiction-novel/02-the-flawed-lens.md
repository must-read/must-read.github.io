## Premise
A young craftsman discovers that his father — killed in a workshop accident two years ago — was actually murdered for sabotaging a secret state weapon. The evidence is hidden in a false compartment of the dead man's tool case: three months of calculations documenting intentional deviations from specification, and a final note written the night before the explosion. The son must decide whether to continue his father's resistance, knowing exactly what it cost.

## Source Inspiration
The chapter "The Discovery" in *The Feast of Ashes*, where Enzo finds Giacomo's hidden papers showing systematic sabotage of weapon lenses — "the surface reads true but the geometry lies." The father's genius was making flawed lenses that passed visual inspection but would fail at the distances needed for destruction. His final cryptic note ("If the glass breaks wrong, remember what I told your mother") transforms a workplace accident into a martyrdom. The son's three weeks studying optics gives him just enough technical knowledge to recognize what his father did — and the horror of understanding arrives through competence, not confession.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Anthony Doerr (style)**: Doerr's luminous, sensory prose — the way *All the Light We Cannot See* renders physics and radio waves as objects of wonder — matches a story where optics and light are both the science and the poetry. His short-chapter mosaic technique can interleave the father's past actions with the son's present discovery.
- **Colson Whitehead (style)**: Whitehead's ability to alternate between lyrical and brutal registers, to render institutional violence through the texture of daily life, grounds the discovery in the physical reality of a minority craftsman's existence. The yellow badge, the curfew, the precarity — these aren't decoration but the conditions that made the father's resistance both necessary and fatal.
- **The Remains of the Day (structure)**: Ishiguro's novel about a servant gradually realizing he wasted his life in service to the wrong master provides the structural model — the son's investigation is also a reckoning with what his father's death actually meant and whether the life of quiet service he's been living is its own form of complicity.
- **Hamlet (themes)**: A dead father's hidden message, a son compelled to act, a corrupt court, the question of whether resistance is duty or suicide. The parallel is direct but the short story should diverge from Hamlet's indecision — this son's choice arrives through understanding craft, not agonizing philosophy.

## Notes
The technical detail of the sabotage — lenses that "read true" on the surface but contain geometric lies — is a metaphor worth building the entire story around. The father's resistance was invisible, expert, and lethal only to himself. The son inherits not just the tools but the knowledge of what they were used for. The story's tension: the son now has the skills his father developed, in a workshop still making the same weapons. Silence is complicity. Action is death. The Hebrew inscriptions in the father's notebook add a dimension of religious contemplation alongside the technical work.
