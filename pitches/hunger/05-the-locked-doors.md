## Premise
A woman with a master's degree in library science, recently laid off, spends a single day trying to access the social services she technically qualifies for. Each office is closed, moved, understaffed, or requires documentation she does not have. The story follows her traversal of a mid-sized American city — bus transfers, waiting rooms, automated phone trees, locked doors — as the architecture of assistance reveals itself to be an architecture of exclusion. She is not starving. She is three weeks from starving. The distinction matters to no one but her.

## Source Inspiration
The "traversal of locked doors" sequence in *Hunger* Part II: the narrator moves through Christiania seeking help — editor out, friend moved, pastor absent, yarn shop owner on tour, pawnbroker laughing at coat buttons. The site's essay calls it "a labyrinth of no." The cumulative effect is not dramatic rejection but bureaucratic absence — the system is not hostile, it is simply not there. The rescue comes not from any institution but from an acquaintance who happens to appear outside the pawnshop.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Orwell)**: The documentary eye of *Down and Out* — the journalist who records the mechanisms of poverty with clinical precision. Orwell's prose is plain, declarative, and devastating in its refusal to editorialize. His particular attention to the bureaucratic infrastructure of destitution (the spike, the casual ward, the rules).
- **AuthorB (Moshfegh)**: The flat affect and dark comedy of educated women navigating systems that were not designed for them. Moshfegh's protagonists observe their own degradation with a detachment that is both protective and corrosive. The specific tone of a woman who is too smart for her situation and too proud to say so.
- **WorkX (Down and Out in Paris and London)**: The structure of a descent through institutional poverty — each chapter a different station on the way down. The contrast between educated observer and the system observed. But Orwell had an exit (he could go home); this protagonist does not.
- **WorkY (My Year of Rest and Relaxation)**: The inverse premise — a woman with resources choosing withdrawal versus a woman without resources forced into frantic engagement. The structural echo reveals what privilege makes optional. Both protagonists are numb; the causes differ.

## Notes
The city should be specific but not New York or LA — somewhere mid-tier where services exist in theory. The protagonist's library science degree should be a quiet joke: she is an expert in organizing information and cannot navigate the information system designed to help her. Each locked door should be rendered with documentary specificity — the hours posted, the website that says "open," the phone number that rings. No villain. No savior. She goes home at the end of the day with nothing resolved and eats the last can of soup in her cabinet.
