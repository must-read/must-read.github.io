## Premise
A young man from a fishing town in Maine, the first in his family to attend college, drops out after one semester and returns home on a Greyhound bus. The story is told as his night-long bus ride, during which he watches the lit windows of houses along the highway and composes, in his head, the letter he will not write to his mother explaining why he left. He is not a failure — he chose to leave — but the distinction is invisible to everyone who matters to him. The lit windows represent everything he was supposed to want and still wants and cannot have in the way it was offered.

## Source Inspiration
The final image of *Hunger*: the narrator on a ship in the fjord, gazing back at Christiania where "windows gleamed so brightly in all the homes." The site's essay identifies this as "everything denied: warmth, shelter, belonging, a desk, a lamp, a room of his own." The phrase "in all the homes" carries "the weight of everything the narrator's homelessness has meant." The narrator bids farewell "for the present" — a lie and a hope simultaneously. This pitch takes the image of gleaming windows seen from a departing vessel and transposes it to a contemporary class story about who gets to belong in institutional spaces.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Robinson)**: The theological attention to light, water, and domestic space. Robinson's *Housekeeping* is about two girls raised in a house that eventually becomes uninhabitable — the question of what "home" means when home cannot hold you. Her prose finds beauty in loss without prettifying it. The long, meditative sentences suit a night-long bus ride.
- **AuthorB (Vuong)**: The immigrant working-class perspective rendered in lyric prose. Vuong's sentences carry the weight of bodies that have labored, the beauty of language learned as a second or third inheritance. His engagement with class, masculinity, and the violence of upward mobility. The letter-to-mother form of *On Earth We're Briefly Gorgeous*.
- **WorkX (Housekeeping)**: The novel about failing to stay in a house, in a town, in a normative life. Ruth and Lucille's divergence — one stays, one leaves — and the cost of each choice. The lake that swallows everything. The drifting as both loss and freedom.
- **WorkY (On Earth We're Briefly Gorgeous)**: The letter a son writes to a mother who cannot read it — communication that is structurally one-directional. The working-class body in an institutional space. The way Vuong's narrator loves his mother and also needs to leave her. The impossibility of explaining, in any language, why you left the opportunity she sacrificed to give you.

## Notes
The bus ride should be the entire temporal frame — departure to arrival, roughly six hours. The lit windows should recur as a motif but never be sentimentalized. The letter-in-his-head should be fragmented, non-linear, honest in ways the actual conversation will not be. He should arrive at dawn. His mother should be awake, at the kitchen table, and the story should end before either of them speaks. The fishing town should be economically specific — not picturesque poverty but real industry in decline.
