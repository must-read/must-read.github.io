## Premise
A woman traveling alone through Scandinavia keeps a notebook of fragments: observations on museums, ferries, hotel rooms, strangers' conversations overheard, and a recurring fixation on Edvard Munch's diary entry about the sky turning "bloody red" over the Ekebergasen hill — the moment that became *The Scream*. She is not on vacation. She is somewhere between fleeing a marriage and researching a book she may never write. The story takes the form of her notebook: discontinuous entries that accumulate into a portrait of a mind using travel to avoid confronting what it already knows.

## Source Inspiration
The site's "Christiania: A City of Ghosts" essay connects Hamsun's *Hunger* to Munch's *The Scream*: both created in the same city, three years apart, both depicting consciousness overwhelmed by sensation. Munch's diary entry — "the sun went down, I felt a gust of melancholy. Suddenly the sky turned a bloody red. I stood there trembling with anxiety, and I felt a vast infinite scream tear through nature" — mirrors the narrator of *Hunger* on his harbor bench, overwhelmed by perception the body cannot contain. The essay also describes the Christiania Bohemians (Hans Jaeger's manifesto: "Thou shalt write thy life"), connecting artistic creation to self-documentation as survival.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Tokarczuk)**: The fragmentary travelogue structure of *Flights* — a novel built from short sections that range from personal observation to historical anecdote to philosophical meditation, unified by the theme of movement. Tokarczuk's attention to the body in transit, the psychology of displacement, the way traveling loosens identity.
- **AuthorB (Offill)**: The compressed, aphoristic fragments of *Dept. of Speculation* — a marriage dissolving in white space. Offill's sentences are short, devastating, often funny, and arranged to create meaning through juxtaposition rather than narrative continuity. The notebook form suits her style.
- **WorkX (Flights)**: The structure of a novel that moves between personal narrative, historical vignettes, and philosophical asides without hierarchy. The traveler who uses movement to think. The anatomy sections of *Flights* — bodies preserved, dissected, displayed — echoing this pitch's interest in the body under psychic stress.
- **WorkY (Dept. of Speculation)**: The disintegrating marriage told in fragments. The wife who becomes "the wife" in her own narrative — third person as dissociation. The tiny, precise notations of domestic catastrophe. The book the narrator is trying to write and cannot, which becomes the book we are reading.

## Notes
The notebook entries should range from one line to a full page. Some should be factual (museum hours, ferry schedules). Some should be lyrical. Some should be raw. The Munch fixation should recur without the protagonist understanding why she keeps returning to it — the reader should understand before she does. The marriage she is fleeing should be revealed in fragments, never directly narrated. The Scandinavian settings (Oslo, Bergen, a ferry through the fjords) should carry the weight of Hamsun's and Munch's landscapes without being literary-tourist kitsch. The notebook should end mid-entry, mid-sentence.
