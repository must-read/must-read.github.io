## Premise
A poet in Mexico City, broke and possibly delusional, spends a single night constructing an elaborate literary hoax: a fake manuscript by a famous dead writer, folded into an envelope of convincing provenance. He plans to sell it to a collector. The story follows the night of fabrication — the meticulous work of forgery blurring into genuine creation — as the poet discovers he cannot tell whether he is conning someone or producing real art. Dawn arrives. He has made something. He does not know what it is.

## Source Inspiration
The paper cornet scene in *Hunger* Part II: the narrator folds a blank page into a cone shaped to suggest it contains silver coins, then watches as a policeman opens it and finds nothing. The site's essay calls this an allegory for fiction itself — "the novelist fills empty pages with imaginary riches and then 'incites' the reader to believe they are real." The scene produces laughter that is "hushed and feverish to the intensity of tears." This is about the act of making something from nothing and the impossibility of distinguishing art from fraud when you are desperate enough.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Denis Johnson)**: Raw, hallucinatory prose. Fractured consciousness. The specific register of *Jesus' Son* where warped perception produces simultaneous devastation and hilarity. Johnson's narrators exist in a state of spiritual yearning amid degradation — exactly the territory of a broke poet forging a manuscript.
- **AuthorB (Bolano)**: The literary underworld of Latin America. Young poets maintaining absurd dignity while broke. The obsessive, doomed pursuit of literature as a life-or-death proposition. Bolano's characteristic fusion of detective story and literary quest.
- **WorkX (Jesus' Son)**: The episodic structure of linked vignettes where each scene is both self-contained and part of a larger dissolution. The narrator's unreliability is not strategic but neurological. The comedy of being too damaged to know you are damaged.
- **WorkY (The Savage Detectives)**: The quest for a lost manuscript/poet that becomes more important than the object itself. The novel's architecture of testimonies and contradictions. The idea that literary devotion is indistinguishable from madness.

## Notes
The forgery should be good. The reader should not be sure it is a forgery. The poet's technical skill is real even if his ethics are not. The story should resist making the hoax a metaphor for anything — it is a man making a fake manuscript because he needs money, and something happens to him during the making that he did not expect. Second person could work here but is not mandatory.
