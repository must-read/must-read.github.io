## Premise
A literature professor prepares to teach Knut Hamsun's *Hunger* to a graduate seminar. She has taught it for fifteen years. This semester, she has decided to tell her students about Hamsun's Nazism before they read the novel rather than after, reversing her lifelong practice. The story takes the form of her preparation notes, marginalia, and the seminar itself — a single three-hour class that becomes an argument about whether knowledge of an author's life should precede or follow the reading of their work. Her students disagree with her. She disagrees with herself. The argument has no winner, and the novel they are discussing is about a man who lies constantly and produces art that may or may not be good.

## Source Inspiration
The site's framing: "This site does not exist to rehabilitate Hamsun. It exists because *Hunger* is a great novel, and reading it carefully means holding the art and the life in the same hand without pretending either one is weightless." The site's entire apparatus — companion texts that help you read the novel while knowing who wrote it — embodies the tension between blind reading and informed reading. The question of whether knowing the creator's biography changes the creation is the site's animating paradox.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Nabokov)**: The supreme literary game-player. Nabokov's disdain for reading literature through biography ("the novelist is first of all the enchanter") meets his own complicated relationship with political history. The ornate, playful prose suits a story told partly through academic marginalia and lecture notes. The unreliable commentator who believes he is the most reliable reader in the room.
- **AuthorB (Everett)**: The author of *Erasure*, which is about a Black novelist whose satirical novel-within-a-novel is taken as authentic. Everett's engagement with how identity frames reception, how the reader's knowledge of the author changes the text. His dry, intellectual humor and structural inventiveness.
- **WorkX (Pale Fire)**: The architecture of a text with commentary that overwhelms and distorts the original. The professor's preparation notes are her Kinbote — a commentary that reveals more about the commentator than the text. The novel-within-a-seminar structure mirrors Pale Fire's novel-within-a-commentary.
- **WorkY (Erasure)**: The novel about being read wrong. The question of what happens when a text escapes its author's intentions and is received through a lens the author cannot control. The professor's reversal — telling students about Hamsun first — is an attempt to control the lens, and the story should show why this also fails.

## Notes
The seminar should contain at least four distinct student voices, each with a different position on the art/life question. The professor should be a compelling teacher — not a strawman. Her fifteen-year practice of withholding Hamsun's biography was not naive; she had good reasons. Her reversal this semester should be motivated by a specific personal event (not specified in the pitch — the story discovers it). The marginalia and lecture notes should be rendered as found documents within the narrative. The story should end with the students leaving and the professor alone in the classroom with the book open.
