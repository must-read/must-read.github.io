## Premise
A Norwegian-American historian walking through the rural Midwest — where Hamsun once worked as a farmhand — finds himself cataloguing the attractions of authoritarian community: the barn-raisings, the conformity that reads as belonging, the surrender of individual judgment that feels like relief. He is researching a book on Scandinavian immigrants, but the essay he is actually writing is about his own susceptibility to the appeal of the collective. The walk becomes a meditation on complicity, landscape, and the gap between knowing something is wrong and feeling its pull.

## Source Inspiration
The site's landing page quotes Knausgaard confessing susceptibility to the appeal of Nazi rallies: "the sheer might of the uniformed, I-less community, the strength of the collective, and oh, how one might long to be part of such a we." The essay frames this alongside Hamsun's Nazi collaboration and the site author's own insomnia over 88 million Americans supporting MAGA. The question is not "how could they?" but "what does it mean that I understand the appeal?" This is the story of a man honest enough to confess susceptibility without performing either guilt or defiance.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Sebald)**: The walking narrator who moves through landscape accumulating historical resonance. Sebald's sentences carry the weight of European catastrophe without melodrama. His method of embedding photographs, documents, and historical fragments into a meditative prose journey is the perfect form for a story about landscape and complicity.
- **AuthorB (Knausgaard)**: The confessional engine that transforms mundane experience into unbearable prose. Knausgaard's willingness to record thoughts that make him look bad — the honest confession of susceptibility rather than performed virtue. His specific engagement with Hamsun and fascism in *Book Six*.
- **WorkX (The Rings of Saturn)**: The structure of a walking tour that becomes an accretion of historical horror. Each stop on the walk reveals another layer of destruction beneath the pastoral surface. The way landscape holds memory that the walker cannot avoid receiving.
- **WorkY (My Struggle: Book Six)**: The extended meditation on Nazism, art, and personal complicity. The refusal to separate the thinker from the thought. The specific passage about watching Nazi rally footage and feeling the pull.

## Notes
Set in present-day Minnesota or North Dakota. The historian should be neither a MAGA sympathizer nor a comfortable liberal — he is someone who recognizes authoritarian appeal in his own psychology and cannot reconcile this with his politics. The Midwest landscape should carry Hamsun's ghost without being heavy-handed about it. The walk should cover one day. No resolution: he does not write the essay, or he writes it and it says something different from what he intended. The 88 million number should appear without editorial comment.
