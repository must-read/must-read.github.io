## Premise
A freelance copy editor, three months behind on rent, is asked by her landlord to reconcile the building's utility accounts. She cannot do the arithmetic. Not because she never could, but because sustained deprivation — financial, nutritional, social — has colonized the cognitive territory she needs. The story follows one afternoon as she performs competence while her mind disintegrates, told in restrained third-person that slowly fractures.

## Source Inspiration
The cheese calculation scene in Hamsun's *Hunger*: the narrator is asked to verify "5 3/16ths of a pound of cheese at nine pence" and cannot do it. The essay on the site calls this the moment where hunger has "colonized the last territory he believed secure" — his mind. The landlady's devastating tact afterward ("Excuse me for taking up your time then") permanently reclassifies him. This is a scene about cognitive collapse under material deprivation, where the sufferer's response is not despair but performance.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Ishiguro)**: Master of restrained narration where what is not said carries the weight. The protagonist's inability to acknowledge her own decline mirrors the emotional suppression in *Remains of the Day*. Ishiguro's unreliable self-assessment is perfect for a character who believes she is performing competence while visibly failing.
- **AuthorB (Saunders)**: His corporate-vernacular absurdism captures the bureaucratic cruelty of being asked to do spreadsheet work while starving. The dark comedy of performing normalcy in degrading circumstances — the Saunders mode of radical empathy inside a dehumanizing system.
- **WorkX (Hunger)**: The direct source. The spiral structure of hope-effort-failure-rescue. The specific mechanism of cognitive collapse under starvation. The landlady scene transposed to contemporary precarity.
- **WorkY (Bartleby)**: The scrivener who stops performing his function within a system that cannot process his refusal. The copy editor's growing inability to do her work echoes Bartleby's "I would prefer not to" — except here the preference is involuntary. The employer's baffled response to a competent person becoming incompetent.

## Notes
Contemporary setting: gig economy, no health insurance, food insecurity masked by educated diction. The protagonist's particular shame is the shame of educated poverty — she can describe her situation in precise, beautiful sentences but cannot add fractions. The gap between linguistic capacity and material reality is the story's engine. Should resist tidy resolution: she does not get the job, or get fed, or get saved. She gets through the afternoon.
