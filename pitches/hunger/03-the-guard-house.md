## Premise
A disgraced academic, recently fired from a small college for fabricating a reference in a published paper, checks into a budget hotel in a city where she knows no one. Over three days she interacts with the hotel staff, a persistent fellow guest, and a woman at a nearby cafe — each encounter becoming a frame in which the protagonist performs a version of herself that omits the disgrace. She tells no one the same story. The narrative is structured as a series of conversations in which the protagonist mostly listens, and what she withholds becomes the story's center of gravity.

## Source Inspiration
The guard-house scene in *Hunger* Part II: the narrator gives a false name (Andreas Tangen) and claims to be a journalist, securing the gentlemen's cell — but his lie prevents him from claiming the breakfast ticket distributed to the genuinely homeless. The site's essay identifies the core mechanism: "Performance purchases dignity at cost of food." The fictions that save the narrator become traps that prevent him from receiving the help he needs. Dignity and survival are in direct, irreconcilable conflict.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Coetzee)**: The prose of a man who maintains elaborate performance of dignity through refusal and withholding. David Lurie in *Disgrace* discovers that dignity becomes "volatile and strange" when stripped of institutional support. Coetzee's austere, controlled sentences are the perfect vehicle for a character who will not allow herself to be seen.
- **AuthorB (Cusk)**: The *Outline* trilogy's method of constructing a protagonist through other people's stories. The protagonist as listener, as absence, as the negative space around which other lives arrange themselves. Cusk's essayistic austerity and her refusal of conventional interiority.
- **WorkX (Disgrace)**: The architecture of a fall from professional grace. The refusal to apologize or perform contrition. The way the novel strips its protagonist of everything — job, daughter's safety, sexual dignity — and watches what remains.
- **WorkY (Outline)**: The structure of a novel told entirely through encounters with strangers. The protagonist who reveals herself only through what she chooses to ask others. The radical proposition that a character can be built from omission.

## Notes
The fabricated reference should be ambiguous — not a clear fraud but a gray-area citation that the protagonist half-believes was honest. The story should not adjudicate her guilt. Each conversation should reveal something about the other person that accidentally illuminates the protagonist's situation without her acknowledging it. The hotel setting provides natural transience — everyone is between places. No redemption arc. She leaves on the fourth day. The reader knows something the other characters do not, and this knowledge is uncomfortable.
