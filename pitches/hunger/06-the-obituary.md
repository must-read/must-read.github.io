## Premise
May 7, 1945. A young Norwegian typesetter at the *Aftenposten* newspaper in Oslo receives a submission: Knut Hamsun's obituary for Adolf Hitler, calling him "a warrior for humankind." The typesetter must set the type. He is twenty-three. His older brother was executed by the Germans in 1943. His hands do the work. The story follows the hours between receiving the copy and the paper going to press — the mechanical labor of arranging metal letters that spell words the typesetter finds monstrous, while around him the city erupts in liberation celebrations.

## Source Inspiration
The site's "After Hunger" essay details the historical fact: Hamsun published his Hitler obituary in *Aftenposten* on the day the war in Europe ended, calling Hitler "a reformer of highest order." The essay emphasizes this was not a private diary entry but a deliberate public statement by an 85-year-old man who knew exactly what he was doing. The typesetter is invented, but someone real set that type. The story is about the people who transmit monstrous words through mechanical labor — the hands that do what the mind refuses.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Sebald)**: The prose of historical aftermath — how catastrophe embeds itself in ordinary objects and mechanical processes. Sebald's narrators encounter history through material artifacts (photographs, architecture, documents), and a typesetter encounters language as physical metal. The melancholy precision of Sebald's sentences suits a man doing careful work while in moral agony.
- **AuthorB (Marias)**: The Spanish novelist's obsession with faces, betrayal, and the question of what you do with knowledge of another person's evil. The long, circling sentences of *Your Face Tomorrow* that delay judgment while accumulating evidence. Marias writes about complicity as a condition that arrives before you notice it.
- **WorkX (Austerlitz)**: The architecture of suppressed memory. The way objects and spaces hold historical violence that their current occupants cannot see. The typesetting equipment is the architecture here — the same machines that printed resistance news now print a Nazi eulogy.
- **WorkY (Your Face Tomorrow)**: The three-volume meditation on what it means to know who someone really is and what to do with that knowledge. The central question: when you see a person's face clearly, what action does that clarity demand? The typesetter sees Hamsun's words clearly. What does he do?

## Notes
The mechanical process of hand typesetting should be rendered with precise technical detail — composing stick, type case, leading, galley proof. The physicality of arranging individual letters that spell "warrior for humankind" should be the story's sensory center. The liberation celebrations outside should be audible throughout. The typesetter should not make a grand gesture of refusal or sabotage. He sets the type. That is the horror and the point. The story is about the countless people who transmit evil through ordinary professional labor.
