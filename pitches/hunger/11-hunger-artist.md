## Premise
In a near-future city where food production has been optimized and hunger eliminated, a woman begins to fast. Not as protest, not as illness, not as religion — she simply stops being hungry. Doctors examine her. Social workers intervene. Her employer mandates nutritional compliance. But her body is not malfunctioning; it has opted out of a system everyone else inhabits without question. As her fast continues, she discovers she can perceive things others cannot — patterns in the city's infrastructure, gaps in collective memory, the architecture of what has been removed to create this frictionless world. The story follows her growing clarity as the society around her grows increasingly alarmed.

## Source Inspiration
The site's "After Hunger" essay connects Hamsun's novel to Kafka's "A Hunger Artist" as a "direct descendant" — the starving man as parable of artistic medium. But the site's deeper insight is about hunger as consciousness: "The body lies to the mind, and the mind believes it. The starving man does not think, 'I am light-headed from hunger and therefore feel grandiose.' He thinks, 'I am strong as a giant.'" What if hunger is not deprivation but perception — and a world without hunger is a world that has sacrificed the ability to see clearly?

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Kafka)**: The bureaucratic surrealism of a system that cannot process deviation. Kafka's prose — precise, calm, procedural — describes impossible situations as though they were administrative matters. The woman's fast is not dramatic; it is a paperwork problem. The state must categorize her condition and cannot.
- **AuthorB (Ogawa)**: The quiet, methodical erasure of *The Memory Police* — a world where things disappear and people forget they existed. Ogawa's prose is gentle and devastating. Her characters accept loss with a politeness that is itself a form of horror. The optimized city in this pitch has Ogawa's quality of pleasantness concealing systematic removal.
- **WorkX (A Hunger Artist)**: The fasting performer whose art is his own deprivation, caged and observed, who dies whispering that he fasted only because he never found food he liked. The parable of the artist whose medium is refusal — and the public that watches without understanding.
- **WorkY (The Memory Police)**: The architecture of a society that eliminates categories of experience and punishes those who remember. The protagonist who hides her ability to remember in a world that has chosen to forget. The slow, courteous totalitarianism of optimization.

## Notes
The near-future setting should be barely speculative — recognizable as our world with one system perfected. No dystopian trappings. The city is pleasant, clean, well-fed. The horror is entirely in the pleasantness. The woman's perceptions as she fasts should be specific and strange — not mystical visions but structural observations (she notices which buildings are missing, which streets end abruptly, which words have dropped from common usage). The ending should be ambiguous: is she seeing truth or starving into delusion? The story inherits Hamsun's central question — does hunger sharpen the mind or destroy it? — and refuses to answer.
