## Premise
A woman keeps waking up in the same week. Not a time loop — she ages, her body changes, she remembers each repetition — but the same seven days recur with slight variations. Monday she interviews for a job she will not get. Wednesday her ex calls. Friday she eats the last of something. Sunday she sleeps. Each cycle intensifies: the job interview grows more absurd, the ex's call more desperate, the food more meager, the sleep more like death. She is aware of the repetition but unable to alter its structure. The story follows four cycles, each compressed tighter than the last, until the pattern breaks — or she does.

## Source Inspiration
The site's central analytical insight about *Hunger*: the novel's spiral structure. The same emotional arc (hope-effort-failure-rescue) repeats four times, but "each cycle intensifies rather than repeats, creating accumulation rather than redundancy." The essay compares it to musical variation form — "each part in a different key." The first reading is suspenseful (will he survive?); the second reveals the pattern as fate. This pitch takes the spiral literally: what if you could see the pattern repeating and still could not escape it?

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Beckett)**: The master of repetition with diminishing returns. Beckett's narrators cycle through the same gestures, the same failures, the same impossible tasks, each iteration stripping away another layer of pretense. *Molloy*'s two-part structure of pursuit and dissolution. The comedy of being trapped in a system you can describe perfectly but cannot exit.
- **AuthorB (Machado)**: The body as site of horror and transformation. Machado's surrealism is grounded in physical experience — the green ribbon, the inventory of lovers, the body that is always being read and rewritten by others. Her feminist reclamation of genre tropes gives the spiral structure a gendered dimension: the week that repeats is a week of specifically female labor and disappointment.
- **WorkX (Molloy)**: The novel in two halves that mirror and distort each other. The quest that becomes its own obstacle. The deteriorating body as the only reliable narrator. Molloy's sucking stones — the obsessive system that occupies the mind while the body falls apart.
- **WorkY (Her Body and Other Parties)**: The inventory structure. The body as text. The way Machado's stories use genre (horror, romance, fairy tale) as lenses through which to examine real violence against women. The specific story "The Husband Stitch" where a woman's body carries a secret that kills her when revealed.

## Notes
Each of the four cycles should be told in a different register — the first realistic, the second slightly heightened, the third absurdist, the fourth compressed to near-poetry. The woman's awareness of repetition should not make her wise or philosophical; it should make her tired. The structural question: is the repetition the world doing something to her, or her mind doing something to itself? The story should not answer this. It should end mid-cycle, in the middle of a sentence she has said before.
