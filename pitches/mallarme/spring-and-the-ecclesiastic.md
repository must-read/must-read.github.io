idea: "A woman walking her dog in a London park in April witnesses a Catholic priest — fully robed, mid-fifties, overweight — throw himself onto the grass and roll in it like a child, face pressed to the earth, laughing. She watches for two full minutes. He doesn't see her. The story follows the woman home, where she describes the scene to her husband, her sister on the phone, and finally her therapist. Each retelling changes — first comic, then envious, then theological, then confessional. By the final version she is no longer talking about the priest at all. She is talking about every form of ecstasy she has denied herself in the name of dignity. The priest never appears again. He doesn't need to."
notes: "From Mallarme's piece about witnessing a priest ecstatically rolling in spring grass, surrendering celibate constraints to physical pleasure. Cusk's austere observation-as-narrative (each retelling of the scene is a different self-portrait of the narrator) meets O'Connor's violent moments of grace (the priest's abandon is both grotesque and holy). WorkX provides the structural architecture of a story told through conversations that reveal the teller. WorkY provides the theme of a self-righteous person undone by a sudden, unwanted revelation about their own nature."
