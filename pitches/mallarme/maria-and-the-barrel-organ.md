idea: "After his wife's death, a retired piano tuner lives alone with a cat in a second-floor apartment. He reads Latin poetry he no longer understands. He favors autumn. One October afternoon, a street musician with a hand-cranked organ appears below his window and plays a waltz that his wife used to hum. The story follows the tuner through the next four hours — making tea, feeding the cat, sitting in the chair by the window — as the music triggers not a flood of memory but a slow, almost geological rearrangement of grief. The musician leaves. The tuner does nothing different. But the story has shifted him, imperceptibly, and the reader can feel it."
notes: "From Mallarme's piece about solitude after Maria's departure, the cat, the autumn season, and the barrel organ that triggers overwhelming memory. Robinson's meditative theological attention to ordinary domestic moments meets Hemingway's theory of omission — the emotion is entirely beneath the surface. WorkX provides the architecture of grief experienced through a house and its small rituals. WorkY provides the theme of a person alone at the end of the night, sustaining themselves through tiny acts of order against the void."
