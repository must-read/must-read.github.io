idea: "A writer takes a regional train to a famous forest in late October, off-season, specifically to be alone among the trees. Fellow passengers chatter about restaurants and parking and phone signal. The writer waits for them to disperse at the trailhead, then enters the forest alone. The essay follows a three-hour walk in which the writer attempts to see the forest without the mediation of previous descriptions — no Romantic sublime, no ecological framework, no metaphor. Just trees. The attempt fails, beautifully. Every observation triggers a reference, a memory, a comparison. The forest is never just itself. The writer sits on a fallen oak and admits that pure perception is impossible for a literate person. The essay ends not with transcendence but with the precise, unsentimental description of one leaf, decomposing."
notes: "From Mallarme's final piece about arriving at Fontainebleau forest by train, resisting tourist chatter and urban banality, seeking 'an extraordinary state of illusion' through solitary communion with autumn woods. Dillard's mystical nature attention (seeing as spiritual practice, the attempt to look without flinching) meets Sebald's digressive, melancholy walking prose (the inability to see a place without seeing its history). WorkX provides the structure of sustained, patient observation of the natural world as a mode of inquiry. WorkY provides the architecture of a walking narrative where the landscape triggers layered memory and the impossibility of pure experience."
