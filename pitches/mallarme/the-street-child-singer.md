idea: "A twelve-year-old busker sings R&B covers in a subway station, projecting his voice with a ferocity that stops commuters mid-stride. A social worker on her way home pauses to listen and recognizes — not the boy, but the exact quality of defiance she once had herself, before college, before the credential, before she learned to modulate. She watches him for the duration of one song. She does not intervene, does not offer money or a card. She walks on. The story alternates between the boy's morning (waking in a cousin's apartment, choosing which songs earn the most, the geometry of foot traffic) and the social worker's evening (a glass of wine, case files, her own mother's voice on the phone asking if she's eating). Neither knows the other exists as a full person. The story does."
notes: "From Mallarme's piece about an orphaned boy singing in the streets, the narrator's admiration mixed with dark prophecy about where poverty leads. Adichie's precise social realism and cultural duality (the social worker's two selves) meets Johnson's raw, fractured consciousness and spiritual yearning (the boy's voice as both survival mechanism and involuntary prayer). WorkX provides the structural architecture of parallel lives that illuminate each other through contrast. WorkY provides the theme of grace appearing in degraded circumstances, witnessed by damaged narrators."
