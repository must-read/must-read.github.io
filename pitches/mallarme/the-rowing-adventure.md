idea: "A landscape architect, recently divorced, borrows a kayak on a slow river to survey riparian plantings for a county project. She drifts too far and finds herself at the edge of a private estate — an overgrown garden, a dock, footsteps on gravel. She never sees the owner clearly: a figure in white moving between hedgerows, the sound of a screen door. She could call out. She could dock the kayak and introduce herself. Instead she lets the current take her back. Over the following week she returns to the same stretch of river three times, each time seeing evidence of the gardener — pruning shears left on the dock, a glass of iced tea, a paperback face-down — but never the person. The story is about choosing the imagined encounter over the real one, and the precise moment when she decides to finally dock."
notes: "From Mallarme's rowing piece where the narrator drifts to an unknown woman's private park, encounters feminine mystery through glimpses and sounds, but deliberately avoids confirmation, preferring imagination to reality. Henry's witty first-person narration and complicated emotional dynamics meets Murakami's dreamlike melancholy and the longing for someone barely seen. WorkX provides the structure of a story where the setting (the river, the garden) is a character in the romance. WorkY provides the theme of desire for someone who may be more powerful as an absence than a presence."
