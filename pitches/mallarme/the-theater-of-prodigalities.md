idea: "At a children's theater matinee of 'Beauty and the Beast,' a philosophy professor watches the audience more closely than the stage. The production features a mime and a trained bear (actually a man in a bear suit, but the professor isn't sure). When the beast is offered roses and candlelight but instead lunges for a raw steak thrown from the wings — breaking character, breaking the fourth wall — the children scream with delight while the parents recoil. The professor, alone in the back row, writes in his notebook: 'The bear chose correctly.' The story intercuts the performance with the professor's increasingly unhinged margin notes about civilization, appetite, and the 'theatrical conspiracy' of pretending humans are not animals. He is fired from his university the following week for unrelated reasons that feel, to him, entirely related."
notes: "From Mallarme's piece about watching a clown-and-bear act where the bear abandons 'civilized' performance to eat raw meat, while the narrator claims superior perception. Wilde's epigrammatic wit and the comedy of surfaces (the professor's pretension, the theater as social ritual) meets Saunders' corporate-absurdist satire (the university, the firing, the delusion). WorkX provides the structural template of social performance collapsing into honest appetite. WorkY provides the theme of institutional decay witnessed by someone who thinks they're above it but isn't."
