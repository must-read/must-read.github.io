idea: "A foster kid, fifteen, runs away from a group home to a traveling carnival that's set up in a Walmart parking lot. He befriends a young acrobat — a girl his age who's been working the circuit since she was nine. Over one night, sharing funnel cake behind the Gravitron, they exchange origin stories with the competitive gallows humor of people who have earned the right to joke about abandonment. She dismisses parents as a concept: 'They're like appendixes. Most people have them. Some people don't. Nobody dies from not having one.' He's not sure he agrees but he laughs. By morning the carnival is packing up. She doesn't invite him. He doesn't ask. The story captures the precise texture of connection between two people who have no safety net — how tenderness looks different when nobody taught you the word."
notes: "From Mallarme's piece about an orphan finding kinship with a young acrobat at a fair — camaraderie born from mutual deprivation. Saunders' radical empathy and vernacular voices in surreal situations (the Walmart parking lot carnival, the specificity of institutional foster care) meets O'Connor's violent moments of grace (the girl's dismissal of parents is both funny and devastating). WorkX provides the structure of a single compressed encounter that illuminates entire lives. WorkY provides the theme of characters in transit who collide briefly and reveal each other's depths."
