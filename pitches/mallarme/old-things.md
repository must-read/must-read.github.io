idea: "Two elderly sisters live together in the house where they grew up, surrounded by objects they refuse to replace: a stopped Saxon clock, a Venetian mirror going blind with age, chairs worn to the shape of bodies no longer alive. They speak to each other in a private language of references to the dead. A nephew visits to discuss selling the house. Through the visit, the story reveals — through the sisters' affectionate, detailed attention to every cobweb and water stain — that their love for decay is not nostalgia but a theology. They believe deterioration is how objects become sacred. The nephew leaves disturbed. The sisters remain, tending their relics."
notes: "From Mallarme's piece about two figures discussing their shared love for aged household objects — the Saxon clock, the Venetian mirror, the spiderwebs and dust. Jackson's domestic unease (the house as organism, the inhabitants as its willing captives) meets Robinson's theological meditation on ordinary objects given radiant attention. WorkX provides the architecture of two sisters in a house that is its own world, hostile to outsiders. WorkY provides the theme of loving attention paid to the deteriorating world as a form of spiritual practice."
