## Premise
A retired government bureaucrat in a near-future America, twenty years after a period of democratic backsliding, prepares to testify before a truth commission. As he rehearses his testimony with his estranged daughter -- who was a child when he signed the deportation orders -- the story excavates the distance between what he did, what he remembers doing, and what his daughter cannot forgive. He keeps returning to the phrase "I followed the process," and the story slowly reveals what the process actually was.

## Source Inspiration
The "Tolerance by the Numbers" essay's devastating "impunity funnel" -- the finding that in every historical case of mass atrocity, fewer than 1% of perpetrators face consequences -- and the analysis of how post-MAGA justice would likely resemble South Africa's TRC more than Nuremberg. The essay's most haunting idea: that the people who caused the damage largely escape consequences while their inheritors bear the repair burden.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Kazuo Ishiguro)**: Ishiguro's mastery of the unreliable narrator who slowly reveals his complicity through carefully maintained dignity. Stevens in *Remains of the Day* is the blueprint for a character who cannot fully see what he has done. The restrained, understated narration would prevent the story from becoming didactic.
- **AuthorB (Roberto Bolano)**: Bolano's long-breathed, circling prose style that approaches atrocity obliquely -- the way *2666* and *By Night in Chile* deal with state violence through accumulating detail rather than direct confrontation. His work understands that bureaucratic evil is banal and literary simultaneously.
- **WorkX (The Remains of the Day)**: Structural influence -- the story unfolds as a journey of reckoning, where each memory the protagonist revisits reveals more than he intends. The architecture of gradual, devastating self-exposure.
- **WorkY (The Reader)**: Thematic influence -- the next generation's relationship to perpetrators, the impossibility of understanding complicity from outside it, and the question of whether literacy (education, culture, civilized norms) can coexist with participation in atrocity.

## Notes
The essay's breakdown of MAGA coalition types (29% hardliners, 30% mainline, 20% reluctant) suggests the protagonist should be from the "mainline" or "reluctant" category -- someone who wasn't ideologically driven but participated through institutional loyalty. The daughter's anger would be more complex than righteous because the essay makes clear that writing off a third of the electorate is counterproductive. Risk card candidate: "Unreliable narrator" or "Withheld information" would pair naturally with this material.
