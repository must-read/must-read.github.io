## Premise
A former college basketball analytics consultant -- the woman who built the prediction models for a mid-major program's shocking tournament run -- now works alone in an insurance actuary's office. She hasn't watched basketball in four years. When her old head coach calls to say the program has been shut down and he wants her to help him write the program's history, she agrees, but the story she tells him about their miracle season is not the story he remembers. The 73% accuracy ceiling -- the fact that no model can reliably predict more -- becomes a metaphor for the limits of knowing anyone, including yourself.

## Source Inspiration
The "March Madness and the Limits of Prediction" essay's core insight: that the tournament "lives in the sweet spot -- predictable enough to reward knowledge, chaotic enough to reward hope." The 73% ceiling is haunting because it means roughly one in four outcomes will always defy the best available understanding. The essay's finding that "approximate predictive accuracy" matters more than any individual prediction maps onto a story about the gap between what we think we know about our own lives and what actually happens.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Don DeLillo)**: DeLillo's prose captures the eerie, almost metaphysical quality of systems and numbers -- the way data can feel like a kind of religion. His flat, precise sentences would serve the analytics world well, and his characters often stand at the edge of something vast and indifferent.
- **AuthorB (Ottessa Moshfegh)**: Moshfegh's uncomfortable interiority -- characters who are brilliant and self-destructive, who see too clearly and act against their own interests. Her protagonists withdraw from the world in ways that are repulsive and sympathetic simultaneously. The analytics consultant's retreat from basketball needs this kind of unflinching inwardness.
- **WorkX (The Art of Fielding)**: Structural influence -- a college sports story that is really about perfectionism, failure, and the moment when mastery collapses into paralysis. The novel's architecture of tracking multiple characters through a season provides a model for weaving past and present.
- **WorkY (The Remains of the Day)**: Thematic influence -- the reckoning with a career defined by service to someone else's vision, and the dawning realization that professional excellence was a substitute for living. The coach is the protagonist's Lord Darlington -- someone she devoted herself to who may not have deserved it.

## Notes
The essay's detail about psychological biases in bracket selection (availability heuristic, narrative fallacy, illusion of control) could be woven into the protagonist's self-awareness -- she recognizes these biases in others but cannot see them operating in her own life. The 27% she can never predict is where the story's emotional weight lives.
