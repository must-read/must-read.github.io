## Idea

A correctional officer at a federal detention center is assigned to monitor the surveillance cameras on a high-profile inmate's cell block. The cameras malfunction. They have always malfunctioned on this corridor. Maintenance has been called fourteen times. Each work order is completed and signed off. Each time the cameras fail again within days. The officer begins keeping a private log of the failures, and the log itself becomes the narrative -- a document that records not what happened, but the precise architecture of not-seeing. On the night the inmate dies, the officer is asleep. Both officers are asleep. The cameras are dark. The officer's log has one final entry, written the next morning: "Cameras down. Usual."

## Source Material

Epstein died on August 10, 2019, in the Metropolitan Correctional Center. "Multiple irregularities noted: guards asleep, cameras malfunctioning, suspicious positioning." Guards were later charged with falsifying records and sleeping on duty; cases were plea-bargained. The source frames the death as preventing "trial examination of co-conspirator network." The circumstances -- the sleeping guards, the broken cameras, the convenient timing -- remain the subject of intense public suspicion.

## Why These Authors/Works

- **Jackson's** domestic gothic applied to institutional space -- the building that does not want you to see; the horror of conformity; the way a system produces its own blindness and calls it normal
- **DeLillo's** paranoid systems-level thinking -- is this conspiracy or entropy? The surveillance state that fails to surveil at the exact moment it matters; the gap between what the cameras should record and what they do
- **The Shining** (King): the institutional building as malevolent presence; the hotel/prison that has its own agenda; the caretaker who is supposed to watch and instead becomes part of what's being watched
- **House of Leaves** (Danielewski): the document as unreliable record; the nested layers of testimony; the space that is larger inside than outside; a narrative that is also an investigation into its own gaps

## Core Tension

The story never states whether the death was murder or suicide. It doesn't need to. The horror is that a system designed to observe produced systematic blindness, and that the blindness was documented -- logged, filed, work-ordered -- at every step. The building didn't hide what happened. It simply arranged itself so that no one was looking.
