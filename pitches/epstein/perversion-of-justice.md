## Idea

A fictionalized literary journalist reconstructing how she broke the story that restarted a dead investigation. She worked the story for two years while every official source told her the case was closed, the deal was done, the victims were unreliable. The piece alternates between her reporting process -- the dead ends, the doors that close, the sources who recant -- and the institutional silence she is trying to penetrate. The question beneath the story: why did it take a newspaper to do what the FBI, DOJ, and state attorneys refused to do for twenty-two years?

## Source Material

Julie K. Brown's November 2018 Miami Herald investigation, "Perversion of Justice," is identified in the source as the turning point that "revived public attention" and created the pressure that led to Epstein's 2019 arrest. The source notes: "Media finally conducts investigative work authorities refused for 23 years, creating public pressure for action." Brown's investigation succeeded where the FBI (1996), the Palm Beach police (2005-2006), and federal prosecutors (2007-2008) had all been stymied or compromised.

## Why These Authors/Works

- **Didion's** cool, precise, devastating observation -- the journalist who watches institutions fail and renders the failure in prose so clean it cuts; California cool applied to Florida corruption; the sentence as scalpel
- **Talese's** elegant narrative journalism -- the novelistic scene-setting; the long-form architecture; the reporter's craft rendered as its own subject
- **In Cold Blood** (Capote): the investigation as narrative; the journalist who embeds so deeply in the story that the boundaries between reporter and subject blur; the question of what journalism can and cannot do
- **The Journalist and the Murderer** (Malcolm): the ethics of the journalist-subject relationship; the uncomfortable truth that the reporter is always using the story and the story is always using the reporter

## Core Tension

The journalist accomplishes what the entire federal government would not. But the story also reveals the limits of journalism: she can create pressure, she can expose, she can shame -- but she cannot prosecute. And the system that failed for twenty-two years is the same system that must now deliver the justice her reporting demands.
