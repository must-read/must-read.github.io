## Idea

A fictionalized account of a fundraising gala in the late 1990s, told from the perspective of a young development officer at a Democratic political organization. Her job is to cultivate major donors. One of her most reliable donors is a charming financier who gives generously to both parties, hosts luminaries on his private island, and whose name appears on every guest list in Manhattan. She knows nothing about him except that his checks clear and his parties are legendary. The story follows a single evening at a charity event where the financier is the guest of honor, and the development officer watches senators, governors, and cultural figures compete for his attention. Years later, she is asked whether she noticed anything. She noticed everything. She noticed that no one ever asked where the money came from.

## Source Material

Epstein's bipartisan donor network included $20,000 to Hillary Clinton's 1999 Senate campaign, donations to the DNC (which "retained at least $32,000"), relationships with Senators Schumer and Kerry, financial backing from Leslie Wexner (major Republican donor), and social ties with Bill Gates and Reid Hoffman. The source emphasizes that "both parties were willing to accept Epstein's money and maintain relationships with him despite his known criminal history." Epstein's 1973 arrest for unlawful sexual abuse was public record.

## Why These Authors/Works

- **Waugh's** savagely funny social satire -- the upper-class event rendered with precision so sharp it draws blood; the comedy of manners where the manners are the crime; the party as moral autopsy
- **Saunders's** corporate dystopia played for laughs -- the development officer's internal monologue, half fundraising metrics and half moral nausea, the language of philanthropy as the language of complicity
- **The Great Gatsby** (Fitzgerald): the party as American theatre; the host whose wealth is both seductive and criminal; the narrator who watches and participates and later reconstructs the evening as elegy and indictment
- **American Psycho** (Ellis): the surface perfection concealing monstrosity; the financial class whose rituals of consumption are indistinguishable from their rituals of destruction; the narrator who tells you everything and sees nothing

## Core Tension

The development officer is not naive. She is competent, observant, and good at her job. Her job is to not ask questions. The comedy is that the entire room -- senators, philanthropists, journalists, celebrities -- is performing the same job. Everyone is a development officer. Everyone's checks clear.
