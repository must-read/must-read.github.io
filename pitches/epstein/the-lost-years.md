## Idea

In a near-future democracy, a mid-level bureaucrat at the Department of Justice discovers a file on her desk -- a complete investigation into a powerful man, ready for prosecution, that has been passed from administration to administration for over a decade without anyone ever acting on it. Each administration stamped it "Under Review" and forwarded it to the next. She traces the file's journey through eight years of one party's control and then six of the other's, and realizes the file was never lost or buried. It was always visible. No one ever chose to open it.

## Source Material

Between 2009 and 2017 (eight years of Obama's DOJ under Holder and Lynch), zero new federal charges were filed against Epstein despite the existing evidence. Between 2021 and 2024 (Biden's DOJ under Garland), zero new co-conspirator prosecutions occurred. The case was not hidden -- it was simply never prioritized by either party. The source describes this as "institutional failure" that "is not a bug in American governance -- it is a feature."

## Why These Authors/Works

- **Orwell's** transparent, precise prose applied to bureaucratic horror -- the language of governance as the instrument of its own paralysis; the horror of a system that functions exactly as designed
- **Ishiguro's** quiet, never-noticed dystopia -- the protagonist who realizes the system she serves is not broken, it is working, and the working is the horror
- **The Trial** (Kafka): an accusation that exists in perpetuity without ever reaching judgment; a system where process replaces justice entirely
- **Blindness** (Saramago): a society-wide failure that no individual caused and no individual can fix; the moral collapse that occurs when everyone agrees not to see

## Core Tension

The bureaucrat's realization that the file's journey is not a scandal or a cover-up. It is the system functioning normally. The question is not who buried the case, but what kind of government requires no conspiracy to produce thirty years of inaction.
