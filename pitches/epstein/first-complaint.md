## Idea

Set in 1996. A young woman files a formal FBI complaint documenting abuse, a trafficking pipeline, and the methods of a wealthy financier operating from a billionaire's Ohio property. She names names. She provides dates. She describes the system. The FBI opens an investigation and closes it within months without charges or follow-up. The story follows the woman through the filing, the brief flicker of institutional response, and the twenty-three years of silence that follow. It is not a story about the abuse. It is a story about what happens to a person who tells the truth to a system that is not designed to hear it.

## Source Material

Maria Farmer was the first documented victim. In September 1996, she filed a formal FBI complaint "detailing abuse, trafficking pipeline, and methods." The FBI opened an investigation but closed it within months "without charges or follow-up." The source describes this as beginning "a 23-year pattern of inaction with no explanation provided." Twenty-three years passed before the arrest that vindicated her complaint.

## Why These Authors/Works

- **Barker's** spare, psychologically acute prose -- trauma rendered without sentimentality; the working-class voice confronting an institution that speaks a different language; the psychological cost of not being believed
- **Whitehead's** alternating registers from lyrical to brutal -- the Black American tradition of testifying to a system built not to listen; the speculative-inflected sense that the real world operates on hidden rules the protagonist cannot see
- **Speak** (Anderson): the experience of telling a truth that the world is structured to deny; the isolation of the witness; the slow, grinding cost of being the first person to say what happened
- **The Lovely Bones** (Sebold): the victim's perspective on a system that moves on while she remains fixed in the moment of the crime; the unbearable duration of justice delayed; watching from outside as the world forgets

## Core Tension

The protagonist did everything right. She documented. She reported. She cooperated. She was specific, credible, and brave. The system received her complaint, stamped it, filed it, and forgot it. The story is about what twenty-three years of institutional silence does to a person who told the truth on the first day.
