## Idea

A Palm Beach police detective builds the most comprehensive case of his career. He has victim testimony, physical evidence, corroboration, a trafficking network mapped and documented. His chief calls it "as serious as it gets." He hands the case to the state attorney's office for prosecution. The state attorney sends it to a grand jury and then presents the victims in a way designed to destroy their credibility. The grand jury returns a single misdemeanor. The detective watches his case -- eighteen months of work, dozens of victims -- reduced to a parking ticket. The story follows the detective as he tries to understand how a case this strong could produce a result this weak, and slowly realizes that the strength of the case was never the issue.

## Source Material

Palm Beach Police Chief Michael Reiter took the investigation seriously, calling it "as serious as it gets." The police investigation was "sophisticated, identifying trafficking networks and documenting Epstein's methods despite political pressure." The completed investigation was forwarded to State Attorney Barry Krischer, who sent it to a grand jury and "inexplicably" presented victims as "prostitutes" -- producing a single solicitation charge from what should have been multiple felonies. The FBI then opened "Operation Leap Year" and federal AUSA Marie Villafana drafted a 53-page, 60-count indictment that was never filed.

## Why These Authors/Works

- **Lehane's** working-class voice and emotional devastation beneath crime narratives -- the detective who does everything right and watches the system betray the victims; Boston noir's moral seriousness transplanted to Palm Beach
- **French's** atmospheric, psychologically rich prose -- the investigation as a journey into institutional corruption; memory and identity as mystery; the detective who cannot let go of the case that defined him
- **L.A. Confidential** (Ellroy): the police procedural that reveals the cops and the prosecutors are the problem; institutional corruption at every level; the detective who follows the evidence into territory the system does not want illuminated
- **Zodiac** (Fincher/Graysmith): the investigator consumed by a case the system has abandoned; the obsessive pursuit of justice that the institutions charged with justice have given up on; the human cost of being the last person who cares

## Core Tension

The detective built a perfect case. The case was not defeated by the defense. It was defeated by the prosecution. And the detective has to live in the same county, the same system, with the same people who dismantled his work, for the rest of his career.
