## Idea

A fictionalized account of a state attorney who deliberately sabotages a grand jury proceeding against a powerful predator. The story is told from the perspective of the lone female prosecutor on the team who drafted the original 60-count indictment and watches in real time as her boss presents the underage victims to the grand jury as "prostitutes," systematically destroying the case from inside.

## Source Material

Barry Krischer (D), the Palm Beach state attorney, received a comprehensive police investigation ready for felony charges in 2006. Instead of pursuing state charges, he sent the case to a grand jury and then presented victims in a way designed to discredit them -- referring to underage girls as "prostitutes." A 60-count federal indictment was drafted but never filed. The grand jury returned a single solicitation count. No explanation was ever provided.

## Why These Authors/Works

- **Ellroy's** staccato, telegraphic prose and obsessive LA-crime-history voice transplanted to Palm Beach -- the breathless institutional corruption, the documents as evidence, the system eating itself
- **Abbott's** noir sensibility applied to female experience -- the young female prosecutor watching the institutional machinery grind the victims, her own complicity in the system she thought she was reforming
- **An American Tragedy** (Dreiser): the crushing weight of class and institutional power determining who gets justice; a system where the outcome is decided before the trial begins
- **A Time to Kill** (Grisham): the courtroom as moral arena; the gap between law and justice; a legal professional confronting the failure of the system they serve

## Core Tension

The protagonist drafted the strongest indictment of her career. She watches her own boss dismantle it from the inside, and she has to decide whether to go along, resign, or blow the whistle -- knowing that any of these choices leads to the same outcome for the victims.
