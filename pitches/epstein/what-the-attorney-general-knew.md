## Idea

Five attorneys general across three decades. Each inherits the same case file. Each does nothing. The story presents five brief monologues -- one per AG -- each explaining why they did not act. The first explains that the evidence was insufficient. The second explains that the political timing was wrong. The third explains that the case was a prior administration's problem. The fourth explains that prosecution would be seen as partisan. The fifth explains that too much time has passed. Each monologue is rational, professional, and complete. Together, they form a perfect closed loop: a system in which there is never a right time, a right reason, or a right person to pursue justice, because the system has been designed so that inaction is always the most defensible choice.

## Source Material

The source documents five distinct periods of AG inaction: Janet Reno (1996 FBI complaint ignored), Eric Holder (2009-2015, zero new charges), Loretta Lynch (2015-2017, zero new charges), Merrick Garland (2021-2025, zero co-conspirator prosecutions), and the essay's treatment of Barr and Bondi. Each AG had evidence, authority, and jurisdiction. None acted proactively. The source's core conclusion: "The system produced consistent results regardless of which party held power."

## Why These Authors/Works

- **Camus's** lucid, clean prose -- the absurd examined with clarity; each monologue delivered with the detachment of Meursault; the philosophical horror of five reasonable people producing an unreasonable outcome
- **Orwell's** transparent, precise prose -- "Politics and the English Language" as aesthetic; each AG's monologue is a case study in how political language converts inaction into policy, complicity into prudence, failure into professionalism
- **The Stranger** (Camus): the narrator who explains everything with perfect logic and perfect moral absence; the trial of a man who is guilty of something other than what he's accused of; the sun as the reason for everything
- **Eichmann in Jerusalem** (Arendt): the banality of institutional failure; each participant doing their job, following procedure, making defensible decisions, and the aggregate producing something none of them would individually endorse; the desk as the site of moral collapse

## Core Tension

No AG is villainous. Each is competent, politically sophisticated, and defensible. That is the horror. Five careful, justified decisions to do nothing produce thirty years of impunity for a child sex trafficking network. The story asks whether a system that makes inaction always defensible can ever produce justice.
