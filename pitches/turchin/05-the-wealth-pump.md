## Premise
A long-haul trucker in the American interior -- eastern Ohio, West Virginia, western Pennsylvania -- drives the same route he has driven for nineteen years, watching the towns along his corridor hollow out in real time. Each trip, another storefront dark, another church converted to storage, another family he knew by name gone. He is not political. He does not have a theory. He has a route, and the route is dying, and he is the last person who sees it whole because he passes through every two weeks while everyone else is trapped inside one town's particular collapse. The story follows him over three runs across six weeks as his employer announces the route is being automated.

## Source Inspiration
The essays describe "the wealth pump" -- mechanisms extracting wealth from workers and transferring it upward: declining unions, labor market globalization, automation, capital-favoring tax policy, and financialization. The concept of "popular immiseration" -- not absolute poverty but relative decline, people expecting improvement but experiencing decline -- is the trucker's world. The insight that "immiseration without poverty" triggers instability because people measuring themselves against their parents' lives find the comparison devastating.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Cormac McCarthy (style)**: McCarthy's biblical, incantatory prose about the American landscape -- the way he renders geography as fate and makes physical terrain carry metaphysical weight -- is essential for a story about driving through dying America. His stripped syntax and refusal of quotation marks create a voice that feels carved from the landscape itself.
- **Denis Johnson (style)**: Johnson's raw, hallucinatory prose and spiritual yearning amid degradation provide the trucker's interior register -- a man who is not intellectual but who perceives with terrible clarity. Johnson's characters see more than they can articulate, and the gap between perception and expression is where the pathos lives.
- **Blood Meridian by Cormac McCarthy (structure)**: The structure of a journey through a landscape that is simultaneously real and mythic, where each stop reveals a new facet of a single systemic violence. The episodic journey structure, with each town as a station of the cross.
- **Jesus' Son by Denis Johnson (themes)**: The themes of bearing witness to collapse without the vocabulary to explain it, of being inside a system whose logic is invisible but whose effects are devastating. The unnamed narrator of Jesus' Son stumbles through a world he cannot quite interpret; the trucker drives through one.

## Notes
The automation announcement should not be the climax -- it should be one more thing, absorbed with the same flat resignation as every other loss. The story's power should come from accumulation rather than crisis: the slow, granular documentation of what "popular immiseration" looks like at ground level, in specific diners and specific gas stations and specific conversations with specific people who are running out of options. No policy solutions. No epiphanies. Just the route, and the route ending.
