## Premise
Rome, 133 BCE. A minor clerk in the offices of the Senate records the proceedings on the day Tiberius Gracchus proposes his land reform bill -- the day that, as the cliodynamics essays argue, "opened Pandora's box" by normalizing political violence in Rome. The clerk is a freedman who understands both the desperate need for reform (he sees the displaced farmers flooding into Rome daily) and the structural impossibility of achieving it (the senators whose land would be redistributed control every lever of power). Over one brutal summer, he watches the Republic's immune system turn against its own reformer, and records -- with increasing horror -- how each escalation makes the next one thinkable.

## Source Inspiration
The Rome essay identifies Tiberius Gracchus's murder in 133 BCE as the pivotal moment when structural pressures overwhelmed institutional norms. The essay describes how "frustrated elites provide leadership, resources, and organization" for revolutionary movements, and how the combination of popular immiseration, elite overproduction, and state fiscal strain creates conditions where violence becomes rational. The freedman clerk's perspective allows the story to see both sides without resolving the contradiction.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Hilary Mantel (style)**: Mantel's ability to render historical power dynamics through intimate, present-tense narration -- making the reader feel the physical texture of political maneuvering -- is essential for a story set in the Roman Senate. Her prose makes institutional machinery feel visceral.
- **Robert Graves (style)**: Graves's sardonic, insider voice -- the wry observer who sees the absurdity and brutality of power from within -- provides the clerk's register. His ability to make ancient Rome feel lived-in rather than museum-piece.
- **I, Claudius by Robert Graves (structure)**: The structure of a seemingly marginal observer narrating the collapse of a political system from within its machinery. Claudius survived by being overlooked; the clerk survives by being useful but invisible.
- **Wolf Hall by Hilary Mantel (themes)**: The theme of a brilliant pragmatist navigating a system whose rules are changing faster than anyone can adapt. Cromwell understood that the old certainties were dissolving; the clerk sees the same dissolution in the Republic's norms.

## Notes
The story should resist the temptation to make Tiberius Gracchus purely heroic or purely reckless. The structural-demographic framing suggests he was responding to real pressures that the Senate's institutional structure could not address -- but his methods created precedents that enabled worse actors later. The clerk's final act of recording could be framed as either futile bureaucratic habit or a desperate act of witness.
