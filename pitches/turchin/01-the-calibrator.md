## Premise
A social scientist at a government forecasting bureau discovers her mathematical models of societal collapse are converging on a crisis date fifteen years out -- but the model's accuracy depends on a parameter she cannot measure directly: the precise threshold at which elite frustration becomes revolutionary violence. As she refines her calibrations against historical data, she realizes the model's predictions are already leaking into policy circles and changing the very dynamics she is trying to measure, creating a reflexivity loop that makes her forecasts simultaneously more urgent and less reliable.

## Source Inspiration
The essays on parameter calibration and forecasting describe the "reflexivity problem" -- published forecasts can become self-fulfilling or self-defeating because they influence the systems they describe. The calibration essay's comparison of parameter-fitting to detective work, and the identifiability crisis where multiple contradictory reconstructions prove equally valid, offer rich dramatic tension. Turchin's actual 2010 prediction of 2020 instability provides a real-world anchor.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Ted Chiang (style)**: Chiang's crystalline, philosophical short fiction -- each story a thought experiment rendered precisely -- is ideal for a narrative built around a mathematical concept. His ability to make abstract ideas emotionally devastating matches the story's need to ground equations in human stakes.
- **Kazuo Ishiguro (style)**: Ishiguro's restrained, understated narration and unreliable memory provide the emotional register for a protagonist who gradually realizes she cannot trust her own model -- or her own role in the system she studies. The quiet devastation of comprehension arriving too late.
- **Foundation by Isaac Asimov (structure)**: Asimov's psychohistory -- mathematical prediction of civilizational trajectories -- provides the structural DNA. The story mirrors Foundation's central tension: can a science of history actually work, and what happens to the scientists who wield it?
- **Never Let Me Go by Kazuo Ishiguro (themes)**: The theme of people who understand their fate intellectually but cannot emotionally process or escape it. The calibrator knows what is coming, has the numbers, but is trapped inside the system her model describes.

## Notes
The story could hinge on the moment the protagonist realizes that her most accurate calibration -- the one that fits all historical data perfectly -- requires her own society to be further along the crisis curve than anyone publicly acknowledges. The model works best when it assumes things are already worse than they appear. A quiet, devastating ending where precision itself becomes the antagonist.
