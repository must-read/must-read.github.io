## Premise
A theoretical physicist has spent twenty years working on a unified field theory that is beautiful, mathematically rigorous, and untestable. Over two decades, she has watched the testability horizon recede -- first the experiments were five years away, then ten, then twenty, then "next generation." The story is told in fragmented chronology, jumping between key moments: the day she chose the problem, a conference where a colleague called her work "ironic science," the birth of her daughter, a grant review where she described her theory as "approaching testability," a dinner with a string theorist who admitted privately that none of them expected verification in their lifetimes. The emotional arc is the slow, undramatic realization that she has spent her career on something that may be a myth dressed in equations.

## Source Inspiration
Section 7 ("Beauty") describes how "mathematical elegance serves as useful heuristic but becomes dogma when substituted for evidence," with string theory as the central example: "deep mathematics and intricate structure lacking testable predictions." Section 15 ("The Performance") describes "horizon-shifting -- moving testability windows from one year to five to twenty -- keeping claims in regions where falsification couldn't occur within budget cycles." Horgan's concept of "ironic science" -- "indefinite theory-tweaking without decisive tests" -- provides the intellectual frame.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Jennifer Egan (style)**: Egan's mastery of temporal fragmentation and the way time transforms meaning. A scene that feels triumphant at 30 looks different at 50. Her ability to show how a life's narrative shape only becomes visible in retrospect.
- **Lauren Groff (style)**: Groff's intensity with interior life, her ability to render intellectual and creative ambition as a physical, bodily experience. The physicist's relationship to her equations should feel as visceral as any love affair.
- **A Visit from the Goon Squad (structure)**: Non-linear chapters, each a different moment in time, each revealing something about the others. The fragmentation mirrors the physicist's own inability to see the shape of her career from inside it. Time itself is the goon squad.
- **Lab Girl (themes)**: The theme of a woman building a scientific life against institutional resistance, and the question of what counts as a meaningful contribution when the work is slow, unglamorous, and may never culminate. The devotion to process when outcome is uncertain.

## Notes
The story should not conclude that her work was wasted. It should leave genuinely open whether beautiful mathematics that cannot be tested is a contribution to human understanding or an elaborate form of self-deception. The colleague who calls it "ironic science" should be sympathetic, not cruel. The physicist should be honest with herself -- she knows the horizon keeps moving. The question is whether that matters, and the story should refuse to answer. Strong candidate for the "non-linear time" risk card.
