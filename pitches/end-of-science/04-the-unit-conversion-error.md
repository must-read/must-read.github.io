## Premise
Gareth O'Neill, a data scientist, spends a week debugging a dataset that shows a dramatic, publication-worthy effect. The result would make his career -- it is clean, statistically significant, and narratively satisfying. On day five, he finds the error: a unit-conversion mistake buried in a preprocessing step. The dramatic effect disappears. The real data shows nothing interesting. Gareth must decide whether to publish the correction -- knowing that the error has already been informally shared, that a collaborator has cited the preliminary result in a grant application, and that silence is the easiest path. The story is told through the week of debugging, and the real subject is the texture of honesty when honesty costs you everything and protects no one.

## Source Inspiration
Section 12 ("The Hands") describes Gareth O'Neill, who "spends a week debugging a dataset showing dramatic effect, finding a unit-conversion error that would have become a headline, choosing silence over publication because attention encourages others toward similar errors, protecting method by refusing story." The essay frames this as an act of invisible heroism -- the choice to not publish, to absorb the cost of honesty privately. The broader argument is that method is maintained by individual acts of renunciation that no institution rewards.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Rachel Cusk (style)**: Cusk's stripped-down, observational prose where the narrator's interiority is revealed through the precise description of external events. Gareth's week of debugging should unfold with Cusk's clinical attention to the texture of each day -- the screen glare, the coffee, the slow dread.
- **Ottessa Moshfegh (style)**: Moshfegh's unflinching rendering of characters in states of moral and physical discomfort. Gareth should feel physically ill as the truth clarifies. Her dark humor would capture the absurdity of a career hinging on a unit conversion.
- **Outline (structure)**: A narrative built from encounters and conversations in which the protagonist is revealed obliquely. Gareth's week should include conversations with colleagues, a lunch with the collaborator, a call with his department head -- each encounter reflecting a different angle of his dilemma without him stating it directly.
- **White Noise (themes)**: The ambient dread of living inside systems whose logic you can see but cannot escape. The background hum of institutional pressure that makes the wrong choice feel like the natural one. The way modernity converts private moral clarity into public irrelevance.

## Notes
The ending should be genuinely ambiguous. The essay says Gareth "chose silence over publication" -- meaning he did not publish the exciting result. But the story could explore what that silence costs: the collaborator's grant, the team's morale, Gareth's own career trajectory. The point is that doing the right thing does not feel heroic. It feels like loss. No one thanks him. Nothing changes. The method is preserved, and no one notices.
