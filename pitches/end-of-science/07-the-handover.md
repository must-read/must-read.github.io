## Premise
Dr. Ayo Fadahunsi is the last human auditor at a pharmaceutical research facility where every other stage of drug discovery -- hypothesis generation, simulation, experimental design, data analysis -- has been automated. She is told her role is essential: the human check on machine reasoning. But over six months, she realizes she can no longer meaningfully audit the systems she oversees. The data volumes exceed human interpretive capacity. The algorithms operate in representational spaces she cannot visualize. Her signatures on audit reports are ceremonial -- she is approving work she cannot evaluate. The story follows her attempt to build an automated audit system to replace herself, and the recursive problem she encounters: if the auditor is part of the system, who audits the auditor?

## Source Inspiration
Section 18 ("Drift") describes how automation progressed from "tedious analysis" to "simulation-outsourcing and experimental-design-outsourcing," with each step shifting authority toward machines. The key insight: "When systems become too complex for manual auditing, automated audit itself becomes necessary, making the auditor part of the system and blurring accountability." The essay frames this as a rational, inevitable process that nonetheless creates conditions where no human can verify what the machines are doing.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Stanislaw Lem (style)**: Lem's philosophical rigor about the limits of human understanding when confronting alien intelligence. Ayo's machines are not alien in origin but are alien in operation -- she cannot follow their reasoning, only their outputs. Lem's unresolved investigations of unknowable systems are the perfect register.
- **Colson Whitehead (style)**: Whitehead's ability to ground speculative scenarios in the lived, bodily experience of his protagonists. Ayo should be a fully realized person -- her commute, her coffee, her conversations with her mother -- not just a philosophical position in a lab coat.
- **Solaris (structure)**: A single protagonist confronting a system that resists human comprehension, with each attempt at understanding revealing new layers of incomprehensibility. The structure spirals inward: each audit Ayo conducts reveals a deeper level she cannot reach.
- **Klara and the Sun (themes)**: The theme of observation without comprehension -- watching a system operate, noting its patterns, forming theories about its motivations, and never knowing whether your understanding is real or projected. Ayo observes the machines as Klara observes humans: with attention, devotion, and fundamental unknowing.

## Notes
The ending should resist the temptation to make Ayo either a whistleblower or a resigned collaborator. The more interesting question is: what does it mean to be a scientist in a world where science has exceeded the capacity of any individual human to verify? She is not being deceived. The machines are not malicious. The system works. She simply cannot tell if it works for the right reasons. The audit problem is epistemological, not ethical, and the story should stay in that uncomfortable space.
