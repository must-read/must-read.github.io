## Premise
A radio astronomer has spent fifteen years listening to pulsars -- the dead, spinning remnants of collapsed stars that tick with metronomic regularity. She is part of a timing array: dozens of telescopes around the world, all measuring the same pulsars, looking for a collective wobble that would indicate gravitational waves washing through the galaxy. For fifteen years, the signal has been indistinguishable from noise. The story follows the day the signal emerges -- not as a dramatic revelation but as a slow statistical accumulation, a line on a graph crossing a threshold. The real story is what fifteen years of patient listening does to a person. What it means to spend your professional life waiting for a signal that might never come, and what it feels like when it does, and why the feeling is nothing like what you expected.

## Source Inspiration
Section 11 ("The Record") describes NANOGrav's June 2023 announcement of gravitational wave background detection through "fifteen-year pulsar-timing analysis," calling it "patient science: measurement using the most reliable clocks humans found, detecting slow signals consistent with supermassive black hole pairs, with cautious language about evidence rather than certainty." Section 25 ("The Signal") notes that NANOGrav "resisted myth-making through cautious language (evidence, consistent, future-correction-space) despite salon reinterpretation as universe-decoding proclamation."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Annie Dillard (style)**: Dillard's ability to render sustained attention to the natural world as a spiritual practice. The astronomer's fifteen years of pulsar-listening should feel like Dillard's creek-watching: an act of devotion that transforms the observer. Her prose makes patience feel like a form of intelligence.
- **Denis Johnson (style)**: Johnson's spare, hallucinatory prose and his gift for rendering altered states of consciousness. Fifteen years of listening to cosmic noise produces its own altered state. Johnson's ability to make the mundane feel numinous without sentimentality.
- **Pilgrim at Tinker Creek (structure)**: A meditation structured around a single place and sustained observation, where the subject is not the thing observed but the quality of attention itself. The radio telescope is the creek; the pulsars are the muskrats. The structure is contemplative, recursive, building meaning through accumulation.
- **Contact (themes)**: The theme of listening for a signal from the cosmos and what the act of listening means for the listener. But where Sagan's signal is dramatic and transformative, this signal is statistical and ambiguous. The gravitational wave background is not a message -- it is evidence of distant collisions. The universe is not speaking to us. It is just there.

## Notes
The tone should avoid triumph. The real NANOGrav announcement was cautious, almost anticlimactic -- "evidence consistent with" rather than "we have detected." The astronomer should feel something more complicated than joy when the signal emerges. Relief, certainly. But also a kind of loss -- the waiting was the work, and now the waiting is over. The story could be structured around a single day (the announcement day) with fifteen years of memory woven through. Avoid making the science inaccessible; Dillard's influence should make the technical luminous.
