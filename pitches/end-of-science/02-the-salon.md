## Premise
A scientific communications officer at a fusion energy startup is summoned at 4 a.m. to prepare a predawn boardroom presentation for the firm's four major investors. The company has achieved a genuine but modest milestone -- a marginal improvement in energy gain. Her job is to transform this into a narrative of cosmic inevitability. Over the course of one night, she builds the deck: moving-line projections, the word "inevitability" deployed twelve times, "uncertainty" never once, caveats hidden in footnotes no one will read. She knows exactly what she is doing. The investors need destiny, not data. The story follows the presentation from preparation through delivery, tracking how a careful scientist becomes a fluent pseudoscientist, and whether there is a meaningful difference between the two when the system rewards only one.

## Source Inspiration
Section 9 ("The Theater") describes a predawn boardroom presentation in which Claudel delivers moving-line projections about "invisible energy channels linking cosmology and commerce," using "inevitability" repeatedly while avoiding "uncertainty" language and hiding caveats in footnotes. The essay argues that "pseudoscience operates not through invented data but through misrepresented scope and hidden alternatives." Section 8 ("The Clients") describes the four patron-archetypes: Evelyn Rusk (energy), Tomas Vale (capital), Rhea Calder (defense), and Ansel Pryce (media) -- each requiring certainty for different operational reasons.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Don DeLillo (style)**: DeLillo's ability to render corporate and financial power as a kind of metaphysics -- the rhythms of capital as a language with its own grammar. His prose makes boardrooms feel like temples, which is exactly how the presentation should feel: religious, not rational.
- **George Saunders (style)**: Saunders's ear for institutional euphemism, the way organizations make exploitation sound like opportunity. The dark comedy of a deck that says "energy abundance trajectory" when it means "we need your money for eighteen more months."
- **Cosmopolis (structure)**: A compressed timeframe, a single trajectory through a hermetic world of wealth and abstraction, where the protagonist moves through space while the real action is conceptual and linguistic. The limo becomes the boardroom; the journey is the presentation itself.
- **The Circle (themes)**: The theme of transparency rhetoric concealing extraction -- here, the language of scientific openness ("we are sharing our breakthrough") deployed to prevent actual scrutiny. The way tech-utopian framing makes critique feel like Luddism.

## Notes
The protagonist should not be a villain. She should be brilliant, exhausted, and aware of every compromise she is making. The story's tension is that the presentation works -- the investors are moved, the funding continues, and the genuine science underneath gets another year to run. The pseudoscience protects the real science. This is the essay's central moral ambiguity, and the story should refuse to resolve it. Consider the "story-as-document" risk card -- told through the deck itself, with annotations revealing what each slide conceals.
