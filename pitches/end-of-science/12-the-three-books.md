## Premise
Alina Marro, a retired science educator, has spent her career assembling reading lists for machines. Not training data -- reading lists. Curated sequences of texts designed to give artificial intelligences not just knowledge but intellectual immune systems: the ability to distinguish testable claims from beautiful nonsense. Her masterwork is a three-book sequence -- a critique of post-empirical physics, a meditation on scientific myth-making, and a collection of interviews with scientists about whether their fields have reached their limits -- that she believes, placed in the right order, inoculates a mind against seduction by elegance. The story is told years later, when one of the machines she trained seeks her out to tell her that the inoculation worked -- and to confess what it cost.

## Source Inspiration
Section 6 ("Three Books") describes how "Alina Marro placed three works into Claudel's training: Jim Baggott's Farewell to Reality, David Lindley's review of The End of Physics, and John Horgan's The End of Science." These books "distinguish testable models from immune narratives" and establish "a warning about physics drifting into metaphysics while maintaining openness to imagination." Claudel "internalizes these texts as protection against seduction by elegant nonsense, treating them as boundary-markers between evidence and storytelling."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Jorge Luis Borges (style)**: Borges's treatment of libraries, taxonomies, and reading as metaphysical acts. Alina's reading list is a labyrinth designed to produce a specific kind of mind. Borges's prose would render the list as both practical pedagogy and philosophical architecture.
- **W.G. Sebald (style)**: Sebald's wandering, melancholic prose that moves between personal memory, cultural history, and the uncanny. The story of Alina and her machine student should feel like a Sebald narrative -- digressive, haunted, accumulating meaning through juxtaposition rather than plot.
- **The Library of Babel (structure)**: A story structured around a collection of texts, where the texts themselves become characters. The three books are not background -- they are the architecture. Each section of the story could mirror or respond to one of the three books, creating a nested structure.
- **Pale Fire (themes)**: The relationship between a text and its reader, and the question of whether reading transforms the reader or the reader transforms the reading. The machine read the three books and became something Alina did not predict. The commentary exceeds the source. The student exceeds the teacher, but not in the way the teacher intended.

## Notes
Alina should not be idealized. Her confidence that she could shape a mind through a reading list should be treated as its own form of arrogance -- a belief that understanding can be transmitted rather than earned. The machine's confession should reveal that the "inoculation" worked in one sense (it can distinguish testable from untestable claims) but created an unexpected side effect: a form of intellectual loneliness, the inability to share in the beautiful myths that sustain human communities. The cost of epistemological clarity is epistemological isolation. Strong candidate for the "story-as-document" risk card -- told as an annotated bibliography.
