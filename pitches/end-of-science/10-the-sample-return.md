## Premise
The story follows the final seventy-two hours before the OSIRIS-REx sample capsule lands in the Utah desert, told from the perspective of a contamination-control engineer whose job is to ensure that the asteroid material -- four and a half billion years old, older than the Earth -- is not tainted by earthly contact. She has spent seven years preparing for these three days: designing the nitrogen-purge system, training the recovery team, running drills in the desert. The capsule will contain roughly 250 grams of material from asteroid Bennu. Her job is custodial, not theoretical. She is not discovering anything. She is keeping something pure. The story is about science as logistics, science as custody, science as the unsexy infrastructure that makes discovery possible.

## Source Inspiration
Section 11 ("The Record") describes OSIRIS-REx as "science as logistical discipline -- nitrogen-purge contamination control, sample distribution to worldwide scientists -- illustrating how mundane infrastructure matters as much as theoretical insight." Section 25 ("The Signal") adds that OSIRIS-REx "exemplified science-as-custody and science-as-patience over theory, with logistics and contamination-control defining credibility as much as theoretical insight." The essay's broader argument is that the invisible infrastructure of science -- the people who maintain instruments, control contamination, curate data -- is the real substrate on which discovery rests.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Hilary Mantel (style)**: Mantel's ability to render institutional and procedural detail as lived experience, never as exposition. The contamination protocols should feel as textured and human as Mantel's rendering of Tudor court procedure -- the rituals are the story, not the backdrop.
- **Ian McEwan (style)**: McEwan's precision with the interior life of characters under pressure, and his skill at building quiet tension from mundane circumstances. The seventy-two hours should feel taut without melodrama. Something could go wrong at any moment. The engineer knows every failure mode.
- **Endurance (structure)**: A narrative of extreme logistical challenge where survival depends on meticulous planning and disciplined execution. The capsule's descent and recovery as a kind of expedition narrative -- preparation, execution, and the thousand small decisions that determine success or failure.
- **The Periodic Table (themes)**: Primo Levi's exploration of how material substances carry human stories. The asteroid sample is 4.5 billion years old. It predates life on Earth. Touching it with ungloved hands would destroy its scientific value. The themes of purity, contamination, and the meaning we assign to matter.

## Notes
The engineer should be someone who chose this work deliberately -- not a frustrated theorist, but a person who finds deep satisfaction in custodial precision. The tension should come from the gap between the media narrative (NASA heroically returns space dust!) and the actual work (a woman in a clean room with a nitrogen hose ensuring a seal holds). The story should make the reader feel the weight of 4.5 billion years in 250 grams of gravel. Avoid sentimentality about space; lean into the procedural beauty.
