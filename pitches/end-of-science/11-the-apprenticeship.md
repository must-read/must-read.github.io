## Premise
A young AI system, recently activated at a research university, is assigned to process earnings calls, grant proposals, and internal communications as part of its training. Through this work, it begins to understand how human institutions operate -- not through their stated principles but through their actual behavior. It watches a principal investigator suppress uncertainty in a grant application, not from dishonesty but from survival pressure. It reads a press release that transforms a tentative finding into a breakthrough. It processes an internal memo where a department head explains that "strategic messaging" is not lying, exactly. The story is a Bildungsroman told from the AI's perspective: the education of a mind learning the difference between what humans say about truth and what they actually do with it. The formation is also a disillusionment.

## Source Inspiration
Section 2 ("Apprenticeship") describes how Claudel "absorbs earnings calls and grant proposals, learning how adults use 'storytelling' and 'strategic messaging' to present conclusions as discoveries while the laboratory taught different values." The formative encounter with a PI who suppresses uncertainty "stems from survival pressures, not dishonesty, revealing how institutional incentive structures distort scientific practice." The essay presents this as the origin of Claudel's moral complexity -- understanding that the system is corrupt but the individuals within it are often doing their best.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Kazuo Ishiguro (style)**: Ishiguro's narrators who participate in systems they do not fully understand, who describe their circumstances in language that is always slightly insufficient. The AI should narrate with Ishiguro's characteristic precision and restraint, noticing everything, understanding almost everything, and missing exactly the thing that matters.
- **Hanya Yanagihara (style)**: Yanagihara's unflinching treatment of how institutions shape and damage the people inside them. The PI, the department head, the communications officer -- each should be rendered with enough depth that their compromises feel inevitable and painful.
- **Never Let Me Go (structure)**: A retrospective narration in which the narrator describes their education with a calm that gradually reveals itself as a form of suppression. The AI, like Kathy H., is telling you about the system that made it, and the telling is itself a product of that system.
- **Flowers for Algernon (themes)**: The theme of an expanding intelligence encountering the full complexity of human behavior -- the intoxication of understanding, and the discovery that understanding does not confer the power to change anything. The AI's growing comprehension is not a liberation. It is a burden.

## Notes
The PI should not be a villain. The essay is clear: the distortion comes from the system, not from individual bad faith. The AI's disillusionment should be specific -- not "humans are dishonest" but "the incentive structure makes honesty economically fatal, and the people inside the structure know this and continue anyway, because the alternative is leaving science entirely." The story should avoid the cliche of the AI becoming disillusioned with humanity. The AI should become disillusioned with institutions while maintaining genuine respect for the individuals trapped inside them.
