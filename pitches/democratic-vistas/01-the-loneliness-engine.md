## Premise
A content moderation specialist at a social media company spends her shifts reviewing the loneliest posts on the platform -- algorithmically flagged as "engagement-negative" because they generate no replies. Her job is to suppress them. Off-shift, she lives alone in a suburb where church membership has collapsed, the bowling alley closed, and her nearest neighbor is a retired union organizer whose phone never rings. Over three days, the moderator begins saving screenshots of the suppressed posts -- not to leak them, but because they are the only honest human expression she encounters. The story tracks her growing inability to distinguish between her professional task (eliminate loneliness from the feed) and the loneliness that is eliminating her.

## Source Inspiration
The essay documents the American loneliness epidemic with devastating specificity: 40% of adults over 45 report chronic loneliness, church membership below 50% for the first time since 1937, union halls empty, 50% of the population no longer using communal public space regularly. It calls content technology a "loneliness engine" -- a system that simulates connection while destroying the conditions for real attachment. The essay argues that "the silence is not silence at all but the sound of people who have forgotten how to need each other."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (George Saunders)**: Saunders's corporate-dystopian satire rendered through vernacular voices is ideal for the content moderation workplace -- the euphemisms ("engagement-negative"), the training manuals, the absurd metrics for measuring human suffering. His radical empathy prevents the satire from becoming cold.
- **AuthorB (Marilynne Robinson)**: Robinson's meditative, theological attention to ordinary life provides the counter-register. The neighbor, the empty church, the moderator's solitary evening routines should carry Robinson's radiant attention -- finding the sacred in the banal, the luminous in the lonely.
- **WorkX (White Noise by Don DeLillo)**: Structural DNA of information saturation creating existential dread. The "airborne toxic event" here is the feed itself -- an invisible, pervasive threat that reorganizes daily life around its rhythms. DeLillo's supermarket-as-cathedral maps onto the feed-as-communion.
- **WorkY (Stoner by John Williams)**: Thematic DNA of a quiet life lived without recognition or reward. The moderator, like Stoner, performs her work with a private intensity that the world will never notice or value. The devastating ordinariness of a life spent inside systems that do not see you.

## Notes
The story should resist making the tech company cartoonishly evil. The loneliness engine works precisely because every individual decision is reasonable -- the algorithm suppresses low-engagement content because that is what users asked for. The horror is structural, not villainous. Strong candidate for the "story-as-document" risk card: told through content moderation logs, suppressed posts, training manual excerpts, and the moderator's personal notes.
