## Premise
A 71-year-old retired machinist in rural Iowa -- plant closed in 2016, wife dead of pancreatic cancer in 2021, left knee replaced, right knee deteriorating -- carries a volunteer fire department pager that has been his tether to purpose for forty-nine years. The story covers a single Tuesday: the morning cafe meeting with four other retired men (a mail carrier, a hog farmer, an electrician, a hardware store manager), an afternoon spent replacing a section of fence on a neighbor's property, and an evening call -- a car accident on Route 69 involving a teenager. He extracts the kid. The pager technology is being replaced by a smartphone app. He does not own a smartphone. The fire chief, who is thirty-four, has been trying to have a conversation with him about this for six weeks.

## Source Inspiration
The portrait of Dale Hendricks in "Democratic Portraits" -- a retired machinist and volunteer firefighter who replaced the weather stripping on his back door the day after his wife's funeral, lifts his arthritic Lab into the truck daily despite his bad knee, and votes in a pattern (Obama, Trump, Biden) that "makes sense individually but doesn't resolve into a category." The essay's larger argument about comradeship -- "fierce, ungovernable attachment" between people who maintain connection through "minimal ritual" -- manifests in the Tuesday/Thursday cafe meetings and the volunteer fire department.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Ernest Hemingway)**: Hemingway's theory of omission -- the grief never named, the meaning beneath the surface of spare declarative sentences. Dale's domestic actions (replacing weather stripping, lifting the dog) are Hemingway's iceberg: enormous feeling expressed through minimal physical gesture. The terse, declarative rhythm of a man who communicates through action, not speech.
- **AuthorB (Larry McMurtry)**: McMurtry's elegiac treatment of the dying West -- here transplanted to the dying rural Midwest. The same quality of warmth and loss, the same knowledge that this way of life is ending and nobody is going to save it, the same love for the specific landscape and the specific men who inhabited it.
- **WorkX (The Old Man and the Sea by Hemingway)**: Structural DNA of an aging man performing a physical task that tests him beyond his capacity, alone, with dignity. The car extraction on Route 69 is the marlin -- the thing that requires everything he has, and the drive home afterward is the skeleton lashed to the boat.
- **WorkY (Lonesome Dove by McMurtry)**: Thematic DNA of men held together by shared labor and unspoken loyalty, in a world that has moved past them. The cafe men are the retired Rangers. The pager is the code. The smartphone app is the railroad.

## Notes
The fire chief should not be a villain. He is kind, practical, and correct -- the app is better. The story's tension is not about technology replacing tradition. It is about what happens to a man whose last connection to usefulness depends on a device that is being retired. The arthritic Lab, Biscuit, should appear in exactly three scenes, each time requiring the old man to bend his bad knee to lift her. The dog should not be a symbol. The dog should be a dog.
