## Premise
A night-shift charge nurse at a public hospital in Atlanta -- the kind that serves the uninsured, the homeless, the gunshot and the overdose -- watches a woman pray with open eyes in the waiting room at 3 AM. The woman is staring at a vending machine. She is praying in Spanish for her daughter's survival. The nurse has seen this before, hundreds of times, but tonight something shifts: she begins to see the prayers. Not metaphorically -- literally. The fluorescent lights flicker in the cadence of the woman's breathing. The vending machine hums a note that matches her voice. The other patients in the waiting room arrange themselves, unknowingly, into a pattern the nurse recognizes from a church she attended as a child. When the doctor emerges at 7 AM to say the daughter has survived, the woman's joy and grief are visually indistinguishable, and the nurse cannot tell whether what she witnessed was a miracle, exhaustion, or the ordinary operation of a hospital at 3 AM.

## Source Inspiration
The hospital scene from "Between Two Chasms" -- a woman praying in Spanish with open eyes, gazing at a vending machine, her daughter in surgery, joy and grief visually indistinguishable when the doctor delivers good news. Combined with the portrait of Keisha Wardlow: a night-shift charge nurse at Grady Memorial in Atlanta who works 12-hour shifts, manages psychiatric crises and gunshot wounds, suffers plantar fasciitis and tension headaches starting at 2 AM, and has witnessed a 50-year-old diabetic die for lack of "$10 of insulin."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Flannery O'Connor)**: O'Connor's violent moments of grace -- the sudden, disorienting intrusion of the sacred into the grotesque mundane. The nurse's vision should carry O'Connor's quality of grace arriving as shock, unwanted, unasked for, and impossible to refuse. Her darkly comic Southern voice grounds the supernatural in specific, physical reality.
- **AuthorB (Mariana Enriquez)**: Enriquez's Latin American horror-realism where the supernatural bleeds from social reality -- poverty, violence, and ghosts intertwined. The hospital itself is a liminal space where the boundary between the living and the dead is already thin. Her dread makes the miracle ambiguous.
- **WorkX (A Good Man Is Hard to Find by O'Connor)**: Structural DNA of ordinary circumstances escalating toward a moment of terrible grace. The night shift is the road trip; each patient encounter ratchets the tension; the praying woman is the moment where everything breaks open and the nurse must confront something she has spent seven years of night shifts refusing to see.
- **WorkY (The Hour of the Star by Clarice Lispector)**: Thematic DNA of one consciousness trying to see another consciousness fully and failing. The nurse tries to see the praying woman -- really see her -- and the story asks whether that act of perception is itself a form of prayer, or just another professional skill.

## Notes
The slipstream element should be radically ambiguous -- every "supernatural" detail has a mundane explanation (fluorescent lights flicker, vending machines hum, exhausted people see patterns). The story should never confirm or deny. The nurse's faith background (she sings alto in a Baptist choir) provides the vocabulary but not the certainty. The ending should be the nurse driving home at 6:30 AM, picking up her sleeping daughter from her mother's house, and making breakfast in a kitchen where the light is ordinary.
