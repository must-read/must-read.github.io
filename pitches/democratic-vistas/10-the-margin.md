## Premise
A 29-year-old home health aide in the hills of West Virginia drives a Chevy Cruze with the check-engine light on for eleven months, visiting eleven clients across the hollows of Ohio County. She changes sheets, checks blood pressure, administers medications, listens to stories, and worries about whether Mrs. Patterson ate and whether Mr. Kessel's oxygen concentrator is working. She earns $13.50 an hour, classified as an independent contractor, no benefits, pays her own Social Security tax. She takes home $1,600 a month. She supports a six-year-old son who draws pictures of them both smiling with all their teeth -- though she is missing a lower left molar she cannot afford to replace. The story follows one day: six clients, the drive between them, and the moment at 7 PM when she picks up her son, makes dinner, and puts his drawing on the refrigerator with a magnet.

## Source Inspiration
The portrait of Crystal Dawson in "Democratic Portraits" -- a home health aide who is "the margin between those people and catastrophe" while "the margin is not sturdy." The essay describes her as the sole infrastructure of care for eleven vulnerable people, earning subsistence wages with no safety net. Her missing tooth is the essay's most precise image of healthcare's exclusions -- a visible gap that marks her as someone the system is supposed to serve but instead employs at below-survival wages. Her son Levi has "unusual gentleness and humor." Her father worked the steel mill that closed, then trucking, now disabled. Her mother works at Walmart.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Denis Johnson)**: Johnson's raw, hallucinatory prose captures the dreamlike quality of exhaustion -- six clients blur into each other, the roads wind, the hills are beautiful and she does not notice because she is calculating drive time. His spiritual yearning amid degradation matches her combination of care and despair. The fractured vignette structure of home visits mirrors Jesus' Son's hospital scenes.
- **AuthorB (Elizabeth Strout)**: Strout's deep empathy for small-town New England characters transplanted to Appalachia -- the same quality of seeing people whole without flinching. Strout's Olive Kitteridge voice: sharp, unsentimental, capable of great tenderness expressed through irritation. The home health aide should have that same quality of love expressed as competence.
- **WorkX (Jesus' Son by Denis Johnson)**: Structural DNA of vignettes that accumulate into devastating argument. Each client visit is a complete scene -- bathroom, medication, wound dressing, conversation -- that alone seems manageable but together reveals a system where one underpaid woman is the only thing standing between eleven people and institutional abandonment.
- **WorkY (Olive Kitteridge by Elizabeth Strout)**: Thematic DNA of a community visible only through one person's daily rounds. Olive sees Crosby, Maine, through her errands and encounters; the home health aide sees Wheeling, West Virginia, through her client list. The novel's structure of linked stories through a single consciousness applies to the linked home visits through a single day.

## Notes
The "staggering beauty" of the West Virginia hills should appear exactly once, in a sentence the aide does not finish because she is mentally calculating whether she has enough gas to reach Client #5 before the tank light comes on. The son's drawing -- both of them smiling with all their teeth -- should be the last image. The missing molar should appear three times: once when she catches it in the rearview mirror, once when a client offers her something to eat, once in the drawing where it is not missing. Do not explain the drawing. Let it sit on the refrigerator.
