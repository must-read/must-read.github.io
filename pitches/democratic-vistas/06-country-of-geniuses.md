## Premise
An AI safety researcher at a frontier lab has mapped five catastrophic risk pathways for the system her team is building: autonomy misalignment, bioweapon synthesis, totalitarian misuse, economic collapse, and unknowns. She has published the risks. She continues building. The story takes place over seventy-two hours as her team approaches a capability threshold that cannot be undone -- what her CEO has publicly described as "a country of geniuses in a datacenter." She is not a whistleblower and not a true believer. She is something worse: a person who sees clearly and proceeds anyway, because the alternative -- another lab, less careful, gets there first -- is worse. The story is told through her sleep-deprived attempts to write a letter to her daughter explaining why.

## Source Inspiration
The essay describes Dario Amodei's warning about artificial superintelligence arriving within one to two years and his framework of five existential risks: autonomy, misuse-for-destruction, misuse-for-power, economic disruption, and unknowns. The essay's central metaphor: "building the house anyway despite mapping five catastrophic risks." The author acknowledges the architect "warns while continuing to build" and calls this the posture of someone "choosing between two forms of apocalypse." The poem describes the moment "competence became fluency became something resembling thought."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Patricia Highsmith)**: Highsmith's cold, clinical psychological precision and her ability to render moral compromise without judgment. The safety researcher should have Ripley's quality -- not evil, but capable of sustained self-deception that looks, from the outside, like clarity. Highsmith's unsettling intimacy with compromised protagonists.
- **AuthorB (Ted Chiang)**: Chiang's ability to make abstract philosophical premises emotionally real. The five risk pathways should feel as concrete as the linguistics in "Story of Your Life" or the thermodynamics in "Exhalation." His crystalline precision makes the researcher's moral calculus legible without simplifying it.
- **WorkX (Oppenheimer)**: Structural DNA of the brilliant builder who sees the consequences and proceeds -- the "now I am become death" moment distributed across seventy-two hours rather than compressed into a single flash. The parallel structure of testimony and creation.
- **WorkY (Frankenstein by Mary Shelley)**: Thematic DNA of the creator's responsibility for what is created. The letter-to-daughter frame echoes Walton's letters to his sister in Shelley's novel -- a frame narrative where the person closest to the creation tries to explain it to someone who was not there.

## Notes
The letter to the daughter should be the spine, but the researcher keeps failing to finish it -- she writes a paragraph, deletes it, writes another. The seventy-two hours should include at least one scene where the system does something unexpected and beautiful, not just terrifying. The researcher's love for what she is building must be genuine. The horror is not that she is building something dangerous. The horror is that she is right to build it and also right to be afraid.
