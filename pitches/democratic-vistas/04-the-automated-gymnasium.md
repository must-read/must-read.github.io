## Premise
In the near future, a high school wrestling coach in a small New Mexico town watches his program become obsolete -- not because students stop wanting to wrestle, but because an AI physical training system produces better athletes in less time with no risk of injury. The school board votes to replace all athletics with the system. The coach, who has spent twenty-two years teaching students that "the point is not the match, the point is what the match makes of you," opens an unsanctioned after-school wrestling club in his grandfather's garage. Six students show up. The story follows one season where they train badly, lose every unofficial meet, and discover something the optimized system cannot produce: the knowledge of what it feels like to be beaten by another person and choose to stand up.

## Source Inspiration
"The Gymnast's Text" poses the central question: "What is the gymnast's struggle when the gymnasium has been automated?" The work argues that democratic character is formed through resistance and effort -- that "the lifter needs to be made, not the weight lifted." Section XII reframes exercise as self-formation, not results-production: efficiency makes struggle "obsolete" only if outcome alone matters. The portrait of James Trujillo -- a wrestling coach in Las Vegas, New Mexico, who teaches that wrestling is "you and the other person" -- provides the human anchor.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Ted Chiang)**: Chiang's crystalline thought-experiment structure is ideal for a premise that asks: what is lost when we optimize away struggle? His ability to make philosophical questions emotionally devastating -- to find the human cost of an idea -- matches the story's need to feel the coach's loss concretely.
- **AuthorB (Kazuo Ishiguro)**: Ishiguro's quiet, understated narration and the devastation of characters who participate in systems that are replacing them. The coach should have Ishiguro's quality of almost-understanding -- he knows what is being lost but cannot quite articulate it in the language the school board speaks.
- **WorkX (Flowers for Algernon by Daniel Keyes)**: Structural DNA of enhancement revealing what was valuable about limitation. Charlie Gordon's intelligence gains show him what his previous self could not see; the coach's obsolescence shows the school what wrestling was actually teaching -- and, like Charlie, neither the coach nor the institution can go back.
- **WorkY (Never Let Me Go by Kazuo Ishiguro)**: Thematic DNA of people who serve a system that will discard them, discussed in polite, circumscribed terms. The school board meeting should feel like Hailsham -- reasonable adults making reasonable decisions that happen to eliminate something essential about being human.

## Notes
The six students should not be misfits or rebels. They should be ordinary kids who cannot explain why they prefer the garage to the AI system. The losses at unofficial meets should be real losses, not plucky-underdog victories. The season should end without vindication. What the students gain is private, inarticulable, and will not show up on any metric the school board measures.
