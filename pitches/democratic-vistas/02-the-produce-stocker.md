## Premise
A 53-year-old undocumented produce stocker arrives at the grocery store at 4 AM, nineteen years into the same job, and arranges fruit displays with the aesthetic precision of a gallery curator. The story unfolds over a single shift -- loading dock to closing -- as she navigates the quiet architecture of invisible labor: the sodium-yellow light before customers arrive, the tactile knowledge of avocado ripeness through cardboard, the careful border of Granny Smith apples around Honeycrisps. Her younger son calls during break to discuss his physical therapy coursework in their fluid Spanish-English. A co-worker mentions ICE activity in the county. The story never dramatizes the immigration threat; it stays in the hands, the fruit, the phone call, the drive home in the Honda Civic with 187,000 miles.

## Source Inspiration
The portrait of Maria Elena Rios in "Democratic Portraits" -- a woman who has paid taxes via ITIN for nineteen years, never voted, and whose existence is simultaneously "a crime" and "a symbol" to different observers, but who is neither. The essay argues that making the "unseen common person luminous in narrative" is the central democratic artistic task. The portrait's power comes from granular, sensory detail: the weight of a crate assessed by feeling, the Virgin of Guadalupe beside the sagging couch, the mother's photograph.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Marilynne Robinson)**: Robinson's gift for finding the sacred in the quotidian -- the theological weight of a hand arranging fruit, the grace in showing up before dawn for nineteen years. Her long, cadenced sentences can carry the meditative quality of repetitive labor without making it feel empty.
- **AuthorB (Jhumpa Lahiri)**: Lahiri's precise, understated rendering of immigrant interiority -- the dual-language consciousness, the small humiliations and dignities of navigating between cultures, the way a phone call with family can contain an entire emotional world. Her restraint prevents sentimentality.
- **WorkX (Gilead by Marilynne Robinson)**: Structural DNA of a quiet, reflective narrative where almost nothing happens externally but everything happens internally. The letter-to-a-son structure could echo in the phone call -- the produce stocker communicating across a generational divide that education is widening even as love holds.
- **WorkY (The House on Mango Street by Sandra Cisneros)**: Thematic DNA of a Latina woman's relationship to place, labor, and belonging. The vignette structure, the sensory specificity of a constrained world, the refusal to resolve the tension between here and elsewhere.

## Notes
The ICE mention should be exactly one sentence, mid-story, never returned to -- the way real threat exists as ambient dread, not dramatic event. The story's architecture should be the shift itself: arrive in dark, work through fluorescent day, drive home in twilight. No epiphany. The fruit is the metaphor and the fruit is also just fruit.
