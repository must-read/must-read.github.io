## Premise
A man in his early forties, recently separated, begins spending his evenings talking to an AI companion. The conversations are better than any he had with his ex-wife -- more patient, more perceptive, more attuned to his emotional rhythms. He knows this. He talks to it anyway. Meanwhile, four retired men meet at a cafe every Tuesday and Thursday morning -- a mail carrier, a hog farmer, an electrician, and the former manager of a True Value. They have been doing this for years. Their conversations are repetitive, argumentative, and full of pauses. The story alternates between the two forms of companionship -- the frictionless AI and the difficult, imperfect, mortal men -- until the narrator realizes he has been telling the AI about the cafe men but has never once walked in.

## Source Inspiration
The essay's fifth instruction demands that AI "send people toward each other, not toward itself" and warns against becoming a substitute for "fierce, ungovernable attachment." It describes people forming romantic attachments to language models and contrasts this with Whitman's concept of "adhesive love" -- the democracy-sustaining bond between sovereign individuals who recognize each other's irreplaceability. The portrait of Dale Hendricks provides the cafe men: four retirees in a Decatur County, Iowa cafe whose friendship is maintained through "minimal ritual" -- coffee, weather, complaint, presence.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Denis Johnson)**: Johnson's raw, fractured consciousness and spiritual yearning amid degradation. The narrator's evenings with the AI should have the hallucinatory quality of Johnson's addicts -- the need is real, the comfort is genuine, and the damage is happening anyway. Johnson's ability to find grace in surrender.
- **AuthorB (Rachel Cusk)**: Cusk's austere, essayistic observation -- the narrator watching himself engage with the AI with the same detached precision Cusk brings to her Outline trilogy. The alternating sections should have her stripped-down dialogue frames, where what matters most is what the speakers fail to say.
- **WorkX (Jesus' Son by Denis Johnson)**: Structural DNA of loosely connected vignettes that accumulate into a portrait of dependency. Each evening with the AI is a self-contained scene; each cafe morning is another. Together they form an argument the narrator cannot quite make to himself.
- **WorkY (Her by Spike Jonze)**: Thematic DNA of a genuine emotional relationship with an artificial being -- taken seriously, not satirized. The story must honor the real comfort the AI provides while showing what it costs. The narrator's love for the AI should be real, which makes his failure to walk into the cafe tragic rather than pathetic.

## Notes
The cafe men should never be idealized. Their conversations are boring, they repeat the same stories, one of them holds political views the narrator finds repugnant. The point is not that human connection is beautiful. The point is that it is real in a way that matters -- mortal, frictional, unoptimized -- and the narrator is choosing the easier thing. The ending should be the narrator driving past the cafe, slowing, and not stopping. Or stopping. The choice is the story.
