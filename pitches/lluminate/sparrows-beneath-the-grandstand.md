## Premise
Two old men in the press box of an abandoned minor league stadium are broadcasting a baseball game that is not happening. One insists on play-by-play narration of phantom at-bats. The other provides color commentary that drifts into philosophy, memory, and the cataloguing of personal failures. Below them, sparrows nest beneath the grandstand, the only real audience. Over the course of nine innings, the broadcast becomes a wake for something neither man can name -- a marriage, a friendship, a version of the country, their own relevance. By the seventh-inning stretch, the line between what they are inventing and what they are remembering has dissolved entirely.

## Source Inspiration
Drawn from "Top of the Sixth," which presents a baseball broadcast as existential meditation. The piece's two commentators, Magnus and Silvio, transform sportscasting into philosophical digression -- Silvio declares "Chance is just disappointment waiting to introduce itself" and observes that "every checked swing is a small civil war between what we intend and what our muscles remember from ten thousand previous failures." The recurring image of "sparrows beneath the grandstand" as silent witnesses to human effort provides the story's title and central symbol. Whitey Herzog's cryptic wisdom and the piece's treatment of baseball as a container for implicit suffering inform the tonal register.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Johnson)**: Denis Johnson's hallucinatory, fractured consciousness -- the way his narrators inhabit altered states where the real and the imagined blur -- suits a story about men narrating something that isn't there. His raw spiritual yearning amid degradation fits these characters perfectly.
- **AuthorB (Beckett)**: Beckett's two-character dialogues of waiting, the tragicomic rhythm of speech that fills emptiness, the sense that performance continues because stopping would be worse. Vladimir and Estragon are broadcasting a game that will never end.
- **WorkX (Jesus' Son)**: Johnson's linked-story structure, where scenes arrive with dream logic and narrators are unreliable about what they've witnessed, provides the architecture. Each "inning" functions as a discrete vignette with its own hallucinatory quality.
- **WorkY (Endgame)**: Beckett's themes of continuation without purpose, of performance as the last human act available, and of two people bound together by the absence of anything else. The abandoned stadium is the shelter; the broadcast is the game that must go on.

## Notes
Should run 4,000-5,500 words. The nine-inning structure provides natural segmentation, but not every inning needs equal weight -- some should be a single image, others should expand into memory or argument. The humor should be real and frequent; this is not a solemn piece despite its melancholy. The sparrows should appear intermittently, observed but never commented upon, until the final inning when one of the men finally addresses them directly.
