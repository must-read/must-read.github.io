## Premise
A retired university archivist discovers that three unrelated scholars -- a political philosopher, an anthropologist, and an industrial engineer -- all named Strauss, submitted nearly identical papers to three different journals in 1952, each arguing that structural concealment is the foundation of civilization. She becomes obsessed with proving they are the same person, even as the evidence increasingly suggests they cannot be. The deeper she digs, the more her own scholarly apparatus begins to resemble the esoteric concealment she is studying.

## Source Inspiration
Drawn from "The Pockets of the Cooked," which deliberately conflates Leo Strauss, Levi Strauss, and Claude Levi-Strauss into "one man who somehow studied the Bororo, reinforced miners' trousers, and decoded Plato's esoteric teachings." The essay's core metaphor -- "The pocket is the esoteric space within the exoteric garment" -- and its argument that "complete transparency destroys the thing it illuminates" provide the philosophical engine. The essay's self-undermining conclusion ("I have not said what I came to say, because what I came to say cannot be said") becomes the story's structural principle.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Borges)**: The labyrinthine scholarly detective story is Borges's native territory. His ability to make intellectual obsession feel like a thriller, and his love of fictional bibliographies and apocryphal scholars, fits this premise exactly.
- **AuthorB (DeLillo)**: DeLillo's paranoid, system-obsessed prose -- the sense that hidden structures govern everything -- creates productive tension with Borges's playfulness. DeLillo would ground the academic conspiracy in institutional texture and contemporary unease.
- **WorkX (Pale Fire)**: Nabokov's structure -- a scholarly commentary that may or may not be the delusion of its narrator -- provides the ideal architecture. The archivist's annotations and footnotes gradually reveal more about her than about her subject.
- **WorkY (The Name of the Rose)**: Eco's themes of hidden knowledge, the danger of interpretation, and the question of whether the pursuit of concealed truth is noble or destructive resonate throughout.

## Notes
This should run 5,000-7,000 words, giving space for the nested scholarly apparatus to develop. The tone should balance intellectual delight with creeping dread -- the archivist's discovery that her own work enacts the same concealment she is analyzing. The ending should resist resolution: is the convergence real or is the archivist projecting pattern onto noise? The reader should not be able to decide.
