## Premise
A Nigerian-American woman who works as a leadership consultant -- teaching Fortune 500 executives how to "lead through uncertainty" -- returns to her mother's neighborhood in Houston for a funeral. Over three days, she watches her mother navigate a community in crisis: an immigration raid has scattered families, children need rides to school, groceries need distributing to households afraid to leave their homes. Her mother does not lead through uncertainty. Her mother weighs tomatoes at the market and adds an unblemished one to a neighbor's bag without comment. The consultant's professional vocabulary -- "resilience frameworks," "adaptive capacity," "stakeholder alignment" -- begins to sound hollow against her mother's wordless, specific acts of care. The story culminates not in revelation but in the consultant sitting at her mother's kitchen table, unable to write the keynote she owes on Monday.

## Source Inspiration
Drawn from two sources. "Organized Bewilderment" closes with the image of "a woman at a market weighing tomatoes, a small transaction containing dignity -- adding an unblemished tomato without comment. This everyday grace suggests that meaning persists in small human interactions despite larger systemic failures." The essay's exploration of leadership through Kermit the Frog (conducting chaos without controlling it) versus Bernie Sanders (unwavering insistence on core principles) frames the consultant's professional worldview. "Compound Interest" documents Minnesota's mutual aid response to immigration enforcement -- "volunteer school runs for children of detained families, 'diaper fairies' distributing 200,000+ diapers, laundry services for sheltering neighbors" -- and argues that "care work that does not scale efficiently represents genuine resistance."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (O'Connor)**: O'Connor's gift for showing how grace arrives through violence, disruption, and the collapse of pretension. The consultant's professional identity is her peacock tail -- beautiful, elaborate, and exactly what prevents her from seeing. O'Connor's darkly comic sensibility should govern the tone of the consultant's encounters with her mother's world.
- **AuthorB (Adichie)**: Adichie's precise rendering of cultural duality -- the Nigerian-American experience of code-switching, of the gap between professional persona and family self, of being simultaneously insider and outsider. The warmth and specificity of Adichie's domestic scenes should anchor the mother's world.
- **WorkX (A Good Man Is Hard to Find)**: O'Connor's structure -- a journey toward an encounter that strips away every self-protective story the protagonist tells herself. The three-day visit compresses the grandmother's road trip; the "misfit" here is the consultant's own irrelevance in a crisis that requires hands, not frameworks.
- **WorkY (Americanah)**: Adichie's themes of return -- the experience of coming home and discovering you have become foreign to your own origins. The consultant's professional success has made her fluent in a language her mother's community does not speak and does not need.

## Notes
Should run 4,000-6,000 words. The mother should never criticize the consultant's work -- that would be too easy. She simply does not engage with it. The consultant's crisis should not resolve into self-flagellation or dramatic career change; it should sit as a quiet discomfort, the recognition that her expertise is real and useful and also completely inadequate to a woman adding a tomato to a bag. The community's mutual aid should be shown through specific acts, not described in aggregate. The keynote she cannot write should be titled something like "Leading Through Disruption: Resilience in the Age of Uncertainty."
