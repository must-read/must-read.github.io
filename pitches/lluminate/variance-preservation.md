## Premise
In a near-future city governed by algorithmic resource allocation, a statistician named Freya works for the Bureau of Predictive Welfare. Her job is building models that anticipate citizens' needs before they arise -- food, housing, medical care -- all distributed with mathematical precision. The system works. Everyone is fed, sheltered, healthy. But Freya has noticed something: as the models improve, the gap between prediction and behavior shrinks. People are becoming their data. She begins secretly embedding "variance preservation" code into her models -- tiny pockets of unpredictability designed to protect the space where people deviate from their profiles. When a colleague's behavior triggers an anomaly flag, Freya must decide how far her quiet sabotage extends.

## Source Inspiration
Drawn directly from the character of Freya in "Dead Letters," who "exposes the mathematical sleight-of-hand undergirding the system" and "secretly develops 'variance preservation' models: algorithms designed to maintain uncertainty and protect deviation from prediction." Her insight that "eliminating error -- the variance between prediction and reality -- effectively eliminates human freedom itself" is the story's philosophical core. Also informed by "Compound Interest," which documents algorithm design that "weights outrage reactions five times higher than neutral content, converting human despair into engagement metrics."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Ishiguro)**: Ishiguro's gift for depicting characters who live inside oppressive systems without fully recognizing them -- the quiet horror of "Never Let Me Go" -- is exactly right. Freya should not be a rebel; she should be someone who loves her work and only slowly understands what it does.
- **AuthorB (Saunders)**: Saunders's ability to render corporate-dystopian language with both satirical precision and genuine empathy. The Bureau's internal memos, performance reviews, and wellness metrics should be darkly funny and deeply sad simultaneously.
- **WorkX (Never Let Me Go)**: The structure of gradual revelation -- a narrator who knows more than she initially shares, whose calm tone masks the horror of her situation. The story should unfold through Freya's professional observations, each one adding a layer of dread.
- **WorkY (Bartleby, the Scrivener)**: Melville's themes of quiet refusal within institutional machinery, and the question of what it means to "prefer not to" when the system requires participation. Freya's variance preservation is a mathematical "I would prefer not to."

## Notes
Should run 5,000-7,000 words. The world should feel benevolent, not oppressive -- that is the horror. The system genuinely helps people. Freya's sabotage may actually cause harm. The story should refuse to resolve whether she is right to introduce unpredictability into a system that feeds and shelters people. The question "what mathematical symbol represents a person who refuses to be averaged?" should haunt but never appear explicitly.
