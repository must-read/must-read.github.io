## Premise
A dream has been spreading through a hospital for four months. The original patient -- a young woman who simply fell asleep -- now has thirty-seven staff members and twelve visitors trapped in synchronized sleep alongside her. Anyone who gets close enough to attempt intervention becomes part of the dream. A specialist is sent in wearing protective equipment rated for one hour. What she finds is not a pathology but a seduction: the dream offers peace, a house, a family, everything the waking world cannot. With forty-seven minutes of protection remaining, she must understand why everyone chose to stay -- and confront the possibility that staying is the rational choice. The story is told from inside the protective equipment, the dream pressing against it, offering warmth and resolution to every unresolved grief in the specialist's life.

## Source Inspiration
World 126 of "Change Without Notice" presents a contagious dream quarantine: "The dream has been active for four months. It started with one patient and now affects thirty-seven staff members and twelve visitors." The specialist's protective equipment has a one-hour limit, the dream shows "a house, a garden, a family that welcomed him," and the devastating final question -- "Maybe we let them sleep. Maybe we ask why they want to stay" -- reframes the horror. The dream is not a trap. It is an offer. That is what makes it terrifying.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Kelly Link (style)**: Link's ability to write the uncanny in domestic register -- the dream should feel like a real house, a real family, comfort that is precisely wrong in ways the protagonist cannot articulate. Her surrealism embedded in the mundane.
- **Haruki Murakami (style)**: Murakami's dreamlike narratives where the boundary between reality and unreality dissolves without announcement. His protagonists who enter strange spaces and find them hauntingly familiar.
- **Annihilation (structure)**: The structural DNA of a team entering a contaminated zone with protective measures that gradually fail, told through scientific observation that becomes increasingly subjective. The specialist's rational documentation breaking down.
- **The Vegetarian (themes)**: The theme of surrender to a transformation the world insists is pathological but the transformed experience as liberation. The sleepers may not be victims. They may have made a choice the waking world cannot accept.

## Notes
The protective equipment timer should structure the narrative -- forty-seven minutes counting down, each minute a section. The specialist's clinical observations should be interrupted by increasingly personal intrusions from the dream: a voice that sounds like her dead mother, a garden that looks like the yard she grew up in. The ending should leave ambiguous whether she removes the equipment.
