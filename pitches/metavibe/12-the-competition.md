## Premise
A man has been winning a competition for three days without sleep. He does not know the rules. He does not know the prize. He only knows that the remaining competitors are not trying to win -- they are trying to navigate some optimal middle ground, losing badly enough to be eliminated but not so badly as to be noticed. He has already passed this zone. His lead is insurmountable. The moderator tells him: "Winning has consequences." The prize is revealed: perpetual responsibility for running the next competition. Victory is a sentence. The story follows the final round, told from inside the frontrunner's growing comprehension that every system designed to produce a winner is actually designed to produce the next administrator of suffering.

## Source Inspiration
World 127 of "Change Without Notice": a competition where "the rules favored him -- whatever the rules were, because no one had explained them clearly," where losing "is not punished" but "winning has consequences," and where the prize is "responsibility for the next competition, a perpetual obligation to compete until someone else won." The other competitors are navigating an "optimal middle ground" the protagonist has already passed. The story expands the vignette into a full examination of the trap.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Kobo Abe (style)**: Abe's nightmarish logic -- systems that operate by rules the protagonist cannot access, environments that constrain without explanation, the individual trapped inside structures that seem designed specifically to process them. His prose of bewildered compliance.
- **Shirley Jackson (style)**: Jackson's "The Lottery" DNA -- communal rituals whose true purpose is hidden from participants, violence administered through procedure, the moment of comprehension arriving too late.
- **The Hunger Games (structure)**: The structural frame of a competition that is actually a mechanism of social control, where winning is the worst outcome and the spectacle serves a purpose invisible to the competitors.
- **No Exit (themes)**: Sartre's theme of hell as other people -- but here, hell as perpetual administration. The prize is not death or torture but responsibility. The winner's punishment is to become the system. "L'enfer, c'est les autres" becomes "l'enfer, c'est la bureaucratie."

## Notes
The other competitors should be more interesting than the protagonist -- they have figured out the game's real rules and are playing accordingly. The frontrunner's confusion should be sympathetic but also infuriating: he won because he did not understand, and his ignorance is not innocence. The final round should be described in enough detail that the reader can see the trap closing, even as the protagonist cannot.
