## Premise
A woman builds a personal reading application -- an RSS reader with text-to-speech -- as a project to manage her accumulating browser tabs, each one an article she saved and never read. The app works. The tabs empty. But as the text-to-speech system reads the articles aloud during her commute, she realizes the tabs were not random: viewed chronologically, they form a diary she did not know she was writing. Articles about divorce law saved the week her husband moved out. Articles about single parenting saved the month after. Articles about grief, then articles about dating, then articles about being alone, then articles about learning to cook for one. She never read them -- she just saved them, the way you press a bruise, not to heal it but to confirm it is still there. The story is told in fragments: the articles' headlines, the dates they were saved, the text-to-speech voice reading them aloud in a car, and the silence in between.

## Source Inspiration
The reading library book describes "a particular kind of guilt" from accumulating unread tabs, the insight that "most potential reading time isn't actually screen-based" and ears are free during commutes, and the app's dual storage of HTML for display and plain text for TTS. The book's philosophical frame -- "building tools for yourself as a clarifying constraint" -- becomes literal: the woman builds a tool for herself and the tool reveals herself to herself.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Jenny Offill (style)**: Offill's fragmented, aphoristic prose -- short sections, white space between them, the architecture of a mind assembling itself from pieces. Her "novel in fragments" form mirrors the browser tabs as diary.
- **Denis Johnson (style)**: Johnson's raw, hallucinatory clarity in moments of personal collapse -- the car, the commute, the TTS voice reading articles about divorce law while the woman drives. His spiritual yearning amid degradation.
- **Dept. of Speculation (structure)**: Offill's structure of a marriage dissolving told through fragments, observations, and found text. The browser tabs function as the found text -- the protagonist's unconscious curating a narrative her conscious mind refused to write.
- **A Visit from the Goon Squad (themes)**: Egan's theme of time as the thing that transforms us without our consent, experienced through the technology that was supposed to organize our lives but instead records our dissolution. The app as time machine.

## Notes
The text-to-speech voice should become a character -- neutral, algorithmic, reading the most devastating content in the same tone it reads recipes and tech articles. The fragments should be short (some a single sentence: the headline, the date, the silence). The ending should be the woman adding a new tab -- the first one she has saved since the app emptied her queue -- and the reader should understand what it means without being told.
