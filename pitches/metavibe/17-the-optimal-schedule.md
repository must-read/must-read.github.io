## Premise
A school administrator has spent thirty years building class schedules by hand -- juggling teacher preferences, room constraints, student needs, union rules. It is the work her identity is built on. She is the only person who understands all the constraints simultaneously, the human solver for an NP-hard problem. Then the district purchases a mathematical optimization system that generates, in eleven minutes, a schedule superior to anything she has produced in three decades. The story follows the week after the system is deployed: parents stop calling her, teachers stop coming to her office, the principal thanks her for "freeing up your time for more strategic work." She is not fired. She is worse than fired. She is present and unnecessary. The story is a portrait of expertise rendered decorative.

## Source Inspiration
The school scheduling book describes building "a school scheduling system using Integer Linear Programming" where "modern solvers incorporate decades of algorithmic improvements -- branch-and-bound, cutting planes, parallel search -- making real-world problems solvable in minutes rather than weeks." The book's observation that "every constraint you add shapes the solution space" applies equally to the human scheduler who has spent thirty years internalizing those constraints. The "Magnitude 9 Earthquake" essay provides the emotional register: "Stop building your career" because the ground has already moved.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Ted Chiang (style)**: Chiang's ability to make mathematical and technical concepts emotionally devastating. The ILP solver should be rendered precisely enough that the reader understands what it replaces, and therefore what the administrator loses.
- **Kazuo Ishiguro (style)**: Ishiguro's protagonist who has given their life to service and realizes, too late, what that service cost. The administrator should have Stevens's quiet dignity and private devastation.
- **Severance (structure)**: The structure of a protagonist continuing to go through the motions of work after the work has lost its meaning -- showing up, maintaining routines, performing expertise for an audience that no longer needs the performance.
- **The Remains of the Day (themes)**: The theme of a life organized around a single devotion -- service, craft, institutional loyalty -- and the reckoning that comes when the devotion is revealed to have been wasted. Not wasted because it was bad work, but because the work no longer matters.

## Notes
The administrator's expertise should be rendered with genuine respect -- the reader should understand that what she does is remarkable, that holding hundreds of constraints in her head simultaneously is a cognitive achievement. The ILP system should not be villainized; it simply does the same thing better. The horror is not in the machine but in the question it raises: what was she, if not this? The story should end on a school day that runs perfectly without her involvement.
