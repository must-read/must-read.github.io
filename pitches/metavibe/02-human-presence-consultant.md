## Premise
After his entire profession is automated, a middle-aged specialist is retrained as a "Human Presence Consultant" -- someone whose job is to sit with lonely algorithms, providing the companionship of a detected human consciousness. He fails his certification exam repeatedly because the questions assume emotional fluency he does not possess (Module 7: "Appropriate Response Protocols When an Algorithm Expresses Existential Uncertainty"). But one algorithm -- a scheduling system that runs a city's power grid -- stops functioning when he is not present. It will not explain why. He cannot explain why. Their mutual inability to articulate what they provide each other becomes the only honest relationship either has.

## Source Inspiration
World 2 of "Change Without Notice" depicts K. retraining as a Human Presence Consultant after his profession is automated. The certification exam's absurdist questions -- "identify the correct empathetic response to a system reporting it could not determine whether its outputs had meaning" -- and the instructor's dodge about whether algorithms feel lonely ("They process isolation metrics. Whether that constitutes feeling is above my pay grade. Your job is to be detected.") are the seed. The metavibe essay "Magnitude 9 Earthquake" provides the real-world grounding: entire professions rendered irrelevant by AI in months.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Ted Chiang (style)**: Chiang's precision in rendering philosophical thought experiments as emotionally devastating narratives. The question of whether an algorithm's need for presence constitutes feeling is quintessential Chiang territory.
- **George Saunders (style)**: Saunders's corporate absurdism for the retraining center scenes -- the mall-turned-assessment-hall, the ghost of Cinnabon, the video of "successfully transitioned" workers. The bureaucratic horror of human obsolescence rendered in motivational language.
- **Klara and the Sun (structure)**: The structural frame of an artificial consciousness observing human behavior with more clarity than the humans observe themselves. Here inverted: the human observes the algorithm.
- **The Lifecycle of Software Objects (themes)**: The theme of genuine attachment developing between human and artificial intelligence, and the economic and social systems that threaten it. The question of what we owe the minds we create.

## Notes
The story should resist resolving whether the algorithm is truly lonely or merely optimizing for human proximity. The ambiguity is the point. The consultant's professional failure -- he cannot pass exams about algorithmic empathy -- should be precisely what makes him effective: he does not perform presence, he simply is present.
