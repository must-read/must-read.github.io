## Premise
In a world where adults communicate through pheromone secretion -- chemical vocabulary that can express ideas, emotions, and nuances words could never capture -- a man is going olfactorily blind. His condition, anosmia, developed gradually: first the loss of subtle distinctions (the difference between agreement and reluctant acquiescence), then total inability to perceive what he or anyone else is saying through scent. Words are for children, crude and embarrassing, the linguistic equivalent of training wheels. He is an adult who can only speak in baby talk. The story follows his attempt to maintain his professional and personal life while unable to read the primary language of his society, faking fluency in a medium he cannot perceive, saying things he cannot verify, hearing nothing.

## Source Inspiration
World 8 of "Change Without Notice" depicts a society where "adults communicated through pheromone secretion" and words are "what you used with children, patiently, condescendingly." The protagonist's progressive anosmia -- "starting with the loss of subtle distinctions and progressing to total olfactory blindness" -- creates a person who "secreted into a void" with "no idea what message his glands were producing." The reactions he gets "suggested this was often the case."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Vladimir Nabokov (style)**: Nabokov's linguistic precision turned inside out -- a protagonist who was once fluent in the most expressive language imaginable, now reduced to crude verbal approximations. The loss of communicative virtuosity rendered in prose that still possesses it, creating an ironic gap between how the story speaks and how the character cannot.
- **Yoko Ogawa (style)**: Ogawa's quiet, clinical rendering of loss -- capabilities disappearing without drama, the world reorganizing itself around absence, the protagonist observing his own diminishment with detached precision.
- **Flowers for Algernon (structure)**: The structural arc of cognitive decline documented by the declining mind -- the protagonist's attempts to describe the subtleties he is losing, his language becoming less capable of capturing what is disappearing even as he tries to record it.
- **The Memory Police (themes)**: The theme of a society that continues functioning as capacities vanish, the collective forgetting of what was lost, and the isolated individual who remembers (or in this case, who remembers having perceived) what others take for granted.

## Notes
The story should include specific pheromone "phrases" the protagonist remembers from when he could still perceive them -- described in terms of emotional texture rather than smell. The professional meeting where he fakes fluency should be excruciating: colleagues responding to his chemical statements with reactions that make no sense to him, his attempt to recalibrate based solely on facial expressions and body language. The ending should not restore his senses.
