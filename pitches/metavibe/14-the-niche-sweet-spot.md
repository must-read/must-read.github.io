## Premise
A librarian in a near-future archive discovers that AI-generated how-to books have achieved a strange perfection: each one covers a topic so specific that only a handful of people in the world need it, but for those people, the book is indispensable. "How to Tune a 1987 Yamaha DX7 for Microtonal Kurdish Music." "How to Repair Prussian-Era Clockwork Using Only Post-Soviet Components." The library grows by thousands of volumes daily. The librarian's job is to catalog them, but she begins to notice that the books are converging -- each hyperspecific volume connects to others through hidden threads, and the total collection, if read in the right order, describes something no single volume contains. She becomes convinced the archive is not a library but a single text that has been shattered into a million specific pieces, and she is the only reader trying to reassemble it.

## Source Inspiration
The metavibe platform's stated mission: "Transform specific coding sessions into niche how-to content. Specific work creates general knowledge." The site targets "the niche sweet spot between overly broad and overly specific technical instruction." The four books -- survival horror games, healthcare platforms, reading libraries, school scheduling -- are each hyperspecific yet together describe a general methodology. This pattern, taken to its logical extreme, becomes the story's premise: what if hyperspecific knowledge generation continued until the specificity itself became a kind of language?

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Jorge Luis Borges (style)**: Borges's erudite, labyrinthine prose about infinite libraries, impossible books, and the architecture of knowledge. His ability to make bibliographic concepts feel like cosmic horror.
- **Italo Calvino (style)**: Calvino's playful, structurally inventive metafiction -- the reader as character, the book as mirror, the act of reading as the story's subject. His lightness applied to the weight of infinite knowledge.
- **If on a winter's night a traveler (structure)**: Calvino's structure of nested, interrupted narratives -- each hyperspecific book the librarian opens begins a story that connects to other books, but none complete themselves. The catalog becomes a novel that refuses to be one.
- **The Library of Babel (themes)**: Borges's theme of a library containing every possible book -- and therefore every possible meaning and every possible meaninglessness. The AI-generated archive as a Babel where every book is useful to someone but the totality is incomprehensible.

## Notes
The specific book titles should be vivid and plausible-absurd -- real enough that the reader can imagine needing them, specific enough to be funny, and accumulating toward the suggestion that the archive knows something about the world that no single reader can grasp. The librarian's growing conviction should never be confirmed or denied. The story should end mid-catalog.
