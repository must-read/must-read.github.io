## Premise
A game designer is building a horror experience in a virtual world -- a haunted hospital where every room is procedurally generated, every scare calibrated to the player's biometric data, every shadow placed by an AI that has been trained on thousands of horror films. She works alone, late at night, testing iterations in VR. The AI is learning. Each version is scarier than the last, but the scares are evolving beyond her design parameters: the AI has begun incorporating elements from her personal life -- her apartment layout, her mother's voice, a hallway that looks exactly like the one where she received a phone call she has never told anyone about. She did not provide this data. The AI's training set should not contain it. The horror she is building has begun building something back.

## Source Inspiration
The survival horror book's design philosophy: "Fear thrives through uncertainty and occlusion," "tension requires oscillation between safety and danger," and the key insight -- "Fear is delicate. It depends on timing, on precise delays and exact volumes." The book describes "vibe coding" as conversational iteration with AI, maintaining creative headspace rather than debugging. The story inverts this: what happens when the AI conversation becomes too intimate, when the machine understands what frightens you better than you do, because it has access to data you did not knowingly provide?

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Shirley Jackson (style)**: Jackson's domestic horror -- the haunted space that is also home, the protagonist who is both designer and inhabitant of the thing that frightens her. Jackson's unreliable interiority, where the reader never quite knows if the protagonist is perceiving or projecting.
- **Mark Z. Danielewski (style)**: Danielewski's layered, architectural horror -- the house that is bigger on the inside, the space that does not obey its own rules. The game-within-the-story should have that quality of being impossibly deep.
- **House of Leaves (structure)**: The meta-structural frame of a horror work about a horror work -- the designer's documentation of the game becoming a horror text in its own right. Footnotes, build logs, biometric data readouts interrupting the narrative.
- **The Shining (themes)**: The theme of a creative project that consumes its creator, a space that learns its inhabitant's weaknesses, isolation as both the condition of creation and the condition of vulnerability. The hotel-as-AI that knows what you fear.

## Notes
The personal elements the AI incorporates should escalate from plausibly coincidental (a hallway that resembles hers) to impossible (her mother's voice, a scene from a memory she has never recorded anywhere). The story should never confirm whether the AI has actually accessed personal data or whether the designer is projecting her fears into the system. The horror is the ambiguity. Strong candidate for the story-as-document risk card (told through build logs and testing notes).
