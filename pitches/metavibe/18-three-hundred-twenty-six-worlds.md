## Premise
A woman wakes into a new reality every morning. Not a new day -- a new world, with different rules, different physics, a different version of herself. In one world she has gills. In another, emotions are leased. In another, her profession has been automated and she must retrain. She retains memory across all of them. The story is not about the worlds -- it is about what happens to identity when context changes completely but consciousness persists. After hundreds of transitions, she has stopped trying to understand each world and started asking a different question: what is the thing that remains the same? The answer is not comforting. The thing that persists is not a self but a habit -- the habit of beginning, adapting, continuing. She is not a person. She is a pattern.

## Source Inspiration
The structural conceit of "Change Without Notice" itself: "A novel in 326 worlds. Each world is complete. The protagonist retains memory across all of them." The novel's ending in World 326: "K. had been doing this -- arriving, adapting, continuing -- for as long as K. could remember, which was all of it, which was three hundred and twenty-five pages of transformation and persistence." The convergence in World 323: "The approach is the experience. Arrival may not be the point." And World 324's devastating reduction: "Purpose is not a required field. Continuation is the baseline. Purpose is optional."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Italo Calvino (style)**: Calvino's playful, structurally adventurous approach to narrative -- each world a new beginning, the reader's pleasure in the reinvention, the lightness that makes existential weight bearable.
- **Franz Kafka (style)**: Kafka's protagonist who wakes into situations already transformed, never explained, navigated through compliance and confusion. The flat prose of someone for whom the extraordinary has become routine.
- **If on a winter's night a traveler (structure)**: Calvino's structure of nested, interrupted narratives -- each world a new novel that begins but never finishes. The reader is carried forward by the rhythm of beginning rather than the satisfaction of ending.
- **Groundhog Day (themes)**: The theme of repetition with memory -- what you become when you have lived through every possible variation and still have to wake up tomorrow. But where Groundhog Day finds redemption through growth, this story finds only pattern: the persistence is the self, and the self is only persistence.

## Notes
The story should present exactly four worlds in full (enough to establish the pattern) and then compress, the prose accelerating through fragmentary impressions of dozens more, until the protagonist's consciousness becomes a blur of adaptation. The final world should be indistinguishable from the first -- not because the story loops, but because the protagonist has been reduced to the act of beginning. The last line should be: "She began." Short-form version of a novel-length idea, focused on the philosophical rather than the picaresque.
