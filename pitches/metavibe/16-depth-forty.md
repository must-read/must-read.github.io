## Premise
In a world where humanity lives entirely underwater -- has always lived underwater, with gills instead of lungs, bioluminescent farms instead of sunlight, pressure gradients instead of altitude -- a man on the 40th depth begins having "surface dreams." He remembers breathing air. Not metaphorically: he remembers the physical sensation of inhaling deeply, filling lungs he no longer has, standing under an open sky with no ceiling. His doctor dismisses it as a common phenomenon, the desire to transcend limitations. But the memory is too specific, too detailed, too much like recollection rather than invention. He begins searching for evidence that the surface exists -- a ceiling above the ceiling, a world above the water -- and finds a community of other dreamers, each with their own fragmentary memories of a place that his entire civilization insists is myth.

## Source Inspiration
World 4 of "Change Without Notice": "The pressure outside would kill a mammal. K. knew this the way he knew his own name. The surface world was myth. Air-breathers were stories told to children, like dragons or democracy." The protagonist's gills, the bioluminescent farms, the 40th depth apartment -- and the haunting memory of air: "A place with no ceiling, no walls, just openness extending forever. Breathing in. The breath going on and on, expanding his chest until he might float away." The doctor's dismissal: "Surface dreams. Very common."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **China Mieville (style)**: Mieville's ability to build fully realized alien societies with their own politics, aesthetics, and contradictions. The underwater civilization should feel as textured and politically complex as Beszel/Ul Qoma, not just a backdrop for the protagonist's quest.
- **Ursula K. Le Guin (style)**: Le Guin's anthropological warmth -- the underwater society should be genuinely appealing, not obviously dystopian. The protagonist's desire for the surface should feel like a betrayal of something real, not an escape from something bad.
- **The City & the City (structure)**: The structure of two overlapping realities that citizens are trained not to perceive, and the violation of "unsee" that triggers investigation. The underwater world and the surface world occupying the same space, separated by trained disbelief.
- **20,000 Leagues Under the Sea (themes)**: The theme of the deep as a complete world sufficient unto itself, and the question of whether the desire to return to the surface is wisdom or failure of imagination.

## Notes
The community of surface dreamers should not be revolutionaries or seekers -- they should be ordinary people embarrassed by their condition, meeting in the equivalent of a support group. The surface should never be confirmed or denied. The story's tension is not "does it exist?" but "what does the longing for it mean?" The final image should be the protagonist putting his hand on his gills, feeling them open and close, water cycling through him, trying to remember what air felt like and failing.
