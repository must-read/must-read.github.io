## Premise
A government happiness auditor -- one of thousands who visit citizens quarterly to verify compliance with mandatory contentment metrics -- arrives at an apartment to assess a man who is three units short for the quarter. The audit is routine. But the man's responses are too honest: he admits that the act of maintaining happiness is exhausting, that the exhaustion drags on the metrics he is trying to improve, that the system's demand for joy produces only the performance of joy. The auditor, who has conducted hundreds of these assessments, recognizes the truth of what he is saying. She begins documenting his responses not as deficiencies but as evidence that the system itself is the pathology. The story follows the audit from arrival to departure, with the auditor's report becoming something her superiors never intended: a clinical description of how mandatory happiness manufactures despair.

## Source Inspiration
World 3 of "Change Without Notice" presents a fully realized happiness-audit dystopia: citizens photograph their breakfasts for wellness logs, listen to approved podcasts, take mandatory smile breaks, and submit to audits when their contentment metrics fall short. The auditor's question "Have you considered our Spontaneous Joy workshop series?" and the citizen's devastating insight -- "I think I might be trying too hard" / "Effortful happiness. Very common" -- are the seed. The story expands by centering the auditor rather than the audited.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Franz Kafka (style)**: Kafka's bureaucratic nightmares rendered in flat, matter-of-fact prose. The happiness audit should feel procedural, inevitable, the machinery of absurdity operating with administrative precision. The auditor should approach her work the way K. approaches his trial -- with bewildered compliance.
- **Yoko Ogawa (style)**: Ogawa's quiet, clinical observation of systems that erase human particularity. Her prose registers horror through precise, emotionally muted description rather than protest. The auditor should notice everything and react to nothing -- until she does.
- **The Trial (structure)**: The structural DNA of a system that processes people without ever revealing its purpose or authority. The audit has forms, protocols, follow-up procedures, escalation paths -- all leading nowhere meaningful.
- **1984 (themes)**: The thematic engine of a state that demands not merely compliance but genuine internal belief, and the psychic violence of being required to feel what you are told to feel. Thoughtcrime as emotional fraud.

## Notes
The story should be claustrophobic -- one apartment, one hour, two people. The auditor's report, quoted in fragments throughout, should begin as standard bureaucratic language and gradually shift into something closer to testimony. The man being audited should never become sympathetic in a conventional way; he should be ordinary, slightly annoying, exactly the kind of person the system was designed for.
