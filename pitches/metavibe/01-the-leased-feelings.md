## Premise
In a world where emotions are licensed from corporations and leased on monthly plans, a collections agent for a feelings repossession firm begins experiencing unauthorized sentiments -- compassion, guilt, attachment -- that belong to no known product catalog. She must decide whether to report the anomaly (which would trigger her own emotional audit) or hide it, knowing that unlicensed feelings are a criminal offense. The story follows one shift: six repossessions, each stripping a debtor of leased joy or grief or love, while her own contraband emotion grows stronger with every collection.

## Source Inspiration
World 128 of "Change Without Notice" depicts a man whose emotions are repossessed by collectors when his "emotional lease" expires. The bureaucratic language -- "premium tier," "basic happiness," "affection-adjacent substitutes" -- treats feeling as product. The devastating final image of a man taking on "new emotional debt" for "rental feelings" that would "have to do" is the seed. The story expands this from vignette to full narrative by centering the collector rather than the collected.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **George Saunders (style)**: Saunders's corporate-vernacular absurdism -- the language of HR memos and customer service applied to human suffering -- is the natural voice for emotion-as-product. His gift for characters trapped inside systems they cannot name.
- **Kazuo Ishiguro (style)**: Ishiguro's understated devastation and characters who participate in monstrous systems with quiet dignity. The collections agent should have Kathy H.'s measured acceptance slowly cracking.
- **Never Let Me Go (structure)**: The structural DNA of characters who serve a system designed to consume them, discussing their fate in polite, circumscribed terms. The agent's compliance should feel as inevitable as organ donation.
- **Repo Men by Eric Garcia (themes)**: The literal repossession of what keeps people alive, and the moral inversion when the collector becomes the collected. The thematic engine of debt as control.

## Notes
The unauthorized emotion should never be named directly -- the reader should recognize it as love or conscience while the protagonist lacks the vocabulary because she has only ever known product categories. Strong candidate for first-person narration in corporate register that slowly breaks down.
