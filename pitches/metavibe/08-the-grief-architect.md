## Premise
A grief architect -- someone who designs experiential environments where mourning can take whatever shape it needs -- is hired to build a grief space for a client whose loss she cannot identify. The client will not say who died, or how, or when. He only says: "I need a room where I can be sad about something I cannot remember." The architect has never encountered this before. Her profession assumes grief has an object. She builds the space anyway, iterating through designs -- a garden, a hospital corridor, an empty apartment -- and each iteration provokes genuine emotion in the client without ever reaching the source. The story is the construction process: what grief looks like when it has no name, no face, no memory to attach to.

## Source Inspiration
Worlds 6 and 7 of "Change Without Notice" juxtapose grief architecture as profession (World 6: "When someone comes to us with loss, we build them an environment where grief can take whatever shape it needs") with unmournable loss (World 7: K. receiving condolences for a spouse he cannot remember, performing grief without feeling it). The combination creates the seed: a professional grief-builder confronted by a client whose grief is real but objectless, mirroring the novel's central condition of inhabiting situations without the memories that would make them legible.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Kazuo Ishiguro (style)**: Ishiguro's mastery of characters who sense something essential is missing but cannot identify it. His restrained narration where the unsaid generates more emotional force than any revelation.
- **Carmen Maria Machado (style)**: Machado's architectural approach to emotion -- spaces as metaphors made literal, the body as a building, grief as a room you enter. Her surreal precision in rendering interior states as physical environments.
- **Lincoln in the Bardo (structure)**: The polyphonic structure of grief from multiple angles simultaneously, the dead and the living occupying the same space, the inability to let go as both pathology and devotion.
- **The Year of Magical Thinking (themes)**: The theme of grief as a cognitive process that resists rationality -- the magical belief that the right arrangement of objects or spaces can undo loss. The grief architect's profession is Didion's magical thinking institutionalized.

## Notes
Each grief space the architect builds should be described in architectural detail -- materials, lighting, acoustics, temperature. The spaces should get stranger as the story progresses, moving from conventional memorial design toward something the architect does not recognize as coming from her own unprocessed losses. The client's objectless grief should remain unresolved; the story's movement is the architect's realization that she is building a room for herself.
