## Premise
A minor deity -- worshipped by a modest cult of 7,832 believers -- receives notice that his godhood has been administratively terminated due to insufficient worship metrics. The Department of Celestial Affairs is reassigning his followers to other deities. The problem: he has no memory of being divine. No memory of answered prayers, of solstice ceremonies, of the people who built their spiritual lives around his existence. He is simply a man who has been told he was a god and failed at it. The story follows his attempt to visit the 7,832 people who are being notified that their god is gone -- not to reclaim divinity but to understand what he was to them, and what, if anything, he owes them now that he is merely human.

## Source Inspiration
World 129 of "Change Without Notice" presents a man whose divinity is revoked: "Insufficient worship metrics. Gods are required to maintain a minimum of 10,000 active worshippers. Your count dropped to 7,832 in the last quarter." The bureaucratic language for divine termination -- "decommissioned," "reassigned," "mortal status" -- and the devastating final image of 7,832 believers being told "their god was gone, that their prayers had been redirected" is the seed. The story expands by following the decommissioned god out of the office and into the lives of his former believers.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Neil Gaiman (style)**: Gaiman's gift for mythological beings navigating mundane modernity -- gods taking buses, divinity as a job with performance reviews. His blend of wonder and bureaucratic absurdity.
- **Flannery O'Connor (style)**: O'Connor's violent moments of grace -- the decommissioned god's encounters with former believers should be uncomfortable, graceless, shot through with revelations that neither party wants. Her grotesque compassion.
- **American Gods (structure)**: The structural DNA of old gods diminished by the modern world, trying to understand what worship means when belief has shifted. The road-trip structure of visiting believers one by one.
- **The Master and Margarita (themes)**: The theme of divine presence in a world that has bureaucratized the sacred. The absurdist collision of celestial power and administrative procedure. What happens when God is subject to a performance review.

## Notes
The former believers should each have a different relationship with the loss: one who is relieved, one who is furious, one who insists the god is still divine despite administrative revocation, one who never actually believed and was only performing worship for social reasons. The decommissioned god should discover, through these visits, what divinity actually meant -- and it should not be what the Department of Celestial Affairs measured.
