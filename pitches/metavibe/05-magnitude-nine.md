## Premise
A senior software architect at a major tech company spends a weekend building -- with an AI agent -- a system more sophisticated than anything her 200-person team has shipped in two years. Monday morning, she walks into the office carrying the knowledge that her entire department is obsolete, including herself. The story follows that single workday: standups she now sees as ritual, code reviews that are performance, sprint planning for features an AI could generate during lunch. She cannot tell anyone. Not because it is secret, but because the implications are so large that speaking them aloud would sound insane. The earthquake has already happened. The buildings are still standing. Nobody has noticed the ground moved.

## Source Inspiration
The "Magnitude 9 Earthquake" essay's central case study: "187 commits, 135,685 lines of TypeScript, and 73 GitHub issues closed in 36 hours -- with roughly 3-4 hours of human effort." The essay's thesis -- stop building your career, the earthquake has already happened -- becomes the protagonist's private revelation. The essay's metaphor of an earthquake where the destruction is invisible gives the story its structure: an ordinary workday experienced as aftermath by someone who has seen the seismograph but cannot show anyone the reading.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Don DeLillo (style)**: DeLillo's prose of ambient dread -- the world continuing normally while something catastrophic hums beneath the surface. His corporate spaces, white noise of office life, characters who perceive invisible toxicity.
- **Jennifer Egan (style)**: Egan's structural inventiveness and her ability to capture how technology reshapes consciousness from inside, particularly in "A Visit from the Goon Squad." Her characters who experience time as transformation happening too fast to articulate.
- **White Noise (structure)**: The structural DNA of an "airborne toxic event" that everyone experiences differently -- the same catastrophe interpreted as background noise by most, existential crisis by one. The office as the toxic plume no one can see.
- **Player Piano (themes)**: Vonnegut's theme of automation rendering the professional class purposeless -- not unemployed but meaningless. The particular despair of people whose skills, education, and identity were built on work that no longer needs doing.

## Notes
The story should never explain what she built over the weekend in technical detail. The reader does not need to understand the code. They need to understand the feeling of sitting in a meeting knowing that everyone in the room -- including yourself -- has been replaced by something that does not yet know it exists. The standup meeting should be the emotional climax: she almost speaks, does not, and the meeting ends.
