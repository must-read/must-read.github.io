## Premise
A Mexican subsistence farmer in the Yaqui Valley, 1952, is visited by a government extension agent carrying a sack of new wheat seed. The farmer has farmed the same land his father farmed. He believes steel plows steal the warmth from soil. He once drove cattle across a freshly plowed field to restore its heat. The story follows his decision -- over three days and two conversations with his dead father -- to plant or not plant the foreign seed. The dead father's voice is not supernatural; it is the way this man thinks, in the cadences and convictions of the person who taught him everything he knows about dirt.

## Source Inspiration
Two moments from the novel's research material: (1) a Mexican peasant who insisted a steel plow would extract the soil's "warmth" and make it infertile, then turned cattle onto his field to restore "heat"; (2) Borlaug's observation that "On Farm-Demonstration is a powerful persuader" -- the strategy of bypassing argument and simply giving farmers seed. The novel treats the farmer's resistance not as ignorance but as a coherent worldview, and the seed's arrival not as salvation but as a rupture. The extension agent is a minor figure in the novel; this pitch makes the farmer the center.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Rulfo)**: The Mexican landscape as a character -- dry, haunted, conversant with the dead. Rulfo's compression, where a single image (a man standing in a field holding a sack) contains an entire history. His ability to write rural poverty without condescension or romanticism, as a fact of the ground.
- **AuthorB (Berry)**: His lifelong argument that traditional farming knowledge is a form of intelligence, not an obstacle to progress. Berry's farmers are not backward; they are in conversation with specific soil. The tension between Berry's reverence for local knowledge and the story's recognition that the new seed will, in fact, feed millions.
- **WorkX (Pedro Paramo)**: The structural device of speaking with the dead as a natural extension of memory. The fragmentary, non-linear approach to time where past and present occupy the same field. The landscape as repository of unfinished conversations.
- **WorkY (Jayber Crow)**: The perspective of a man who watches his community change from inside it, neither fully resistant nor fully accepting. Jayber's understanding that what is lost in progress is real, even when what is gained is also real. The refusal to resolve that contradiction.

## Notes
The story should resist the obvious moral (accept the seed, feed your family, modernity wins). The farmer should plant the seed -- the historical record demands it -- but the story should make the cost of that planting legible. What dies is not superstition but a relationship with specific ground. The dead father should speak in short, declarative sentences that carry the weight of proverb without the tidiness. The extension agent should be well-meaning, young, educated, and unable to hear what the farmer is telling him. Two languages operating simultaneously, neither adequate. Target 4,000-5,500 words.
