## Premise
In 1942 Britain, a retired marine biologist is conscripted into a classified civilian program to harvest agar-producing seaweed from Scotland's coastline -- a substance suddenly vital to penicillin production and the war effort. Paired with a young lighthouse keeper's daughter who knows the tides better than anyone alive, he must navigate both the treacherous coast and his growing awareness that the quiet, overlooked substance he once studied in obscurity may determine whether the Allies can produce enough antibiotics to win the war. The story is really about how a man who spent decades being dismissed as irrelevant discovers that significance often arrives in forms no one recognizes.

## Source Inspiration
The article "The Origins of Agar" by Corrado Nai describes how in 1942, a remarkable civilian coalition -- researchers, lighthouse keepers, RAF members, scouts, teachers, students -- scavenged British coastlines for agar-producing seaweed. Agar was classified as a "critical war material" alongside copper and nickel because it was essential for growing the bacterial cultures needed to produce penicillin and vaccines. The image of a nation's most obscure citizens suddenly becoming vital to the war effort is deeply compelling fiction material.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Hilary Mantel (style)**: Her ability to render historical periods as lived, sensory experience rather than costume drama. Her prose gives weight to institutional maneuvering and the texture of daily life under large historical forces.
- **Ian McEwan (style)**: His precision with the interior life of characters confronting moral and physical peril, and his skill at building quiet dread from mundane circumstances.
- **The Remains of the Day (structure)**: The story of a person who devoted their life to something they believed was small and dignified, only to confront the question of whether it mattered. The journey structure, the retrospective narration, the restrained emotional devastation.
- **The Periodic Table (themes)**: Primo Levi's exploration of how chemical elements carry human stories -- here, agar as a substance that bridges the mundane and the monumental. The idea that a material can be a lens for understanding an era.

## Notes
The risk with WWII homefront stories is sentimentality. The McEwan influence should keep the emotional register honest -- the biologist's late-life reckoning with relevance should feel earned, not saccharine. The lighthouse keeper's daughter should be her own person, not a symbol. Consider a risk card like "withheld information" -- the biologist knows something about the agar program he cannot share.
