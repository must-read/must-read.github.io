## Premise
A biotech sales rep who supplies gray-market peptides to Silicon Valley's optimization elite attends a "peptide party" at a Woodside mansion where guests learn to mix and self-inject experimental compounds under the guidance of a charismatic former research chemist. The party is part networking event, part wellness ritual, part pharmaceutical speakeasy. Over the course of the evening, the sales rep watches the wealthy guests pursue biological transcendence while she -- who has access to everything they desire -- cannot afford the health insurance that would let her treat her own chronic illness through legitimate channels. The story traces one night in which the American pharmaceutical supply chain reveals itself as a closed loop of absurdity.

## Source Inspiration
The "Chinese Peptides" article by Jordan Schneider in ChinaTalk describes a gray-market peptide industry worth hundreds of millions, with Chinese pharmaceutical manufacturers supplying American consumers outside regulatory frameworks. Silicon Valley "peptide raves" where people "host parties sponsored by suppliers, where they teach you how to mix and inject your own peptides." The borderline-infinite pharmacological possibilities, the FDA-approved substances like semaglutide (Ozempic) sitting alongside experimental compounds -- the whole ecosystem is a ready-made satirical landscape.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Don DeLillo (style)**: The ambient dread of consumer capitalism rendered as white noise, the way his prose makes the absurd feel inevitable. His characters speak in the language of systems they don't understand.
- **Jennifer Egan (style)**: The structural inventiveness, the empathy for people caught in systems larger than themselves, the ability to render technology's effects on consciousness without resorting to techno-thriller mechanics.
- **White Noise (structure)**: A toxic event experienced through the fog of consumer culture. The protagonist surrounded by ambient information about health and risk, unable to process any of it into actionable knowledge. The party as "airborne toxic event."
- **Severance (themes)**: Late-capitalism as a literal disease. The way economic systems become biological systems, the protagonist who keeps working through the apocalypse because she doesn't know how to stop. The supply chain as narrative infrastructure.

## Notes
The single-evening structure is important -- this should feel like a chamber piece, not an epic. The party guests should be individually sympathetic, collectively monstrous. The sales rep's chronic illness should never become a metaphor; it should remain stubbornly, specifically medical. The Chinese supply chain should be present but not exoticized -- it's just the plumbing of global pharmaceutical capitalism.
