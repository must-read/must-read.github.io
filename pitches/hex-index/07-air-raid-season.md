## Premise
Two Ukrainian women -- a sports journalist in Kyiv and her college roommate who emigrated to Toronto in 2019 -- exchange voice messages during the 2026 Winter Olympics. The journalist is trying to cover the Games from a studio where the broadcast might be interrupted at any moment by an air raid siren. The emigre is trying to care about the coverage while her son asks why his cousins can't visit anymore. The story is told entirely through their voice messages -- never synchronized, always slightly off-rhythm, full of the things people say to fill silence and the silences that say more. It is about the fracture between those who live inside a war and those who watch it from the outside, and how neither position is bearable.

## Source Inspiration
Timothy Snyder's "Watching the Olympics in Ukraine" describes viewing sports where "viewing might be interrupted by an air raid siren or your electricity cut by a missile attack." Ukrainian coverage using a visible laptop sticker on set -- honest improvisation versus polished propaganda. The detail about Ukraine's skeleton racer disqualified for wearing a memorial helmet. The observation that normalcy becomes resistance. The brutal closing idea that Ukraine is "buying us time, buying us moments, with their pain, with their lives."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Svetlana Alexievich (style)**: The polyphonic voice, the way individual testimony builds into collective witness. Her refusal to synthesize or explain -- she lets the voices accumulate until the pattern emerges.
- **Olga Tokarczuk (style)**: The ability to hold multiple scales simultaneously -- the intimate and the geopolitical, the body and the nation. Her fragmentary narratives that trust the reader to assemble meaning.
- **Beautiful World, Where Are You (structure)**: Two women communicating across distance, the personal and the political braided together in real-time correspondence. The way Rooney uses the message format to expose what people choose to say and choose to omit.
- **The White Album (themes)**: Didion's exploration of how individuals process historical catastrophe through personal narrative, the way large events are experienced as fragmented sensory details rather than coherent stories.

## Notes
The voice-message format must be rigorous -- no third-person narration, no omniscient bridging. Each message should feel like a real person talking into her phone: half-finished thoughts, background noise, interruptions. The sports journalist should be funny -- gallows humor is essential. The emigre's guilt should manifest as over-attentiveness, not melodrama. The Olympics should remain in the background, a frame rather than a subject.
