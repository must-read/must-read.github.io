## Premise
During the All-India General Strike of February 2026, a young union organizer named Anjum rides her motorcycle through Bangalore's industrial outskirts, enforcing strike compliance at multinational factories. She is good at this -- persuading, cajoling, sometimes threatening workers who want to cross the picket line. At a Japanese-Indian joint venture plant, she encounters a childhood friend who cannot afford to miss a day's wages because her daughter needs surgery. The story follows a single day in which the abstract nobility of collective action meets the concrete cruelty of individual sacrifice, and Anjum discovers that enforcing solidarity can feel indistinguishable from enforcing obedience.

## Source Inspiration
Pavan Kulkarni's "Anatomy of a General Strike" describes 600,000+ workers participating in the All-India General Strike in Karnataka, February 2026. A key figure is Anjum, secretary of the Bangalore rural district committee for CITU, who serves as on-ground organizer. Workers on motorcycles in red T-shirts conduct advance reconnaissance and rapid response to non-compliant factories. The tension between Swiss, German, American, and Japanese multinationals and Indian workers provides the backdrop. The article describes "red flags fluttering" and revolutionary slogans alongside the gritty reality of enforcement.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Arundhati Roy (style)**: The sensory overload of India rendered in lyrical, politically furious prose. Her ability to hold the personal and the systemic in the same sentence, the way her metaphors carry political weight.
- **Rohinton Mistry (style)**: The compassionate, granular rendering of working-class Indian life, the bureaucratic absurdities that crush people, the humor that persists in conditions that should extinguish it.
- **A Fine Balance (structure)**: Ordinary people caught in political machinery larger than themselves, their individual stories revealing the human cost of abstract economic and political forces. The way Mistry builds toward catastrophe through accumulating small indignities.
- **Sorry to Bother You (themes)**: The surreal absurdity of labor under capitalism, the way organizing can become its own form of coercion, the comedy and horror of a system in which everyone is performing a role they didn't choose.

## Notes
The surrealist element should be light -- Bangalore's tech corridor already feels surreal, with gleaming campuses surrounded by slums. The childhood friend should not be a moral lesson; she should be right to want to cross the picket line. Anjum should be right to stop her. The story should refuse to resolve this contradiction. The slipstream element could emerge through the heat, the exhaustion, the way a 14-hour day on a motorcycle makes reality shimmer.
