## Premise
A thirty-one-year-old woman who was laid off from a competitive consulting firm six months ago has not looked for another job. She has instead constructed an elaborate daily routine of candles, journaling, linen bedding, slow mornings, and curated domestic rituals -- the "cozy girl" lifestyle that her Instagram documents with genuine artistry. Her former colleagues mock her. Her mother is alarmed. Her therapist is cautiously supportive. The story follows a single week in which three events converge: a former colleague's mental breakdown, a brand deal that would make her retreat financially viable, and the slow realization that her withdrawal from ambition is not rebellion or healing but a third thing she doesn't have a word for yet.

## Source Inspiration
Freddie deBoer's "Cozy Girl Lifestyle is a Rational Response to a Winner-Take-All Culture" argues that the aesthetic of "softness, comfort, domestic rituals, and emotional self-soothing" represents a rational response to impossible economic pressures, not frivolous escapism. The article identifies a dual nature: some frame it as "gentle rebellion" against hustle culture, others focus on authentic sensory experience. The mockery problem -- "self-styled clear-eyed, hard-headed" critics, often other women -- and the tension between performative wellness culture and genuine retreat both provide rich fictional territory.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **Ottessa Moshfegh (style)**: The unflinching interiority of women in states of withdrawal, the dark comedy of self-care as self-destruction, the body as both prison and refuge. Her prose refuses to judge her protagonists while making clear they are not okay.
- **Sheila Heti (style)**: The essayistic self-interrogation, the way her narrators circle questions about what a woman's life should look like without ever landing on an answer. Her refusal of plot in favor of thought.
- **Convenience Store Woman (structure)**: A protagonist whose contentment within a system everyone else finds pathological reveals more about the system than about her. The comedy of a person who has found exactly what they need in a place no one else values.
- **My Year of Rest and Relaxation (themes)**: Withdrawal as both symptom and cure, the American woman's body as site of cultural anxiety, the question of whether opting out of a broken system is wisdom or cowardice.

## Notes
The protagonist must not be satirized. The story should take her domestic practice seriously as an aesthetic and spiritual discipline while also acknowledging that Instagram monetization complicates every claim to authenticity. The brand deal should be the fulcrum: the moment retreat becomes career, does it stop being retreat? The former colleague's breakdown should not serve as vindication. The story should end without the protagonist knowing whether she is free or trapped.
