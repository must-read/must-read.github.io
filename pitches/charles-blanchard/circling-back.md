idea: "A writer attempts to tell the story of her father's childhood poverty. She writes it once as a realist account: the cold house, the bread, the absent mother. She writes it again as a lyric meditation on the carousel horses her father watched but never rode. She writes it a third time as a portrait of the small town, its transparent happiness, its wide streets that excluded one family without visible walls. Each version returns to the same images — the house, the cold, the wooden horses — but from a different angle, a different register, a different year of the writer's own life. The versions do not converge. The story never finishes. The fragmented structure enacts the central insight: poverty is not a story with a beginning, middle, and end. It is a condition. It circles."
notes: "Philippe left Charles Blanchard in multiple versions — not drafts toward a final form, but distinct approaches to the same material. The 'About' page argues this incompleteness may be intentional: 'The book that Philippe left is complete in its incompleteness.' Faulkner's Absalom structure — multiple narrators attempting and failing to construct a single coherent story from contradictory evidence — meets Cusk's austere, essayistic method where observation replaces narrative. WorkX gives the architecture: a story told three times, each telling revealing something the others cannot. WorkY gives the formal discipline: stripped-down prose where the frame itself (a writer trying and failing to write) becomes the subject."
