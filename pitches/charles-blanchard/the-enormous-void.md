idea: "A ten-year-old boy encounters a Thursday market for the first time — not the individual vegetables he has seen growing in fields, but the overwhelming congregation of all food in one place, 'a great disorder where the seasons were confused.' The encounter triggers something the text names precisely: 'a part of himself collapsed and left in the very middle of his body an enormous void.' He has discovered hunger. Not physical appetite — he has always been hungry — but existential hunger: the recognition of want as a permanent condition. After market day, the boy wanders his town obsessively, examining everything he encounters, unable to name what he is searching for. The story follows one week from Thursday to Wednesday, as the boy moves through a philosophical awakening he has no language for."
notes: "Philippe's market scene describes something more profound than a child seeing food: it is the moment consciousness divides into the self that desires and the self that knows it will not receive. The void is not hunger in the stomach but hunger as identity. Camus's lucid, Mediterranean prose about the absurd — a consciousness confronting a truth that reorganizes everything — meets Lispector's philosophical interiority, where a single encounter (a cockroach, a market stall) can dissolve the boundary between self and world. WorkX gives the structure: a week narrated in present tense, each day's events measured against a single transformative moment. WorkY gives the theme: a rational framework dissolving in the face of embodied experience, the body knowing what the mind cannot yet articulate."
