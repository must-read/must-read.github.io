idea: "A woman and her eleven-year-old son live in a one-room apartment in a Mississippi Delta town. She bakes bread once a week from flour and whatever she can stretch into it. As the days pass, the bread goes stale, then hard, then requires water to chew. The story follows one week — Sunday baking to Saturday crumbs — through the accumulating tension between what the bread is and what it must be made to last. She never says the word 'hungry.' He never asks for more. The dialogue is spare, functional, circling the unspeakable. The bread itself becomes an object of religious intensity: covered, protected, rationed, spoken of in whispers."
notes: "From Philippe's extended meditation on bread as the central reality of poverty. Solange treats bread with 'veneration' — protecting it from waste, mice, and her own appetite. She cannot afford illness or growth because both threaten the bread supply. Hemingway's iceberg method — declarative sentences bearing submerged weight, dialogue where what matters goes unsaid — meets Morrison's sacred, incantatory treatment of objects that hold historical suffering. WorkX gives the architecture: two people in a confined space, a reality between them that conversation cannot touch. WorkY gives the theme: an inherited, embodied grief that possesses the physical world — the bread as haunted object, the house that will not let them forget."
