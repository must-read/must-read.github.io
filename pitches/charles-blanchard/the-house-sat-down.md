idea: "A seven-year-old girl is left alone each morning in a house that Philippe describes as resembling 'an old woman who had sat down at a certain distance from the road.' The house is set apart from the others in a small town — not dramatically, just enough. It has three chairs (one broken), a bed, a bread-bin, and walls that sweat when the temperature drops. The story spans a single winter day, dawn to dark, as the child waits for her mother to return from cleaning other people's houses. The house is not haunted in any supernatural sense. But the cold has a will. The walls lean inward. The afternoon dark arrives like something that has been waiting. By evening, the child feels 'ancient,' approaching a death she cannot name. The domestic gothic here is literal: the house itself, through deprivation, becomes the monster."
notes: "Philippe renders the Blanchard house as a living presence — withdrawn, sorrowful, leaning — and the cold as an active antagonist. The child sits alone for hours, aging decades in an afternoon. Jackson's domestic gothic sensibility — Hill House as a malevolent architecture that wants to keep you — meets Enriquez's social-realist horror where poverty and the supernatural are indistinguishable. WorkX provides the structure of a house with its own intention, pressing in on an isolated consciousness. WorkY provides the Latin American tradition of ghosts that are actually social conditions, horror that emerges from material deprivation rather than metaphysical threat."
