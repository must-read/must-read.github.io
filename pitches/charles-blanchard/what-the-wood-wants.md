idea: "A twelve-year-old boy who has spent his childhood in a state approaching catatonia — disproportionately long arms, damp skin, enormous blue eyes with 'the expression of madness,' hands flat on his knees for hours — is delivered by his desperate mother to her brother-in-law's clog-making workshop in a neighboring village. The workshop is an overwhelming sensory assault: tools with names like asciot and paroir and butoir, wood shavings covering every surface, the uncle working with 'furious' intensity. The boy is terrified. He retreats to the dark back room, the 'chamber,' where the uncle's wife resents his presence. The story follows his first month: expelled from the chamber repeatedly, trembling, slowly learning to rasp and blacken and wax clogs — discovering that labor can be purpose, that a shaped thing can hold meaning his hollow existence never could. One day he asks: 'Uncle, would you like me to try splitting your wood?'"
notes: "Philippe's account of Charles's transformation through craft is one of the most unsentimental depictions of redemption in French literature. The boy is not healed by love or understanding but by repetitive physical labor. Robinson's meditative, theological attention to domestic space and the sacred in ordinary work meets Johnson's raw, hallucinatory prose about damaged people finding unexpected grace. WorkX gives the structure: a child navigating between two incompatible domestic spaces (the shop and the chamber, like Ruth between Fingerbone and the world). WorkY gives the emotional texture: sudden, unearned moments of transcendence amid squalor — not redemption exactly, but something that briefly makes living bearable."
