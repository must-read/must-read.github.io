idea: "A boy attends a county fair with his grandmother in rural Kansas, sometime in the 1980s. There is a Ferris wheel. Every child rides it. He does not ride it. Not because anyone forbids him — his grandmother would find the dollar — but because something inside him has already learned where he belongs. He steps back when others press forward. He occupies, as Philippe writes, 'the last place with naturalness.' The story follows a single afternoon, told in retrospect by the man he becomes thirty years later, who now understands that the Ferris wheel was the first moment his class became his identity — not imposed from outside but grown from within, like a second skeleton."
notes: "From the famous carousel passage where Charles watches other children ride while 'crossing his hands on his belly.' Philippe's insight is not that poverty excludes the child but that the child internalizes exclusion before anyone needs to enforce it. Ishiguro's retrospective narration — a man looking back at the moment he should have understood — meets Robinson's radiant, theological attention to ordinary life. WorkX gives the structure: a narrator who always knew the truth but waited decades to face it. WorkY gives the tone: a letter-like confession that finds holiness in small-town poverty, the way Ames finds God in the light on a child's face."
