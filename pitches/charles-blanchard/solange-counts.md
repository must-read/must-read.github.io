idea: "A widow named Solange earns ten francs a month cleaning two households. Her rent takes most of it. She must calculate everything: how much wood to burn (enough to survive, not enough to feel warm), how many days the bread will last, whether she can afford her son to grow (larger feet mean new shoes, and new shoes are impossible). The story follows Solange through one month — four weeks of arithmetic that is also a prayer. She is not passive. She is ferociously intelligent in her deprivation, engineering survival from almost nothing. The story's drama is entirely internal: the constant computation of a woman who knows that one illness, one unexpected expense, one broken chair will collapse the fragile equation she lives inside."
notes: "Philippe gives Solange extraordinary interiority — she is not a suffering object but a strategist whose battlefield is a budget of ten francs. Her hope is fixated on her son's twelfth birthday, when he can apprentice with her brother-in-law the clog-maker, and this milestone functions like a promised land she walks toward for years. Morrison's incantatory, myth-making prose about Black women who build worlds from nothing meets Ferrante's fierce, intellectual narration of female consciousness under economic pressure. WorkX gives the structure: a woman's interior life expanding to fill the narrative, her friendship with poverty as adversary rendered with the same complexity as Sula and Nel's bond. WorkY gives the class tension: the shame of begging bread from farm women, the precise social geometry of who gives and who receives."
