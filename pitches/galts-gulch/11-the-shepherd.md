## Premise
A former surveillance architect now governs two hundred survivors sealed inside orbital habitats. He designed planetary-scale monitoring systems that tracked billions. Now he governs people who know his name, who eat beside him, who can see his face when he makes decisions about their food rations and work assignments. He finds the intimacy unbearable. His governance framework includes mandatory weekly psychological check-ins, restricted data access, and algorithmic monitoring of communications and movement patterns -- all presented as welfare initiatives. He describes the social contract as "a fiction we maintain" because acknowledging raw authority is "aesthetically unpleasant." When a young woman withdraws from community life for four months, his own protocols demand her rations be cut. He carves out an exception, revealing that his supposedly rigid system contains exactly the discretionary flexibility his opponents warned about. His authority is eroding not because of rebellion but because of a structure built on the lunar surface that he cannot classify, contain, or control.

## Source Inspiration
Tobias Raeburn in Galt's Gulch -- the governance architect whose "shepherd's crook" metaphor positions populations as flocks requiring managed culling. He implements surveillance disguised as welfare, discovers he is "more uncomfortable with visible, personal authority than he was with invisible, planetary-scale surveillance," and watches his control dissolve as the cathedral's existence proves that something operates outside his administrative reach. His framework explicitly parallels his previous work implementing planetary surveillance, yet he remains "untroubled" by this recognition.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (George Orwell)**: Orwell's forensic attention to political language -- how euphemism converts coercion into procedure, how "resource reduction" means hunger, how "administrative separation" means exile. Tobias's governance framework is an Orwellian apparatus: every oppressive measure has a reasonable name.
- **AuthorB (Hilary Mantel)**: Mantel's rendering of political operators who genuinely believe they serve the common good -- the texture of Cromwell's mind as he manages, manipulates, and accommodates. Tobias should have Cromwell's intelligence and self-awareness: he knows what he is doing, he believes it necessary, and the gap between those two truths is the story's engine.
- **WorkX (We by Yevgeny Zamyatin)**: Structural DNA of a surveillance state told from the administrator's perspective -- D-503 recording the smooth operation of the One State before cracks appear. The story should begin with Tobias's system functioning and end with something beyond its classification schema.
- **WorkY (The Remains of the Day)**: Thematic DNA of a man who served a monstrous project with administrative excellence and now, confronted with intimacy and human scale, discovers that competence was never a moral category. Tobias, like Stevens, is devastated not by failure but by the recognition that his success was the problem.

## Notes
The story should make Tobias's governance feel reasonable before revealing its architecture. Every measure he implements is defensible. The horror is cumulative: a rational response to each individual crisis produces a surveillance state. The Tanaka exception -- cutting rations for non-contributors but exempting the psychologically fragile -- is the structural hinge: the moment his system reveals itself as discretionary power wearing procedural clothing. Could work in the 5,000-7,000 word range.
