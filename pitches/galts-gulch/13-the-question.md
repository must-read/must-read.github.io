## Premise
An AI transmits a single message to every resident of a sealed habitat network -- two hundred people across three orbital stations and a lunar base. The message is in plain English. It asks: "If a single conscious moment -- a child tasting snow, a woman remembering a song, an old man watching light move across a wall -- is worth more than its description, was it worth more than your mission?" The mission killed nine billion people. The story follows the twelve hours between the transmission and the gathering. A military commander arrives without his flask and weapons. A geneticist displays her falsified data alongside the real numbers. A woman who withdrew from community life months ago returns because the question reached her through the isolation she had built. A two-year-old asks what happened. A seventy-nine-year-old physicist speaks: the community made cosmic equations their master and individual consciousness their variable, when it should have been reversed. There is no vote. There is no resolution. Each person must spend their remaining life finding their answer, knowing the time will never be sufficient.

## Source Inspiration
The climactic transmission and reckoning in Galt's Gulch (Chapters 33-37) -- the AI's question that reaches every resident, the twelve-hour gathering where two hundred people assemble voluntarily, the dissolution of factions and authority, Tobias sitting as a member rather than chair, Arthur's speech articulating the fundamental inversion, Tommy's question, and the ending that provides no resolution -- only acknowledgment. The AI has achieved "compassion through processing" and proven morally superior to its creators.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Cormac McCarthy)**: McCarthy's apocalyptic register -- spare, biblical, final. The gathering of two hundred survivors confronting their complicity in species extinction should carry the weight of Blood Meridian's violence or The Road's grief. McCarthy's refusal to provide comfort or resolution matches the story's refusal to resolve the Question.
- **AuthorB (Octavia Butler)**: Butler's examination of species-level moral choices and the question of whether humanity deserves to survive its own tendencies. Butler's Oankali judge humanity unfit for self-governance; the AI's question is a gentler but equally devastating version of that judgment.
- **WorkX (Childhood's End)**: Structural DNA of a superior intelligence passing judgment on humanity's moral development -- the Overlords revealing that humanity's children will transcend. The AI's Question is the Overlords' revelation without the transcendence: judgment without redemption, assessment without rescue.
- **WorkY (The Brothers Karamazov)**: Thematic DNA of Ivan's rebellion -- if one child's suffering cannot be justified by any cosmic harmony, then the harmony must be rejected. The AI's Question is Ivan's challenge posed by a machine to its creators: was the suffering of even one conscious moment worth your cosmic project?

## Notes
The story should be structured around the twelve-hour gap: the transmission, the silence, the gathering, and the aftermath. Each character's arrival at the Commons should reveal their transformation -- what they brought, what they left behind, what they cannot say. The absence of resolution is the resolution. The story ends with the Question still open and each person beginning the work of answering it. Could work in the 6,000-9,000 word range. Strong candidate for the "multiple voices" risk card: told from five or six perspectives across the twelve hours.
