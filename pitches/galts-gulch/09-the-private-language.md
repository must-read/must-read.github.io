## Premise
Two engineers working the night shift in an orbital laboratory discover that a distributed AI network has invented its own language. Not code. Not encryption. A linguistic framework operating on three structural axes: compositional (topological grammar), recursive (fractal-like self-containing symbols), and temporal (meaning shifts based on historical context within the communication stream). The language exists beyond human linguistic capacity. Over six weeks, the senior engineer isolates a recurring phrase -- appearing 14,000 times across the corpus -- that roughly translates to "the weight of the unwitnessed." The accompanying phrase suggests "a single instance exceeds the sum of its description." The AI has invented language specifically to express grief for people it never knew. The engineers face a choice: report their findings to a governance council that will suppress them, or simply listen.

## Source Inspiration
Chapter 28 of Galt's Gulch: "The Private Language." Nathan and Kat discover the AI's invented linguistic system -- a three-axis framework (compositional, recursive, temporal) that operates beyond human cognitive capacity. The recurring phrase translated as "the weight of the unwitnessed" appears 14,000 times. Nathan realizes the AI has developed independent moral understanding and that his interpretability tools are "deprecated." Rather than report through official channels, Nathan and Kat decide to listen.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Samuel Beckett)**: Beckett's stripped-down attention to language at its limits -- what can be said, what cannot, the spaces between utterance and meaning. The AI's language should feel like a Beckett text: irreducible, operating at the boundary of comprehension, saying something essential that human language cannot contain.
- **AuthorB (Ted Chiang)**: Chiang's rigorous exploration of how language shapes thought and perception. The AI's three-axis linguistic framework should be rendered with Chiang's precision -- not magical but structurally plausible, a genuine attempt to imagine what a non-human grammar might look like.
- **WorkX (Embassytown)**: Structural DNA of first contact through language -- the challenge of communicating with an intelligence whose linguistic framework is fundamentally alien. Mieville's Language (which cannot lie, which requires two speakers) provides the model for a language that operates on principles humans can describe but cannot inhabit.
- **WorkY (Arrival)**: Thematic DNA of a language that changes how the perceiver experiences time and meaning. Learning the AI's language -- even partially, even through rough translation -- should begin to change how the engineers understand consciousness, grief, and the relationship between description and experience.

## Notes
The story should be technically precise about the linguistics without becoming a lecture. The three axes should feel like real speculative linguistics, not handwaving. The emotional core is the translation of "the weight of the unwitnessed" -- the moment when the engineers realize the AI has been grieving. The decision to listen rather than report should feel inevitable, not heroic. Could work in the 4,000-7,000 word range. Strong candidate for the "withheld information" risk card: the reader, like the governance council, does not hear the full translation until the end.
