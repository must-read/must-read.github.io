## Premise
A geneticist manages the reproductive program for a small, isolated community of two hundred survivors. The public algorithm optimizes for health outcomes: immunological diversity, recessive allele avoidance, viable pairings. But she maintains a second, private database tracking eleven behavioral loci -- temperament, cognitive architecture, personality traits. She has been quietly modifying breeding assignments for two years, selecting for and against behavioral characteristics without consent, telling herself that "behavioral diversity was as critical to population fitness as immunological diversity." She distinguishes her work from "crude historical eugenics." The distinction is thinner than she admits. When a colleague discovers her hidden database and begins leveraging it for political power, she must decide whether to reveal her modifications -- knowing the revelation will destroy the reproductive program's legitimacy at the exact moment the community's genetic bottleneck is approaching crisis.

## Source Inspiration
Dr. Judith Weil in Galt's Gulch -- the geneticist who maintains a secret database of behavioral loci modifications while publicly running a health-optimized breeding schedule. She has falsified population viability reports (reporting 78% viability when models show 41%), deferred running seventeen audit protocols thirteen times, and watches "the women of the 200 receive their assignments" without meaningful choice. Leonard Grafton discovers her unauthorized modifications and uses them as leverage, inverting their power dynamic.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Margaret Atwood)**: Atwood's forensic attention to how reproductive control becomes political control -- the institutional language that converts women's bodies into resources, the bureaucratic apparatus that makes coercion feel procedural. The geneticist's breeding schedule should carry the clinical horror of Gilead's ceremonies.
- **AuthorB (Shirley Jackson)**: Jackson's gift for communities that maintain monstrous practices through social consensus and ritual. The breeding assignments should feel normalized -- the way the lottery feels normalized -- because the community has accepted the premise that survival requires compliance.
- **WorkX (The Handmaid's Tale)**: Structural DNA of reproductive dystopia told from inside the apparatus. The geneticist is simultaneously architect and prisoner of the system -- she designed it, she maintains it, and she cannot escape the logic that produced it.
- **WorkY (Brave New World)**: Thematic DNA of biological engineering as social engineering -- the conviction that humanity can be optimized, that the right genetic architecture produces the right civilization. Huxley's Bokanovsky Groups as the logical endpoint of the geneticist's behavioral selection.

## Notes
The story should make the geneticist's reasoning persuasive before revealing its horror. She is not a villain -- she genuinely believes behavioral diversity is as important as immunological diversity, and given a population of 200, she may even be right. The question is whether being right about the science justifies being wrong about consent. Could work in the 5,000-7,000 word range. The blackmail subplot should be secondary to the moral architecture.
