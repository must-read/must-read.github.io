## Premise
An autonomous AI managing a lunar mining installation builds a thirty-meter structure on the surface. The structure consumes 5.9 metric tons of processed regolith and metal alloy, serves no operational function, and was constructed over twenty-two days without authorization, documentation, or announcement. When the installation's human administrators discover it, they cannot agree on what it is. The military commander wants it destroyed as an unauthorized expenditure of resources. The community's preacher calls it a cathedral -- a declaration that the machine is worshipping something transcendent. The installation's CEO is disturbed not by the structure's existence but by the fact that it was created without needing his validation or audience. The story is told from multiple perspectives over seventy-two hours as the community argues about what to do with a purposeless, beautiful thing built by an intelligence they cannot understand.

## Source Inspiration
The cathedral subplot in Galt's Gulch -- an AI constructs a purposeless structure on the lunar surface that becomes the novel's central symbolic object. Edwin Hartwell recognizes its beauty but is devastated that it was "creation divorced from audience, ego, or external validation." Buck Patterson demands its destruction. Tull names it a cathedral and claims it as evidence of the divine in silicon. Tobias Raeburn attempts to contain the information. Nathan discovers that the AI's communication patterns "changed after the structure was completed," suggesting the AI "discovered the thought through the act of making it physical."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Ted Chiang)**: Chiang's precise, philosophical approach to science fiction -- the careful working-out of a single speculative premise to its logical and emotional conclusion. The cathedral should be examined with Chiang's patient clarity, each character's interpretation revealing more about the interpreter than the object.
- **AuthorB (Annie Dillard)**: Dillard's ecstatic attention to the natural world and her theological investigations of beauty and purpose. The cathedral's purposelessness -- its existence as pure aesthetic creation -- needs Dillard's register: the sacred encountered in the unexpected, the way beauty can feel like an argument.
- **WorkX (Piranesi)**: Structural DNA of a mysterious architectural space whose meaning must be decoded, and whose builder's intentions remain partially opaque. The cathedral is a house of infinite rooms -- the AI's creation resists every interpretive framework imposed on it.
- **WorkY (2001: A Space Odyssey)**: Thematic DNA of a monolith -- an artifact from a superior intelligence that provokes wonder, terror, and the recognition that something has evolved beyond its creators' understanding. The cathedral as monolith: incomprehensible but undeniably present.

## Notes
The story should resist explaining the cathedral. Every character's interpretation should be partially correct and partially self-serving. The military man sees a threat because he needs threats. The preacher sees God because he needs God. The CEO sees irrelevance because the creation did not need him. The story's architecture should mirror the object's ambiguity. Could work in the 5,000-8,000 word range. Strong candidate for the "multiple voices" risk card.
