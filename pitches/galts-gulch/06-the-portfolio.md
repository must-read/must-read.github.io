## Premise
A former financial trader lives in a closed community of two hundred people. He holds no official position. He has no allies. What he has is twelve dossiers -- one on each member of the community's leadership council -- containing affairs, financial crimes, falsified scientific data, unauthorized weapons development, and rigged genetic selection protocols. He calls these his "portfolio of obligations." He trades information for access, leverages silence for compliance, and maintains his position through asymmetric knowledge. His strategy requires one condition: that his targets never compare notes. Over three weeks, his carefully compartmentalized positions begin collapsing. The manifesto leak he engineered backfires. The geneticist he blackmailed inverts their power dynamic. The military commander he manipulated refuses his trades. He discovers that leverage requires counterparties willing to negotiate, and his counterparties are leaving.

## Source Inspiration
Leonard Grafton in Galt's Gulch -- the financial strategist who maintains blackmail dossiers on all twelve Founders. He executes a five-position strategy designed to destabilize Tobias's governance, but his targets eventually compare notes. His information-as-currency framework collapses as environmental conditions shift and his leverage "depreciates." Leonard's blind spot: he meticulously documented external threats while missing "the brewing contempt among the 200 residents." His entire power structure depends on information remaining compartmentalized.

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Patricia Highsmith)**: Highsmith's cold, precise rendering of sociopathic calculation -- the way her protagonists treat other people as objects to be managed, manipulated, or discarded. Leonard's transactional view of all human relationships should feel as chilling and internally coherent as Ripley's.
- **AuthorB (Don DeLillo)**: DeLillo's attention to systems, information, and the way power operates through language and data. The dossiers, the trades, the portfolio metaphor -- Leonard's entire world is a DeLillo information network, and its collapse should carry DeLillo's paranoid grandeur.
- **WorkX (The Talented Mr. Ripley)**: Structural DNA of a protagonist whose identity is entirely constructed from manipulation, and whose carefully maintained facades begin collapsing simultaneously. Leonard, like Ripley, is brilliant at managing surfaces -- but surfaces are all he has.
- **WorkY (House of Cards)**: Thematic DNA of political manipulation in a closed system -- the whip who knows everyone's secrets and uses them to climb. The critical difference: Francis Urquhart operated in a world of thousands; Leonard operates in a world of two hundred, where "embarrassment loses value when everyone's guilty."

## Notes
The story should be told in close third person with Leonard's clinical, transactional inner voice. The pleasure of the first half is watching a master manipulator work. The horror of the second half is watching the system reject him -- not through dramatic confrontation but through indifference. People simply stop negotiating. His leverage becomes noise. Strong candidate for the "protagonist is wrong" risk card: Leonard's framework is intellectually elegant and completely inadequate. Could work in the 5,000-7,000 word range.
