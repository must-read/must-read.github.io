## Premise
An anonymous source sends an encrypted file to a tech company's ethics officer -- not a whistleblower dump, but a single question: "What do you actually know about Clarity?" The officer begins investigating internally, discovering that the personalization product she approved is a subsystem of something much larger: a predictive influence architecture that models not just user desires but future actions and beliefs. The thriller tension comes not from external danger but from the officer's dawning recognition that she cannot unknow what she has learned, and that the system already predicted she would learn it.

## Source Inspiration
The anonymous encrypted email in Book 1 -- the classified documents revealing Clarity's true scope, the single devastating question "What do you actually know about Clarity?" The discovery that Project Prism (personalization) feeds into Clarity (prediction and influence), with "influence" mentioned 17 times and "persuasion" 11 times in the documents. The consent theater problem: "Offering controls doesn't address the core consent issue if most users never change the defaults."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Patricia Highsmith)**: Highsmith's cold, clinical psychological suspense -- the moral ambiguity, the unsettling intimacy of discovering something terrible about a system you helped build. Her protagonists don't fight evil; they recognize it in themselves. The ethics officer should have that Highsmith quality of the ground shifting beneath a character who thought she was standing on solid ground.
- **AuthorB (George Orwell)**: Orwell's transparent, precise prose and his horror of political language -- the way "personalization" means surveillance, "optimization" means control, "user modeling" means prediction of future behavior. The Orwellian element is in the vocabulary: every euphemism is load-bearing.
- **WorkX (We)**: The structural DNA of Zamyatin's mathematical dystopia -- a society of total transparency where the protagonist's growing awareness of their own unfreedom IS the plot. The ethics officer's investigation should have that quality of discovering the walls of a prison she didn't know she was in.
- **WorkY (The Age of Surveillance Capitalism)**: The thematic DNA of Zuboff's framework -- behavioral surplus extraction, the prediction and modification of human behavior as a market, the rendering of human experience as raw material. Clarity isn't science fiction; it's Zuboff's argument given a product name.

## Notes
The psychological thriller frame works because the danger is epistemological, not physical. No one threatens the ethics officer. No one needs to. The system's prediction includes her investigation -- her discovery is a feature, not a bug, because her eventual silence (or departure, or public statement that changes nothing) was modeled. The "unreliable narrator" risk card would be devastating: the reader should gradually wonder whether the officer's investigation is genuine or whether she's performing the role of "concerned ethics officer" that the system designed for her.
