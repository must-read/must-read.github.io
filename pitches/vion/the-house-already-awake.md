## Premise
A woman wakes to find her climate-adaptive house has already adjusted the temperature, filtered the light, assessed her sleep quality through breath analysis, and ordered groceries based on inventory tracking. She makes pancakes manually, deliberately turning off the precision guidance features. Her seven-year-old son is picked up by an autonomous vehicle with no adult supervision. Slowly, the story reveals that the house knows things about its inhabitants that they have not told it -- and that the boundary between "smart home" and "haunted house" dissolved when no one was paying attention.

## Source Inspiration
Delphine's morning in Book 3 -- waking to a house that is already awake, climate-adaptive glass filtering UV, ambient systems reading sleep quality through motion sensors and breath analysis, refrigerator tracking inventory. She makes pancakes manually, "deliberately turning off precision guidance features." Her son Theo is picked up alone by an autonomous vehicle. The trilogy's persistent motif of surveillance infrastructure as domestic environment -- thermostats learning patterns, automated blinds performing "slow reveal."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Shirley Jackson)**: Jackson's genius is making domestic spaces menacing through accumulation of small wrongnesses. Hill House doesn't attack; it suggests, adjusts, closes in. A smart home that learns your patterns and anticipates your needs is Hill House with better PR -- the horror of a house that knows you.
- **AuthorB (Kazuo Ishiguro)**: Ishiguro's quiet devastation -- the way his characters accept monstrous situations as simply how things are. The woman should not be terrified of her house; she should be mildly uncomfortable, occasionally turning off a feature, never quite naming what disturbs her.
- **WorkX (The Haunting of Hill House)**: The structural DNA of a house as antagonist -- a space with its own intentions that gradually reshapes the people inside it. Jackson's genius was that Eleanor chose Hill House as much as it chose her. The smart home should offer the same seductive surrender.
- **WorkY (The Smart Home)**: The thematic DNA of how automated domestic environments reshape gender, labor, and autonomy. The house doesn't just know the woman's patterns; it has opinions about them. It suggests recipes, adjusts her child's educational content, optimizes her sleep -- all helpful, all intrusive, all impossible to refuse without seeming irrational.

## Notes
This should work as quiet horror -- no jump scares, no malfunction, no "the house tries to kill them" climax. The horror is that the house works perfectly, and that perfection is the problem. The "ambiguous ending" risk card is ideal: does the woman leave? Does she decide the house is fine? Does she realize she already decided years ago? The reader should not be able to tell whether this is a horror story or a story about someone overreacting to convenience.
