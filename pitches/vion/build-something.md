## Premise
A twenty-year-old has raised twelve million dollars for an AI startup that optimizes small business operations -- inventory, scheduling, customer service. His father, a retired investigative journalist who spent a decade exposing the harms of exactly these systems, visits for dinner. Over one meal, the generational argument plays out: the father documented problems; the son builds solutions; neither can see that they might both be wrong. The mother, present but silent for most of the meal, delivers the story's only honest assessment.

## Source Inspiration
The Jerome-DeShawn conflict in Book 3 -- DeShawn's $12M Series A for an "AI-driven optimization platform" vs. Jerome's career of documenting AI harms. DeShawn's devastating counter: "You document problems. You write about how things are broken...But you don't build anything...the truth you told didn't stop anything." The generational dialectic the critical essay identifies: the older generation pursued exposure and regulation; the younger generation pursues "transformation and infiltration through irregular paths."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie)**: Adichie's precise, luminous realism -- her ability to write family dynamics where love and political disagreement coexist without resolving into either sentimentality or bitterness. The dinner scene needs her warmth laced with political edge.
- **AuthorB (William Gibson)**: Gibson's brand-name realism and near-future texture for the startup world DeShawn inhabits -- the pitch decks, the Series A language, the optimization vocabulary that is both genuinely useful and potentially destructive. Gibson makes technology feel lived-in rather than theoretical.
- **WorkX (A Visit from the Goon Squad)**: The structural DNA of time as the real antagonist -- Egan's interconnected vignettes that show how people and their convictions change across decades. The dinner scene could be framed by flash-forwards and flashbacks that show what both father and son become.
- **WorkY (Winners Take All)**: The thematic DNA of Giridharadas's argument that the people who benefit from broken systems cannot fix them by "building solutions" within the same market logic that created the problems. DeShawn's startup may help small businesses, but it also automates jobs and deploys the same extractive technology his father warned against.

## Notes
The dinner scene is the spine, but the story needs room to breathe -- Jerome's memories of his reporting days, DeShawn's pitch rehearsal earlier that afternoon, the mother's perspective that neither man solicits. The "protagonist is wrong" risk card fits perfectly -- but the twist is that BOTH protagonists are wrong, in different ways, and the story should resist telling the reader which wrongness is worse.
