## Premise
A corporate ethics officer at a major AI company discovers that her carefully documented objections to a surveillance product have been repurposed as marketing copy -- her dissent is being cited in investor decks as proof of "rigorous internal ethics review." She must decide whether to keep her seat at the table or burn it down, knowing that leaving will simply mean her replacement approves everything she blocked.

## Source Inspiration
Ananya Ramaswamy in Book 1 of the Eighth Oblivion trilogy -- an ethics officer whose genuine concerns about Project Prism and Clarity are absorbed into "ethics theater." Her objections lead to cosmetic two-week delays that were already planned, and her presence provides legitimacy cover. The critical essay names this "distributed mendacity" -- harm occurring through "the aggregate of reasonable decisions."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (Kazuo Ishiguro)**: Ishiguro's gift is rendering people who participate in monstrous systems with quiet dignity, never quite naming what they're complicit in. The ethics officer should have that same understated devastation -- she almost understands what she's part of, but the language keeps slipping.
- **AuthorB (George Saunders)**: Saunders's corporate vernacular -- the euphemisms, the motivational language, the way institutions make horror sound reasonable -- is the perfect counterweight. His dark absurdism captures how "personalization" means surveillance.
- **WorkX (Never Let Me Go)**: The structural DNA of characters who serve a system that will destroy them, and who discuss their situation in polite, circumscribed terms. The ethics officer's compliance should feel as inevitable and heartbreaking as Kathy H.'s.
- **WorkY (The Circle)**: The thematic DNA of tech utopianism as totalitarianism -- the language of transparency, optimization, and user empowerment that conceals extraction. The story's vocabulary should drip with this.

## Notes
Could work as a single day -- the ethics officer arrives at work, has three meetings, reads an anonymous leak, and leaves. The horror should be structural, not dramatic: nothing "happens" except she realizes the shape of the thing she's inside. Strong candidate for the "story-as-document" risk card (told through meeting minutes, emails, ethics review forms).
