## Premise
A retired federal judge, widowed two years, prepares a lecture on constitutional resilience in the age of algorithmic governance. As she drafts and redrafts, the lecture keeps collapsing into confession: the frameworks she built held, and they held nothing. Between drafts, she tends her late wife's garden, grades student papers, and confronts a student's question she cannot answer -- "If the frameworks don't work, why do we study this?"

## Source Inspiration
Ruth Abramson across Books 2 and 3 -- the constitutional law professor and retired judge whose expertise failed to prevent the crisis she predicted. Her unfinished op-ed about Fourth Amendment inadequacy, her admission that "The frameworks held. And they held nothing," her widow's grief for Susan. The Stanford lecture where she deviates from prepared remarks toward honesty: "Technological systems adapt faster than legal systems."

## Why These Formula Elements
- **AuthorA (John le Carre)**: Le Carre's moral exhaustion -- the weariness of someone who served institutions they no longer believe in -- is Ruth's exact condition. His bureaucratic realism, the layered prose that conveys how systems grind down the people who serve them, matches her voice.
- **AuthorB (Marilynne Robinson)**: Robinson's meditative, theological attention to ordinary life anchors the story in the body -- the garden, the empty bed, the kitchen where Ruth no longer cooks for two. Her long, cadenced sentences give the intellectual material warmth and gravity.
- **WorkX (Remains of the Day)**: The structural DNA of a dignified professional reviewing a career of service and slowly recognizing that the institution they served was unworthy. Stevens's emotional suppression maps directly onto Ruth's intellectual suppression -- she keeps trying to write the lecture instead of the confession.
- **WorkY (The Age of Surveillance Capitalism)**: The thematic DNA of how legal and institutional frameworks are structurally inadequate to constrain technological systems that operate at speeds and scales those frameworks were never designed for.

## Notes
The story alternates between lecture drafts (increasingly honest) and domestic scenes (increasingly raw). The garden is Susan's -- maintaining it is both grief work and a metaphor Ruth would hate for its neatness. The "unreliable narrator" risk card could work: Ruth's intellectual authority is impeccable, but her emotional self-assessment is devastatingly wrong.
